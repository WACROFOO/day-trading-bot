## 1. Large-sample evidence: how many retail day traders and retail derivative traders make money

### Takeaway
Every large-sample study found says the same thing. Less than 1% of Taiwanese day traders are predictably profitable net of fees. 97% of Brazilians who day traded futures for 300+ days lost money. 74–89% of EU retail CFD accounts lose money (ESMA, 2018). Over 4 years, more than 89% of French retail forex/CFD clients lost money (AMF, 2014). US retail options traders lost money in aggregate, mostly through transaction costs. The base rate for a retail day-trading strategy that is profitable after costs is close to zero, and costs are the main driver.

### Inferences
- Different methods give the same order of magnitude: <1% predictably profitable (Taiwan, all day traders); 97% of persistent traders losing (Brazil futures; Taiwan future-loss probability); 84% losing in a single year and >89% over 4 years (AMF CFD/forex). A candidate retail bot should start from a prior near the 1–3% tail, not 50/50.
- In every study, costs decide the outcome. US retail options were positive before costs ($10.4–12.9M/day) and negative after ($−5.0 to −1.6M/day). Indirect costs (spread to mid, $6.4B) were about 7× commissions (about $900M). Any candidate must be judged net of spread, not only commission.
- Activity raises losses. In the AMF data, 1,000+ orders went with larger average losses (€31,349) than the overall average (about €10,900). This bears directly on high-turnover intraday bots.
- "Persistence/experience" does not rescue results. Taiwan shows no rational learning, Brazil shows no learning, and AMF's 4-year persistent cohort was 88% losers.

### Gaps
- Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda (2023): primary abstract and figures not read (SSRN 403). The $358,000/day, >$70M and >$50M figures are secondary, and outlets disagree on the totals.
- No AMF loss-rate study more recent than October 2014 was found for CFDs, turbos, options or crypto derivatives. If a newer one exists, it was not found.
- I found no large-sample study of *retail algorithmic* traders' live profitability outside the Quantopian cohort (section 2).
- The earlier Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean working paper "Do Individual Day Traders Make Money? Evidence from Taiwan" (2004) and its reported "82% of all traders lost money" figure appear only in secondary snippets and were not verified.

## 2. Evidence that backtests overstate live (out-of-sample) results

### Takeaway
On 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe ratio barely predicted out-of-sample Sharpe (R² < 0.025), and more backtesting meant a larger in-sample/out-of-sample gap. The theory papers explain why. Selecting the best of many configurations guarantees a false positive eventually (Bailey & López de Prado). In worked examples, 100% of in-sample Sharpe ratios were positive while 53–78% of out-of-sample ones were negative. Harvey & Liu show the multiple-testing "haircut" exceeds 50% for Sharpe ratios below 0.4.

### Cited Findings
**Wiecki, Campbell, Lent & Stauth (Quantopian), "All that glitters is not gold: Comparing backtest and out-of-sample performance on a large cohort of trading algorithms", March 9, 2016, SSRN 2745220.** The abstract was read from the Quantopian forum archive; SSRN itself returned 403.
- "When automated trading strategies are developed and evaluated using backtests on historical pricing data, there exists a tendency to overfit to the past. Using a unique dataset of 888 algorithmic trading strategies developed and backtested on the Quantopian platform with at least 6 months of out-of-sample performance, we study the prevalence and impact of backtest overfitting." — [Quantopian archive thread](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/q-paper-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-comparing-backtest-and-out-of-sample-performance-on-a-large-cohort-of-trading-algorithms)
- "commonly reported backtest evaluation metrics like the Sharpe ratio offer little value in predicting out of sample performance (R² < 0.025). In contrast, higher order moments, like volatility and maximum drawdown, as well as portfolio construction features, like hedging, show significant predictive value of relevance to quantitative finance practitioners." — [same](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/q-paper-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-comparing-backtest-and-out-of-sample-performance-on-a-large-cohort-of-trading-algorithms)
- "we find empirical evidence of overfitting … the more backtesting a quant has done for a strategy, the larger the discrepancy between backtest and out-of-sample performance." — [same](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/q-paper-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-comparing-backtest-and-out-of-sample-performance-on-a-large-cohort-of-trading-algorithms)
- "by training non-linear machine learning classifiers on a variety of features that describe backtest behavior, out-of-sample performance can be predicted at a much higher accuracy (R² = 0.17) on hold-out data compared to using linear, univariate features. A portfolio constructed on predictions on hold-out data performed significantly better out-of-sample than one constructed from algorithms with the highest backtest Sharpe ratios." — [same](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/q-paper-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-comparing-backtest-and-out-of-sample-performance-on-a-large-cohort-of-trading-algorithms)
- Sample construction (search extract): the study began with 7,152 algorithms; preprocessing reduced them to 888; backtests covered 2010–2015. — [Quantopian archive thread](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/q-paper-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-comparing-backtest-and-out-of-sample-performance-on-a-large-cohort-of-trading-algorithms)

**Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (revised version February 27, 2015; Journal of Computational Finance; fetched).**
- Abstract: "Standard statistical techniques designed to prevent regression overfitting, such as hold-out, tend to be unreliable and inaccurate in the context of investment backtests. We propose a general framework to assess the probability of backtest overfitting (PBO)." — [Bailey et al. PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)
- Example: "Whereas 100% of the SR IS are positive, about 78% of the SR OOS are negative. Also, Sharpe ratios IS range between 1 and 3, indicating that backtests with high Sharpe ratios tell us nothing regarding the representativeness of that result. … it appears that the higher the SR IS, the lower the SR OOS." The same strategy is shown "with a PBO of 74%". — [Bailey et al. PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)
- Random-walk example: "a time series of 1,000 daily prices (about 4 years) was generated by drawing from a random walk. Parameters were optimized … resulting in an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.27." Then: "approx. 53% of the SR OOS are negative, despite all SR IS being positive and ranging between 1 and 2.2. … despite the elevated SR IS, the PBO is as high as 55%." — [Bailey et al. PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)

**Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality" (version July 31, 2014; Journal of Portfolio Management; fetched).**
- "After a sufficient number of trials, it is guaranteed that a researcher will always find a misleadingly profitable strategy, a false positive." … "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) corrects for two leading sources of performance inflation: Selection bias under multiple testing and non-Normally distributed returns." — [DSR PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)
- Numerical example (annualized SR of 2.5 on a daily 5-year sample, many configurations tried): "The investor has recognized that there is only a 90% chance that the true SR associated with this strategy is greater than zero. Should the strategist have made his discovery after running only N=46 independent trials, the investor may have allocated some funds, as [DSR] would have been 0.9505, above the 95% confidence level." — [DSR PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)

**Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting", Journal of Portfolio Management, Fall 2015 (fetched).**
- "A common practice in evaluating backtests of trading strategies is to discount the reported Sharpe ratios by 50%. … The 50% haircut is only a rule of thumb." — [Harvey & Liu PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)
- "We argue that it is a serious mistake to use the usual 50% haircut. Our results show that the multiple testing haircut is nonlinear. The highest Sharpe ratios are only moderately penalized, while the marginal Sharpe ratios are heavily penalized." — [Harvey & Liu PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)
- "the haircut is almost always more than and sometimes much larger than 50% when the annualized Sharpe ratio is less than 0.4. On the other hand, when the Sharpe ratio is greater than 1.0, the haircut is at most 25%. This shows the 50% rule of thumb discount for the Sharpe ratio is inappropriate: 50% is too lenient for relatively small Sharpe ratios (< 0.4) and too harsh for large ones (> 1.0)." — [Harvey & Liu PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)
- Multiple-testing context: "Harvey, Liu, and Zhu [2015] (HLZ) document that at least 316 factors have been" tested (the quoted text is truncated at the page break). — [Harvey & Liu PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)

### Inferences
- R² < 0.025 means in-sample Sharpe explained under 2.5% of the variation in out-of-sample Sharpe across 888 real retail-built algorithms. A good backtest should be treated as close to uninformative unless the number of trials is disclosed and corrected for (DSR/PBO).
- Count every parameter sweep, filter variant and re-run as a trial. Wiecki et al. found the backtest/live gap grows with the amount of backtesting, consistent with the DSR's rising threshold as N grows.
- For a typical retail intraday strategy whose honest Sharpe is below 0.4, Harvey & Liu imply a haircut above 50%. Most of the backtested edge should be assumed gone before costs are even modelled.

### Gaps
- "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism" (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, Notices of the AMS, May 2014) was not read (ams.org returned 403). Its minimum-backtest-length figures (how few trials give an in-sample Sharpe of about 1 on noise) are not verified here.
- The Wiecki et al. full-text figures (the IS-to-OOS Sharpe decline magnitude, the counts behind "more backtests → larger gap") were not read. Only the abstract was verified.

## 3. The US Pattern Day Trader (PDT) rule in 2026: FINRA's replacement, status as of October 2026, and how IBKR applies it

### Takeaway
FINRA's Rule 4210 amendments (SR-FINRA-2025-017) removed the pattern-day-trader definition and the $25,000 minimum. They replaced them with an intraday-margin-deficit standard. The SEC approved them on April 14, 2026 (Release No. 105226). They took effect June 4, 2026, and firms may phase in until October 20, 2027. As of the latest IBKR page found (updated June 17, 2026), an IBKR account "may still be subject to existing PDT rules" during that transition. The $2,000 Reg T minimum for margin trading remains. For French residents carried by IB Ireland, the only text saying they are exempt from PDT is a forum quote, not an official IBKR page.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA (fetched, weekly update of April 15, 2026): "On April 14, the SEC approved SR-FINRA-2025-017", which "establishes new intraday margin standards to replace the longstanding day trading margin requirements under FINRA Rule 4210, including the day trade count requirements for designating a customer as a 'pattern day trader' … and the $25,000 pattern day trader minimum equity requirement". The effective date would be "45 days from publication of the regulatory notice". Members needing more time may "phase in their implementation over a period of 18 months from publication of the regulatory notice". — [FINRA weekly archive 04/15/2026](https://www.finra.org/compliance-tools/weekly-archive/04152026); [rule filing page SR-FINRA-2025-017](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rule-filings/sr-finra-2025-017)
- FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10 (fetched): "The effective date of the amendments is June 4, 2026, 45 days from publication of this Notice." Firms may "phase in their implementation over a period of 18 months, until October 20, 2027." It cites the SEC approval as "Securities Exchange Act Release No. 105226 (April 14, 2026)", 91 FR 20731 (April 17, 2026). — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10); [SEC approval order 34-105226 (PDF)](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-105226.pdf)
- Mechanics, a summary of the Notice text (fetched, paraphrased, not verbatim). Each member must determine an "intraday margin deficit" for each applicable margin account on any day with a transaction that reduces the intraday margin level. Good-faith and portfolio-margin accounts are excluded. Deficits must be satisfied "as promptly as possible". A deficit stays outstanding until satisfied or until the close of business on the 15th business day. Habitual failure triggers a 90-day restriction on new short positions or debit balances. Deficits up to the lesser of 5% of account equity or $1,000 are excepted. Real-time monitoring is not required. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)
- FINRA investor page (fetched): "These changes, which will become effective June 4, 2026 (with a permitted transition period through October 20, 2027, for brokerage firms that need more time), are designed to provide more trading flexibility while maintaining investor protections." … "$2,000 is the minimum equity required to engage in leveraged trading (trading on margin). ### Inferences
- Status as of 2026-10-31: the new intraday margin rule is **in force** (since June 4, 2026), not a proposal. Whether a given broker still applies the old PDT test depends on its phase-in, which can run until October 20, 2027. IBKR's June 2026 text leaves open that it still applies PDT to accounts it carries under FINRA rules.
- With a ~$2,000 account at a FINRA-member carrier under the new regime, the binding constraints are no longer the $25,000/4-trades rule. They are (a) the $2,000 Reg T minimum for leverage or shorting and (b) intraday margin deficits (IBKR: cure "ideally within 3 business days"; 4+ failures in 12 months → 90-day restriction). Under $2,000, a margin account trades only with available cash.
- A French resident would normally be carried by an EU IBKR entity (IB Ireland per the forum/IBKR-entity logic). If the forum-quoted IBKR text is accurate, PDT never applied to such an account. Either way the $25,000 constraint is gone or not applicable; only EU leverage, product rules and IBKR house margin remain. This must be confirmed with IBKR before the report relies on it.
- Cash accounts: Rule 4210 day-trading requirements attach to margin accounts. A cash account avoids PDT/IMD but is limited to settled cash. This is an inference; no source retrieved states it for 2026 (see Gaps).

## 4. EU/France constraints: PRIIPs/KID and US ETFs, ESMA leverage limits, and French tax on trading gains in 2026

### Takeaway
A French retail client at IBKR cannot buy US-domiciled ETFs such as SPY or QQQ, because US issuers do not publish PRIIPs KIDs. IBKR's stated alternatives are European-domiciled (UCITS) equivalents, CFDs on large US ETFs issued by IBKR's EU entities, and reclassification as a professional client. Retail CFDs are capped at 30:1 to 2:1 leverage (5:1 for single equities, 2:1 for crypto), with a 50% margin close-out and negative balance protection. The French flat tax (PFU) on securities gains is **31.4%** (12.8% income tax + 18.6% social levies), not 30%. The same 31.4% applies to private crypto gains "quel que soit le nombre de transactions". Professional-like crypto trading falls under BNC instead.

### Inferences (arithmetic from the cited rates; not published figures)
- Commission drag on a $2,000 account. 100 shares of a $5 stock ($500 notional) at Fixed pricing: 100 × 0.005 = $0.50, so the $1.00 minimum applies. That is $2.00 per round trip, or 0.40% of notional and 0.10% of equity per round trip. At Tiered: 100 × 0.0035 = $0.35 (minimum), plus clearing 100 × 0.0002 = $0.02 per side, plus exchange/ECN fees.
- Spread drag dominates. A round trip crossing the full quoted spread on a sub-$100M-cap $2–5.99 stock costs about the median 1.749% (mean 2.593%), on 2013 data. Against the 0.40% Fixed commission in the example above, that is about 4.4× (median) to 6.5× (mean), and comparable to a typical intraday stop distance. The same logic matches the options evidence: indirect costs were about 7× commissions (section 1).
- Market-data hurdle. USD 10.00/month for the waiver bundle is 0.5% of a $2,000 account per month (6%/year) unless the account generates USD 30.00/month in commissions. The Add-On streaming bundle needed for NBBO streaming adds more (price not captured). Free Cboe One + IEX data has no NBBO, which matters for a bot that models fills against the inside quote.

## 6. Automation access: IBKR APIs for EU clients, Alpaca for French residents, other broker APIs

### Takeaway
IBKR's API page lists a Web API and FIX (the TWS API is not named in the text captured from that page).
