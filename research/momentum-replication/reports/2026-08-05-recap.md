## What the day actually says

**1. The exit was worth more than the entry.** Every winner round-tripped 30–49%.
Holding for the high was punished on all twelve names without exception.

**2. Timing of the high moved later as the day got stranger.** The names that
topped inside the prime window (XGN 09:33, GTE 09:41, APPS 09:47, JDZG 09:47)
faded from there. The biggest moves topped at 11:11, 13:42 and **15:16** —
all outside the documented 11:30 hard stop. The stop cost the upside on YXT
and ASTC and saved the −49% on both. Not resolvable from one day.

**3. Capital structure was the highest-yield check of the day, and it is free.**
Four of the six traps were visible in filings before the open: INLF's dilution
vote, ZJYL's 1:20 split, YXT's 1:10 split, ASTC's ATM. None require price data.

**4. The pre-market fade veto was wrong and the morning report already
established why** — faded names run more, but only 7% take out the pre-market
high, so it is a setup classifier, not a filter. Today: JLHL faded −32.6% into
the bell and ran +126%; XOS held −3.2% and ran +3.1%.

## Added to the pre-trade checklist

Before any nano-cap gapper, in this order — all free, all pre-open:

1. **Float**, from a source that reconciles with the day's volume
2. **Reverse split history** — anything inside 12 months is a flag
3. **Effective shelf or ATM** — compare capacity to market cap
4. **Short % of float** — no shorts means no squeeze mechanism
5. Only then, the tape

## Caveat on the halt counts

The gap-based halt detector in `scripts/premarket_dd.py` over-counts on
illiquid names — it read 55 "halts" on SGHT, which traded 422k shares all day.
On thin names a gap in the print stream is absence of trading, not a halt. The
counts are reliable on the high-volume names (YXT 30, ASTC 17, INLF 7, JLHL 5)
and should be ignored below roughly 1M shares.
