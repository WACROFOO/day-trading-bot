## The hypothesis this produced, tested on July

If fading into the bell is not bearish, that contradicts pillar 5 as we apply
it ("up 10% **and still rising**"). Tested over the cached July data —
**722 symbol-days** with at least 30 pre-market bars and a full session:

| bucket at the bell | n | median run | ≥10% run |
|---|---|---|---|
| HELD (off peak > −10%) | 477 | 5.67% | 27% |
| MID (−25% to −10%) | 184 | 7.25% | 42% |
| FADED (off peak < −25%) | 61 | **10.81%** | **54%** |

Monotonic, and today was not a fluke. Linear correlation is ~0 (−0.017), so the
effect lives in the tail, not in a straight line.

### But the naive reading is wrong

The decisive control — does the faded group make *genuine new highs*?

| bucket | HOD > pre-market high | median gap from open to pm-high |
|---|---|---|
| HELD | **53%** | 4.5% |
| MID | 26% | 17.7% |
| FADED | **7%** | **48.2%** |

**Only 7% of faded names take out their pre-market high.** They open a median
48% below it, so a partial bounce prints a large percentage off a small base.
The "run" is *retracement*, not breakout — a base effect, largely.

Excluding days whose high printed in the first five minutes, the bounce is
still real and still tradeable:

| bucket | n | median run | ≥10% run |
|---|---|---|---|
| HELD | 363 | 7.40% | 35% |
| MID | 135 | 12.96% | 57% |
| FADED | 43 | **16.45%** | **72%** |

## What this changes

1. **"Faded in pre-market" is not a rejection reason.** It was used as one all
   morning and it cost the day's biggest winner. It is a *setup classifier*.
2. **The two groups need different plans.** A HELD name (XOS) makes new highs
   53% of the time — break of the pre-market high is the correct trigger, and
   that plan was right in kind even though it never fired. A FADED name (JLHL)
   takes out the pre-market high only 7% of the time — the pre-market high is
   its **target**, not its trigger. That is the dip-and-rip / break-of-VWAP
   family, not gap-and-go.
3. The 09:50 JLHL re-read used the pre-market high as a target and was correct.
   The 08:45 call used the fade as a veto and was not.

## Caveats

n=61 in the FADED tail, one month, one market regime. `run%` is open → HOD and
says nothing about whether an entry rule would have captured it. Nothing here
has been put through the engine; it is a hypothesis with a measurement
attached, not a result.
