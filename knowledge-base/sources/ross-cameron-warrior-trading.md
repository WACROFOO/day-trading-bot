# Ross Cameron / Warrior Trading

**Role:** Day trader and founder of Warrior Trading, a paid trading-education company.
**Known for:** The "Small Account Challenge" — starting with a few hundred dollars and publicly trading it up, repeated as a new challenge most years.
**Relevance to this project:** He is the most prominent example of the format the project is modeled on, and his method is documented in enough mechanical detail to be codified and tested.

## The challenges

| Year | Starting capital | Claimed outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 | $1,000 | ~$8,653 in one month |
| 2017 | $583.15 | >$100,000 in ~44 trading days; ~$335,000 by year end |
| 2018 | — | ~$800,000+ cumulative by year end |
| 2019 | $500 | ~$20,000 in 14 trading days; crossed $1M cumulative in May 2019 (~553 trading days) |
| 2022 | — | >$12.5M cumulative claimed |
| 2024 | $600 | ~$16,000 in 20 trading days |
| 2025 | — | $1,000,000 reached in a claimed 51 trading days |

The famous $583.15 figure was not a deliberate choice. He intended to start with $700, but a $125 platform fee on account opening left him with $583.15 against a $500 account minimum.

## Verification status

**Partially audited, heavily disputed.**

- Warrior Trading has published a track record audited by Citrin Cooperman, a third-party CPA firm. This is more verification than most trading educators offer.
- An audit confirms that the trades happened and the P&L is arithmetically correct. It does **not** establish that the strategy is repeatable, that the results are attributable to skill rather than a favorable market regime, or that anyone else can reproduce them.
- Warrior Trading's own disclaimers state that most day traders lose money and that Cameron's results are not typical.

## Regulatory record — read this before weighting anything above

In April 2022 the **FTC sued Warrior Trading and Ross Cameron personally** (D. Mass., No. 3:22-cv-30048) over deceptive earnings claims, alleging violations of the FTC Act and the Telemarketing Sales Rule.

- The complaint alleged that **the vast majority of customer accounts lost money**, and that many customers lost thousands trading on top of thousands paid for the courses.
- Warrior Trading settled for **$3 million**, neither admitting nor denying the allegations, and was barred from making unsubstantiated earnings claims.
- In January 2023 the FTC distributed **$2.9M to 20,402 harmed consumers**.
- A nuance worth recording: reporting indicates the FTC did not allege Cameron's own numbers were factually false. The allegation was that presenting accurate personal results *implied* customers would achieve similar outcomes — and that implication was deceptive.

**What this means for us:** The strategy may well have an edge in his hands. The evidence strongly suggests that edge does not transfer to the average person applying the same rules. Since a bot is closer to "average mechanical application" than to "Cameron's discretionary execution," we should expect the codified version to underperform his published numbers substantially — possibly to have no edge at all.

## Why the results may not transfer

- **Execution speed and fills.** Average winning trade is held ~3 minutes on low-float, fast-moving small caps. Slippage and partial fills dominate outcomes at this timescale.
- **Discretion.** Catalyst quality, tape reading, and knowing when *not* to trade are judgment calls that the written rules do not capture.
- **Capacity.** Sub-10M float stocks cannot absorb size. The strategy that works on $500 may not work on $500,000, and the reverse is also a concern — costs and minimums bite hardest at tiny account sizes.
- **Regime dependence.** The largest gains coincide with the 2017 and 2020–2021 small-cap speculative booms.
- **PDT rule.** Accounts under $25,000 in the US are limited to 3 day trades per 5 business days. The original challenge used an offshore broker (SureTrader) to sidestep this; that broker has since shut down.
