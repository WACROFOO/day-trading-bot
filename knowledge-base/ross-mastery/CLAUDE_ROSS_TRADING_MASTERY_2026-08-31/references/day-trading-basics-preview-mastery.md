## Core operating doctrine

1. Treat profits as a byproduct of a repeatable process. Process discipline matters more than a streak of green days.
2. Prove a strategy in a simulator before risking real money. Start live with very small size only after metrics show consistency.
3. Be a hunter of volatility and a manager of risk. A scanner candidate is not automatically a trade.
4. Focus on the obvious leading movers rather than forcing obscure ideas. Ross generally does best on the top few percentage gainers.
5. Require at least four of the Five Pillars. Five out of five is strongest; three out of five should normally be rejected.
6. Trade the first clean part of a move. In a cool market, take the first leg and avoid overstaying; in a hot market, later legs can work but need fresh confirmation.
7. Use the fastest chart that can define a tight, logical stop. For this approach, the 1-minute chart is primary, the 10-second chart can expose micro pullbacks, and the 5-minute chart supplies context.
8. Favor breakout-or-bailout behavior: if expected resolution does not appear quickly, reduce or exit rather than widen the stop.
9. Keep tools simple and widely observed. Indicators confirm or reject a chart hypothesis; they do not create certainty.
10. Build redundancy for broker, data, internet, power and devices before trading live.

## Chapter 1 - Becoming a Day Trader

### Purpose and learning architecture

- Day Trading: The Basics establishes a foundation and aims to leave the student with one executable setup.
- Strategies & Scaling revisits the same pillars with more detail, more setups and live archives. The Preview is not the full advanced strategy course.
- Ross describes his rule set as derived from analysis of more than 25,000 of his own trades. That evidence supports his own process but does not guarantee another trader's results.

### Process and risk

- Do not impose a daily or monthly income demand on the market. Scarcity and desperation increase impulsivity, overtrading and rule breaking.
- Use the simulator, scanners and charts as a safe practice environment. Transition to live trading only after profitable simulator metrics.
- The survival principle is `survive till you thrive`: avoid blowups, control large losses, stay in the learning environment and accumulate disciplined days.
- Mirroring another trader is not a substitute for understanding the setup, managing personal risk and making an independent decision.
- Day trading is presented as the highest-risk/highest-return category compared with swing trading and passive long-term investing. Published success rates and Ross's own results are not typical.

### Equipment and business setup

- Start with a minimum viable setup rather than spending heavily before proof of concept.
- A capable computer, stable internet, suitable chart/scanner access, a broker platform and at least one useful display can be enough to learn.
- Multiple displays are convenient, not a prerequisite for profitability. Portable monitors and USB display adapters can expand a laptop setup.
- Treat the trading operation as a business only after consulting appropriate tax/accounting professionals; course examples are education, not tax advice.

### Long-term financial framing

- The course distinguishes regular taxable trading income from retirement-account compounding.
- The 4% heuristic says roughly 4% of a diversified retirement portfolio may be withdrawn annually; the Rule of 300 translates this into `desired monthly spending x 300` as a rough target.
- These are broad planning heuristics, not guarantees. Sequence-of-returns risk, taxes, fees, allocation and personal circumstances matter.
- Roth IRA examples emphasize post-tax contributions and potentially tax-free qualified withdrawals. Verify current jurisdiction-specific rules with a professional.

## Chapter 2 - Accounts, brokers and emergency planning

### Account types

- `Professional` market-data status refers primarily to licensing/industry status, not whether trading is one's main occupation.
- A cash account uses settled cash. The course's current quiz states settlement is the next business day. Reusing unsettled proceeds can violate account rules.
- A margin account makes buying power available without waiting for each trade to settle and may add leverage. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses and can trigger a margin call.
- A retirement account may offer tax advantages but typically restricts borrowing, leverage and short selling.
- The course contains a June 4, 2026 statement about changes to FINRA's Pattern Day Trader treatment. Treat this as **course-specific regulatory commentary** and verify the live rule with FINRA and the broker before relying on it.
- A round trip is a completed entry and exit in the same symbol; scaling in or out can create multiple executions without necessarily representing multiple independent ideas.

### Broker scorecard

Prioritize these features for fast momentum trading:

1. near-instant execution;
2. reliable, stable software;
3. direct-access routing when size makes routing material;
4. programmable hotkeys or hot buttons;
5. transparent and competitive total execution cost;
6. adequate Level 2, Time & Sales and chart data;
7. suitable short inventory only if the strategy genuinely uses short selling;
8. responsive support and documented outage procedures.

Commission-free does not mean cost-free. Payment for order flow, weaker price improvement, slower execution or poorer data can create an indirect cost. Direct access is more consequential at large share size than for a beginner trading very small size.

### Emergency plan

- Broker/platform outage: try desktop, mobile app, web portal, then call the broker. Keep phone numbers and support contacts immediately available.
- Frozen charts/data: use an independent chart or data source, broker Level 2 and Time & Sales to monitor risk.
- Stuck order: attempt cancel, document the screen, reconnect, and contact the broker. Never assume an unconfirmed order is gone.
- Local power loss: use an uninterrupted power supply for modem/router and critical equipment. A generator alone leaves a restart gap.
- Internet loss: keep a mobile hotspot or secondary connection ready.
- Device loss: keep a second computer, phone or tablet capable of reaching the account.
- Broker terms usually disclaim losses caused by outages; redundancy is part of risk management.

## Chapter 3 - Stock selection

### Market segment

- The strategy specializes in US-listed small-cap momentum stocks, generally long-biased and usually priced below $20.
- Course quiz bands: micro-cap below $100M, small-cap about $100M-$2B, mid-cap $2B-$10B, large-cap $10B+.
- Large caps attract institutional and algorithmic activity and usually have much larger supply. Small caps can create sharper supply-demand imbalances accessible to retail traders.
- Penny stocks and very illiquid names can show huge percentage moves but may have unsafe spreads, poor liquidity and misleading apparent opportunity.
- The preferred market niche is not proof that other markets or styles cannot work; it is specialization.

### Long versus short

- Long: buy shares, profit from a rise, and the theoretical maximum loss is the capital placed in the position if the stock goes to zero.
- Short: borrow and sell shares, then buy to cover. The theoretical loss is unlimited because price has no upper bound.
- Shorting requires an account and broker that permit it and available borrow; hard-to-borrow shares may have fees or be unavailable.
- A short squeeze is a rapid rise amplified by short sellers buying to cover.
- Short Sale Restriction is triggered after a 10% decline from the prior close and generally requires short orders to execute on an uptick/at the ask rather than hitting the bid. Verify live broker/exchange implementation.

### Five Pillars - confirmed Preview thresholds

| Pillar | Preview rule | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility | Usually already up at least 10%; leading gainers preferred | Demand is already visible |
| Price | Working range $2-$20; strongest analytics around $5-$10 | Affordable enough for retail demand, with useful dollar range |
| Relative volume | About 5x or higher | Today's activity is exceptional for that stock |
| Catalyst | Breaking news preferred but not mandatory | A technical breakout can supply the fourth qualifying pillar |
| Supply/demand | Float generally below 20M shares; lower is better | Small supply can magnify demand |

Decision rule: 5/5 is excellent, 4/5 is acceptable, 3/5 should usually be passed. Also require usable liquidity, tolerable spread, a valid chart and clear room to the next resistance.

### Scanner and flame interpretation

- The flame marks the presence/recency of a news item. The lesson explicitly describes a stock with a news catalyst as having a flame.
- A flame does **not** mean all Five Pillars are satisfied and does not grade news quality.
- A stock may meet four pillars without news and still be tradable as a technical breakout.
- Conversely, a flame on an illiquid, wide-spread or badly positioned chart does not make the stock safe.
- `Obvious` matters: leading gainers, HOD breaks and high relative volume attract more eyes and can become self-reinforcing.

## Chapter 4 - Fundamentals, filings and catalysts

### Role of fundamentals

- Fundamental analysis examines assets, liabilities, cash flow, revenue, earnings, competitive advantage and fair value.
- Long-term investors care most; swing traders may use it substantially; fast day traders use a lighter version.
- For this momentum process, the main fundamental question is usually: `Why is the stock moving today, and is dilution/financing risk unusually high?`
- Technical price action can become disconnected from book value and earnings. Do not reject every loss-making company if the strategy is specifically trading short-term momentum.

### SEC filing map

| Filing | Practical meaning |
|---|---|
| 10-Q | Quarterly US-company financial report |
| 10-K | Annual US-company report |
| 20-F | Annual report for many foreign issuers |
| S-3 | Shelf registration that can permit future share sales |
| 13-D | Beneficial owner with a significant/activist stake |
| 8-K | Material current event, including some management changes |
| Form 4 | Insider purchase or sale |

Look for cash runway, sustained losses, debt, shares outstanding, warrants, active shelf registrations and insider behavior. A company with a short cash runway and active shelf may use a price spike to raise capital. A shelf is a risk flag, not proof that an offering will occur immediately.

### Catalyst taxonomy

The Preview introduces these common catalyst families:

1. technical momentum without company news;
2. earnings;
3. FDA approval or clinical-trial results;
4. buyout;
5. split or reverse split;
6. analyst target or rating;
7. activist investor stake;
8. order, contract, deal or partnership;
9. secondary offering;
10. private placement;
11. IPO;
12. high short interest;
13. patent or trademark;
14. uplist or delist;
15. SPAC, merger or acquisition;
16. lawsuit/regulatory event;
17. broad market sentiment;
18. current sympathy/theme.

Nuance:

- FDA/late-stage clinical news can be powerful but requires interpretation.
- Partnerships must be economically meaningful; promotional wording can exaggerate ordinary vendor relationships.
- Buyouts often pin price near the announced consideration and leave poor reward/risk.
- A secondary offering is usually bearish, but pricing near market can remove uncertainty and support a squeeze; price, size and lockups matter.
- A private placement is stronger when the investor pays near market, performs due diligence and faces resale restrictions.
- Short interest, current themes and sympathy moves can act as technical catalysts.
- Scheduled releases often cluster at the top or bottom of the hour. Volume typically grows from 7:00 ET and again at 8:00 and 9:30.

## Chapter 5 - Technical analysis

### Charts and time frames

- Candlesticks encode open, high, low and close. For a green candle, the body runs from open at the bottom to close at the top; for a red candle, open is at the top and close at the bottom.
- Chronological formation is left-to-right, while Ross often begins analysis at the newest right edge and scans left for historical support/resistance.
- A 1-minute candle closes one minute after its opening timestamp; a 5-minute candle starting at 9:30 closes at 9:35.
- Standard daily candles usually represent 9:30-16:00 ET and may omit extended-hours price action even when the volume bar includes it.

### Single-candle language

- Long body: conviction/momentum.
- Short body: reduced conviction.
- Doji: open and close near the same price; indecision.
- Spinning top: small body with wicks; indecision.
- Hammer after a selloff/pullback: sellers pushed down, buyers recovered, possible base.
- Inverted hammer at the bottom is not automatically a reversal; sellers still rejected the high.
- Shooting star/topping tail after extension: rejection and possible reversal warning.
- Candle names have little value during low-volume sideways `barcode` action. Context, trend, volume, float and catalyst determine significance.

### First pullback and micro pullback

Confirmed basic pattern model:

1. Stock meets at least four Five Pillars, is obvious and near HOD.
2. Initial catalyst/volume impulse creates one or more strong green candles.
3. Normal first pullback has roughly 2-4 candles. Five or six pullback candles suggest the move is losing interest.
4. Micro pullback has one red candle; on a 10-second chart it may occur inside a fully green 1-minute candle.
5. Desired volume profile: high volume on the impulse, lighter volume on pullback, renewed volume on breakout.
6. **Entry:** first candle to make a new high after the pullback. Anticipating just below the trigger using tape/Level 2 is an advanced technique, not the beginner default.
7. **Maximum loss / stop reference:** low of the pullback or signal structure.
8. **First target:** retest of HOD or the nearest obvious resistance.
9. Require at least about 2:1 reward/risk before entry. After HOD, watch half-dollar and whole-dollar levels.
10. If a new large seller appears below the original target or the volume profile fails, the target must adapt; do not hold blindly.

Example from the lesson: entry around 6.75, structural stop around 6.65, first target near 7.00. That is $0.10 risk for $0.25 potential. These values illustrate the method; they are not universal prices.

### Other patterns

- ABCD: impulse, 2+ candle pullback, first new-high attempt that may not clear HOD, consolidation, then renewed break of the later apex/HOD.
- Flat top breakout: repeated resistance at one price, then breakout; often clusters at half/whole dollars.
- Cup and handle: deeper U-shaped recovery back to resistance, followed by a smaller pullback/handle. It is not the same as a shallow first pullback.
- Double top: rejection near the prior high; confirmation comes from failure and subsequent weakness.
- Favor the first leg. A negative MACD after a mature move often separates front side from back side.

### Support and resistance

- Draw obvious horizontal levels at HOD, recent highs/lows, premarket pivots, daily pivots and gap/window edges.
- Half dollars and whole dollars are psychological levels because orders cluster there.
- Trend lines need at least two anchors; more touches make them more credible. Avoid covering the chart with speculative lines.
- Broken resistance can become support; broken support can become resistance.
- A HOD break attracts scanner attention and can force short covering, but it still needs volume and room.
- Intraday levels lose relevance over time; on a 5-minute chart Ross rarely needs more than roughly a week of history.

### Gaps and windows

- Gap: regular-session open is materially above or below the prior close, often due to extended-hours news.
- Window: a large area with little visible support/resistance, created by a true gap or a large long-body candle.
- A meaningful gap/window should be roughly 2x a typical daily candle or 2x daily ATR.
- Recent price action overrides older levels; scan from the current right edge to the left and upward for resistance.
- Gap fill is the move back through the empty zone to the boundary of the candle before the gap.
- Do not skip across a newer gap/window to use an older minor level as if the newer structure did not exist.

### Indicators

Use a small confirmation set:

- `9 EMA` and `20 EMA`: short-term trend/support on the active timeframe. EMA weights recent prices more heavily than SMA.
- `200 EMA`: major daily support/resistance. If price is below it, require substantial room; being within about $0.50-$1.00 can be problematic depending on stock price/ATR.
- `VWAP`: intraday volume-weighted equilibrium. Above VWAP is buyer-controlled/bullish; below is weaker. VWAP is the same across intraday time frames because it uses the day's cumulative price and volume.
- `MACD 12/26/9`: mainly read on the 1-minute chart. Positive favors front-side aggression; a negative crossover is a reason to stop pressing. Ten-second MACD can flip too often; 5-minute MACD can lag and conflict.
- Volume bars: validate impulse/pullback structure. Heavy red pullback volume rejects a nominally attractive candle pattern.

Indicators are lagging confirmations, not a holy grail. A valid setup can still lose.

### Strong daily chart checklist

1. Large gaps/windows create room.
2. No nearby horizontal, diagonal, gap-edge or window-edge resistance.
3. Above the daily 200 EMA, or far enough below it to have meaningful room.
4. Prefer price above the daily 9 and 20 EMA, or at least a well-based chart rather than an active selloff.
5. A good chart without an intraday catalyst is not enough for a small-cap day trade.
6. Today's relative volume should be exceptional.
7. History of major moves, a recent IPO or reverse split is a bonus; history of high-volume rejection is a warning.

A strong intraday catalyst can override a mediocre daily chart, but the trader should know exactly which resistance is being accepted.

### Multi-time-frame alignment

- Best case: 1-minute and 5-minute both show constructive structure.
- A good 5-minute with a bad 1-minute is usually rejected because precise entry/risk cannot be defined.
- A strong 1-minute can still be traded before the 5-minute has formed a pattern, especially on an exceptional fast mover.
- The 1-minute chart is Ross's primary execution chart; 10-second helps with micro pullbacks; 5-minute and daily provide context and room.
- Do not base a large volatile-stock stop solely on the low of a wide 5-minute candle unless position size is reduced accordingly.

## Chapter 6 - Platform walkthrough

### Minimum layout

1. linked 1-minute, 5-minute and daily charts;
2. Level 2/order book;
3. Time & Sales/tape;
4. order-entry controls or tested hotkeys;
5. positions window with symbol, shares, average cost and realized/unrealized P&L;
6. working/open orders separated from completed orders;
7. executions/trade log;
8. scanners and news/catalyst panel.

Level 2 shows resting buyers and sellers; Time & Sales shows executed trades. Neither should be interpreted alone.

### Order and P&L mechanics

- Realized P&L comes from shares already closed; unrealized P&L comes from shares still held.
- Average cost is share-weighted. Buying 1,000 at $5 and 1,000 at $6 gives a $5.50 average.
- Under FIFO, the first lot is treated as sold first for realized P&L. The remaining lot determines current unrealized P&L.
- Execution count includes each buy or sell fill. Total commissions on a per-execution schedule are `executions x rate`.
- A partial fill leaves the remainder live until filled or cancelled. Display ordered quantity versus executed quantity.
- A forgotten open limit order can fill later if price reaches it. Keep working orders visible and cancel intentionally before leaving.
- Hiding the dollar P&L temporarily can reduce FOMO, revenge trading and fear; still monitor shares, risk and active orders.

### Platform evaluation

- The lesson evaluates platforms by speed, stability, hotkey support, order-book/tape presentation, routing and integration.
- Ross criticizes beginner-oriented platforms that lack fast execution, true hotkeys or conventional Level 2.
- DAS Trader is described as broker-connectable third-party software; specialized direct-access brokers may provide better execution tools but cost more.
- Treat platform opinions as strategy-specific observations, not universal rankings. Test with simulator size and record fills/slippage.

## TradingView translation

### Candidate score

Expose independent fields rather than one opaque signal:

- price in $2-$20, with a separate ideal $5-$10 flag;
- percentage gain >= 10%;
- daily RVOL >= 5x;
- float < 20M when reliable data exists;
- catalyst/manual-news confirmation;
- usable spread/liquidity proxy;
- strong-daily-chart room score;
- HOD proximity and first-pullback state.

Do not encode the flame as the full score. If TradingView lacks live headline data, catalyst must remain manual or come from an external licensed news feed.

### Entry/stop/target bands

For the basic first-pullback model:

- entry band center: signal/pullback trigger high plus configurable buffer;
- stop band center: pullback low minus configurable buffer;
- risk per share: `entry - stop`;
- minimum target: `entry + 2 x risk`;
- optional first-target override: HOD when it is closer but still offers acceptable reward/risk;
- next targets: nearest half dollar, whole dollar or daily resistance;
- invalidate when stop is not below entry, reward/risk is inadequate, volume diverges, or price is too extended.

Bands are planning aids, not autonomous orders. Freeze them on the confirmed signal bar to avoid repainting.

### Scenarios with answers

6. `Charts freeze during an open trade.` Switch to independent data/broker Level 2 and tape, use a backup connection/device, manage risk and contact the broker if orders cannot be confirmed.
