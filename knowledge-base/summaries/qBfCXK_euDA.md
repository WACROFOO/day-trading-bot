# The Ultimate Short Selling Guide

## Summary

Complete walkthrough of short selling mechanics including borrowing shares, covering positions, fees, easy-to-borrow vs hard-to-borrow terminology, and catastrophic risk examples. Warns against shorting low-float parabolic moves due to unlimited loss potential and short squeezes.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:01:10] To short, borrow shares from broker, sell them, then buy back to return shares (create negative position, cover to close).
- [00:03:01] Borrowing fee charged daily by broker; fee varies by stock availability.
- [00:03:08] Must cover position within days-to-cover limit (7-30 days) or broker will auto-liquidate.
- [00:05:27] Shorts into strength (as stock rises) when extended, anticipating eventual reversal.
- [00:18:11] Always set hard stops on short positions; losses can exceed account balance requiring margin call.

## Setups and patterns

- **Shorting into extended move** — Short starter position as stock squeezes higher, add more on each push, anticipate top reversal. [00:14:17]
- **Short at resistance level** — Wait for stock to fade from key resistance (e.g., 200-moving average) rather than shorting early into strength. [00:16:46]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Easy to borrow (E/ETB)** — Large-cap stocks with abundant shares; no borrowing fee. [00:08:15]
- **Hard to borrow (L/locate)** — Small-cap stocks; broker must search; fees up to $1/share per day. [00:09:09]
- 200-moving average as critical resistance level. [00:13:07]

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:03:21] Short example: short 1,000 shares at $10, cover at $5 = $5,000 profit (50% return).
- [00:04:21] Short example downside: short at $10, price rises to $40, buy back = $30,000 loss (loss exceeds initial $10,000 position value).
- [00:06:25] Borrowing fee example: 10 cents per share on 1,000 shares = $100/day; $1,000-$3,000 for 10-20 day holds.
- [00:06:58] GameStop case: Melvin Capital lost $6.8 billion on short squeeze.
- [00:17:39] Hard to borrow fee example: $1/share on small cap (prohibitive).
- [00:15:54] LFIN parabolic example: moved from $5 to $26 to $100+ over multiple days; 5,000 share short = $122.5M loss; 10,000 shares = $25M loss.
- [00:17:49] Extreme example: stock moved $20 to $2,500 (25,000% move) on short squeeze.
