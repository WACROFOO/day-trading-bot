# How to Predict A False Breakout

## Summary

Panel discussion of six traders sharing techniques to identify and avoid false breakouts (flush events where stock briefly breaks resistance then reverses sharply). Focuses on daily chart history, pre-market action, level 2 depth analysis, hidden seller detection, volume profile, multi-timeframe alignment, and the psychology of stop-loss hunting by market makers and short-sellers.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:00:48] First: examine daily chart looking for wick highs and historical performance (act like detective).
- [00:01:18] Check pre-market action: if spike up followed by dump, signals big seller coming in after market open.
- [00:01:36] At market open (9:30 a.m.): if stock dumps despite pre-market strength, indicates overhead seller.
- [00:01:54] Level 2 critical level test: if stock can't break above a critical level and sellers keep reloading, exit and avoid (don't get "smoked").
- [00:02:30] Trader intuition and experience: learn stock personality/behavior; if it false-breakouts once or twice, it will likely do it again.
- [00:02:56] Short-sale restriction indicator: if seeing 50,000+ share flush on restricted short stock, that's forced unwinding of long position, not shorting.
- [00:03:31] Institutional ownership analysis: research what % of shares are held by institutions; combine with stock history for context.
- [00:04:00] Breakout strength metric: weak breakout (breaks by only 2 cents) = high probability false breakout; strong breakout (15+ cents) = more conviction.
- [00:04:33] Short-seller stop-loss theory: short sellers short off high of day with stop at high of day; weak breakout = they hold/add; strong breakout = they cover (buy).
- [00:05:34] Iceberg order detection: hidden large seller behind small visible ask (e.g., 100 shares visible but 20,000 share seller hidden); order fills don't drop bid, indicating hidden size.
- [00:06:01] Hidden seller anticipation: when you submit order with expected slippage but get none, you bought from hidden seller (iceberg filled).
- [00:07:00] Position quality threshold: if hidden seller is present, question the setup strength; if setup very strong otherwise, might overcome hidden seller.
- [00:08:04] Hidden sellers at critical levels: sellers often stack large orders at chart resistance/VWAP to prevent breakout.
- [00:09:31] Pre-market false breakout window: light volume breakout in pre-market likely fails when market opens; wait for real volume at market open.
- [00:09:50] Volume confirmation at breakout: if breakout happens on light volume, odds are high it reverses; need more volume coming in at the breakout to confirm.
- [00:11:30] First candle break volume: if breakout candle has medium/green volume bigger than prior red candles, likely resolves higher; if lighter volume, false breakout more probable.
- [00:11:43] High-volume flush candle pattern: if resolves down with high-volume red candle, pattern is broken/reversal confirmed.
- [00:12:35] Multi-timeframe alignment check: stock extended up? Consolidation on 1-minute with declining volume = higher probability pop-and-fade; consolidation with volume increasing = more likely resolves up.
- [00:13:06] Market state/theme awareness: when market is in strong theme (recent reverse splits, Chinese IPOs, etc.), false breakouts less common; when between themes or theme fading, traps increase.
- [00:15:20] Tap-tap-tap-break pattern warning: if stock taps resistance multiple times (3rd, 4th attempt) before breaking, each failed tap = more resistance; breaks on 4th tap by only 2 cents = immediate reversal likely.
- [00:15:48] Risk management after weak breakout: if weak breakout (2 cents), shorts add positions; longs bail = double-sided selling = flush.
- [00:17:45] Double-bottom vs. double-top: if dips and bounces twice at same level (double bottom), second bounce opportunity often stronger than first (shorts covering).
- [00:18:10] Emotional hedge theory: trade opposite side based on psychology; if going long dip, think "where would short sellers have stop loss?" (high of day); they cover = up.
- [00:21:00] Consolidation before breakout: tighter consolidation (support and resistance closer) = more likely false breakout; coiling pattern (support getting closer together) = more conviction before break.
- [00:22:00] Liquidity trap suspicion: some believe market makers use high-frequency algos to create traps; breakout immediately followed by dump suggests algo setting trap.
- [00:23:26] Reversal candle (high-volume red after push up) = marks high; acts as signal to avoid further long entries or flip short.
- [00:25:36] Trend line web theory: if you draw trend lines connecting multiple candlestick wicks (premarket through day), reactions occur at those levels consistently; suggests market maker orders stacked there.
- [00:27:04] Order effect knowledge: experienced traders know approximate slippage expected for their share size; if order fills with zero slippage despite size, hidden seller is likely.
- [00:28:33] Consistent indicator usage: must apply technical indicator or system consistently; using it only 10% of the time means missing its signal 90% of the time.
- [00:28:41] Volume profile as primary: prioritize high-volume candles on breakout; that is biggest sign of conviction.
- [00:31:34] Market maker role: they profit from spread (buy bid, sell ask, same shares daily); motivated to keep stock range-bound to maximize trades; incentivized to use algos to stop people out both directions.
- [00:32:23] Range-bound psychology: market makers keep things choppy to generate trade flow; when stock goes straight up sharply, they lose opportunity.
- [00:33:22] Specific stock avoidance: if stock has false-breakout personality + short-sale restriction creating 75-cent flush events, avoid entirely (risk management).
- [00:34:00] Stock selection over timing: spend more time finding clean-price-action stocks than trying to time flushes on choppy stocks.

## Setups and patterns

- **Weak breakout false reversal** — Breaks resistance by only 2 cents; immediately reverses; shorts add, longs bail; double-sided selling. [00:04:33]
- **Strong breakout continuation** — Breaks resistance by 10-15+ cents convincingly; shorts cover (buy), shorts stop out higher. [00:05:07]
- **Iceberg order trap** — 100 shares visible on ask but 20,000 shares hidden behind; order fills at better price than expected, revealing hidden seller. [00:05:34]
- **Pre-market spike-dump** — Stock spikes up pre-market then dumps; signals big seller ready at market open. [00:01:18]
- **Double-bottom** — Stock dips twice to same support level; second bounce often stronger as short sellers' stops are triggered. [00:17:45]
- **Tap-tap-tap-break** — Resistance tested multiple times (3-4 taps); each failure increases resistance credibility; break on 4th attempt by small amount = false. [00:15:20]
- **Consolidation into breakout** — Tighter consolidation before breakout = higher false-breakout risk; loose consolidation = more conviction. [00:21:00]
- **High-volume red candle reversal** — After extended move up, high-volume red candle marks the high; signals potential reversal. [00:23:26]
- **Trend line web reaction** — Multiple trend lines drawn across candle wicks show reaction points; suspect hidden orders stacked at those levels. [00:25:36]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Daily chart analysis** — Historical price action identifying prior resistance, support, gaps, patterns. [00:00:50]
- **Level 2 depth-of-market** — Shows bid/ask orders, order sizes, helps identify large sellers at specific prices. [00:01:54]
- **Time and sales tape** — Individual order flows; green trades = buying (aggressive), red trades = selling. [00:00:00]
- **Pre-market volume profile** — Volume during pre-market trading hours before 9:30 a.m. market open. [00:01:18]
- **VWAP (volume-weighted average price)** — Equilibrium level; hidden buyers/sellers often stack orders at VWAP. [00:08:31]
- **Moving averages (EMA, SMA)** — Used to identify support/resistance; 20 SMA vs. 9 EMA mentioned in context. [00:29:41]
- **Trend lines** — Manual lines drawn connecting candlestick wicks; where market reactions occur (suspected hidden order placement). [00:25:36]
- **Short-sale restriction status** — Regulatory indicator; under short-sale restriction, stock cannot be shorted on downticks. [00:02:56]
- **Institutional ownership %** — Research data showing what percentage of company held by institutional investors. [00:03:31]
