# Pattern Day Trader Rule EXPLAINED (And How to Get Around it)

## Summary

Comprehensive explanation of the Pattern Day Trader (PDT) rule enacted in 2001 that restricts margin account traders from exceeding three day trades in five business days unless they maintain $25,000 minimum. Covers rule history, margin vs. cash accounts, leverage restrictions, and practical workarounds for US and international traders.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:00:21] PDT rule: any trader with margin account executing more than three day trades within five business days must maintain minimum $25,000 account balance.
- [00:01:30] Day trade definition: one round trip (buy and sell = 1 trade); buy-sell-buy-sell = 2 trades; each change of direction counts as 1 trade.
- [00:10:32] To avoid PDT: use cash account where cash must settle before next trade (no unsettled cash trading allowed).
- [00:10:48] Cash account traders can trade unlimited times until they run out of available cash balance.
- [00:10:58] Once account grows above $25,000, convert from cash to margin account and PDT no longer restricts trading.
- [00:16:09] US brokers (Thinkorswim, Robin Hood, Lightspeed, Webull) MUST enforce PDT for US residents.
- [00:17:50] Non-US brokers (Interactive Brokers international, Webull international, TradeZero) do NOT enforce PDT on non-US residents.

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Margin accounts** — allow short selling, trading on unsettled cash, and leverage (4x typically); subject to PDT.
- **Cash accounts** — require settlement before next trade, no leverage, no shorting, but unlimited day trades with available cash.
- **TradeZero, Interactive Brokers** — international brokers accepting non-US residents without PDT restriction; may offer 6x leverage.
- **CME (offshore broker in Caribbean)** — accepts US residents without advertising to them; does not enforce PDT (gray area regulatory status).

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:00:40] Minimum account balance for PDT compliance: $25,000.
- [00:03:06] 1920s leverage example: 10% down payment on stock purchase, rest on monthly payment (like buying car on layaway).
- [00:03:58] Dot-com bubble leverage example: 4x leverage on $1,000 account = $4,000 buying power.
- [00:04:07] 4x leverage position drop: $1,000 down to $0.15 per share = $2,500 loss, margin call issued.
- [00:07:45] Nvidia margin requirement: 25% (4x leverage).
- [00:08:02] Higher-risk stock margin requirement: 60%.
- [00:08:15] Restricted stocks margin requirement: 100% (no leverage).
- [00:20:49] Goal for small account: grow 10% per day on $1,000 = $100 daily profit.
- [00:20:55] Example: $1,000 account buys ~333 shares at $3/share; 10% move to $3.30 = ~$99 profit.

## Claims needing verification

- [00:01:49] PDT rule enacted February 27, 2001, following the dot-com bubble to prevent broker losses (not retail trader losses).
- [00:05:31] PDT rule was actually designed to protect brokers from leverage-induced losses, not to protect retail traders.
- [00:22:39] Trading on leverage as beginner is more likely to result in losses than consistent profitability.
- [00:22:45] Brokers restrict leverage on stocks under $3 and high-momentum stocks (50%+ up days) to manage risk.
