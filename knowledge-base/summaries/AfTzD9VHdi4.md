# Stock Market for Beginners | How to Become a Trader

## Summary

Introductory overview of stock market structure, how companies raise capital through IPOs, the role of broker-dealers, and difference between long-term investing and active day trading. Emphasizes 7.5% historical average market returns, inflation protection, and momentum trading as strategy for volatility capture.

## Setups and patterns

- **Momentum trading** — Buy stocks moving strongly up on catalyst (news, earnings, partnership); hold minutes to hours for profit. [00:14:23]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- Stock brokers (TD Ameritrade, E-Trade, Interactive Brokers, custom platforms) as intermediary to market access. [00:15:48]
- SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K) for financial transparency on publicly traded companies. [00:06:23]

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:02:05] Historical market return: 7.5% average per year.
- [00:02:08] 20-year compounding example: $100 at 7.5% = $450 after 20 years.
- [00:02:17] Scaling example: $1,000 = $4,500; $10,000 = $45,000; $100,000 = $450,000.
- [00:04:32] IPO example: company raises $200M by issuing 10M shares at $20/share.
- [00:08:13] Inflation average: 2% per year.
- [00:10:23] S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average as indexed funds for reducing single-stock exposure.
- [00:15:11] RVSN case study: stock moved from $1.60 to $2.50 (53% gain in one day).
