# PROVEN Bear Market Trading & Investing Strategies

## Summary

Three distinct strategies for profiting during market corrections and bear markets: long-term investing using cost basis averaging, swing trading with volatility instruments, and day trading with high-volume momentum stocks. Suited for investors at all experience levels seeking to understand how to trade through downturns rather than panic-sell.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:12:02] Use cost basis averaging: add fixed dollar amounts at regular intervals rather than trying to time market bottoms.
- [00:12:04] Dollar amount must be consistent at fixed time intervals (e.g., same amount weekly or monthly).
- [00:12:35] When market has correction/bear market, same fixed amount buys more shares, bringing down average cost.
- [00:19:20] Swing trading: enter as close to support as possible to minimize max loss.
- [00:20:21] Set stop order just below support level as max loss exit.
- [00:21:01] Best trades have entries at support with stops just below support for best risk-to-reward ratio.
- [00:22:01] Day trading focus: trade instruments with high volatility in the market.
- [00:22:08] Trend trade (buy up, short down) rather than reversal trade; trend trading produces more success.
- [00:25:55] Do not invest on margin/leverage as inexperienced investor; use diversified approach instead.

## Setups and patterns

- **Cost basis averaging** — adding fixed amounts regularly to reduce average purchase price over market cycles. [00:12:02]
- **Support and resistance** — historical price levels where stock has shown clear support or resistance; use as entry and exit points. [00:19:28]
- **VIX volatility instruments** — UVIX and SVIX allow trading market volatility spikes without directly shorting overall market. [00:14:32]
- **Cup and handle** — bullish chart pattern with consolidation forming handle; breakout above resistance signals continuation. [00:18:30]
- **Dip and rip** — stock dips during halt then rips higher as panic sellers capitulate. [00:23:20]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **VIX (Volatility Index)** — measures expected volatility over next 30 days; spikes during market stress. [00:15:04]
- **UVIX** — bullish VIX instrument; goes up when VIX spikes. [00:14:32]
- **SVIX** — bearish VIX instrument; goes down when VIX spikes; useful for traders in retirement accounts. [00:17:49]
- **Moving averages** — identify trends and support levels on candlestick charts. [00:24:48]
- **MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)** — technical indicator for identifying momentum and reversals. [00:24:48]
- **Stock scanners** — find leading gainers and top movers during market sessions. [00:22:15]
