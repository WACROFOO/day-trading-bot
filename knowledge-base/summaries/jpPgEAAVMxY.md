# +$15,113.96 Day Trading a SPAC and a Recent Reverse Split Setup

## Summary

Two profitable trades: ESHA (SPAC) earning $15,000 across two trades with adding/scaling; INBS (recent reverse split) earning $442 on dip-and-rip after halt resumption. Demonstrated the power of low-float, recent reverse-split stocks after news and haltable breakouts.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:02:01] SPAC entry: break of high-of-day level when volume starts to sustain upward movement. Example: break of $21.50, added at $22.
- [00:04:00] Add to position when already profitable in driver's seat (up $1–2 per share, looking for continuation).
- [00:04:00] Exit: when big seller appears at resistance and stock stalls; take profit rather than hold through seller.
- [00:08:00] Dip-and-rip: after halt resumption, buy the dip; when price rips back up, scale out.
- [00:03:30] Check Level 2 for halt level (maximum price during halt). [00:06:48] On ESHA, halt level was 25.83.

## Setups and patterns

- **SPAC (Special Purpose Acquisition Company)** — shell company formed to acquire other businesses; can have significant moves on merger announcements. [00:00:15]
- **Recent reverse split** — stock that has been beaten down, does reverse split to maintain minimum price for exchange listing (NYSE/NASDAQ min. $1). After split, news catalyst often follows, causing squeeze. [00:00:28]
- **Dip and rip** — stock halts on extreme move, resumes, dips slightly, then rips higher. [00:07:59]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Level 2** — depth-of-market display showing bid/ask stacks and halt levels. [00:06:48]
- **MACD** — used for momentum confirmation (negative MACD = caution). [00:06:06]
