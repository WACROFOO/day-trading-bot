# Top 5 Reasons Why Traders Lose Money

## Summary

Identifies the five primary psychological and behavioral failures that cause traders to lose money. Emphasizes that losses aren't due to lack of effort but fundamental misalignment between skill level and risk taken. Teaches proper profit/loss ratio statistics, market environment assessment, and the importance of patience and discipline as non-negotiable skills.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:03:30] Trade below your skill level (smaller position sizes than intuition suggests) until proven profitable over 18+ months.
- [00:06:44] Use a 2:1 profit-to-loss ratio minimum: if you risk $1, aim to make $2 back to set the bar lower for accuracy.
- [00:15:21] When trading first position of the day, use starter size to assess market condition, not full size.
- [00:15:51] If the first trade fails (even with tight stop), recognize immediately that market is cold and reduce overall activity for the day.
- [00:31:22] Practice with simulator before risking real money; always manage risk first, profit second.

## Setups and patterns

- **Beginner's luck followed by drawdown pattern** — initial wins due to hot market, then panic selling losses that give back early gains. [00:04:36]
- **Market environment shift adaptation** — when market transitions from hot to cold, reduce position size rather than increase it (counter-intuitive). [00:20:22]
- **Starter position technique** — first trade of day with small size shows whether market is conducive to strategy. [00:12:27]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Scanner (leading percentage gainers)** — used to assess overall market heat; count of stocks up 100%+ indicates market strength. [00:13:15]
- **Five Pillars of Stock Selection** — scanner-based filter for high-quality trade candidates; mentioned in separate video. [00:09:55]
- **Candlestick chart patterns** — strongest patterns should be identified and traded only; mentioned in separate video. [00:26:40]

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:02:28] Beginner trader skill level: start at level 1, should trade with 100-share positions.
- [00:03:40] Beginner mistake: trading 10,000-30,000 shares at level 1 skill.
- [00:06:02] Risk/reward ratios: 1:1 ratio requires 50% accuracy to break even; 2:1 ratio requires only 33% accuracy to break even; 1:2 ratio requires 66% accuracy.
- [00:09:49] Experience example: trader after 18 months profitable, but quit after 5 years total; $50/day vs $500/day (10x difference).
- [00:09:51] Scaling math: moving from 100 to 1,000 to 10,000 shares = 10x scaling per step.
- [00:12:06] Average winners: 7 minutes; average losers: 16 minutes (shows improper trade management).
- [00:12:16] Share size example: 20,000 shares dropping $1/share = $20,000 loss.
