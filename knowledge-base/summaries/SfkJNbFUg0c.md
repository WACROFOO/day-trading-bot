# $EVAX +130% on the Beaten Up Bottom Bounce Day Trading Setup

## Summary

Setup for trading stocks that have been beaten down for extended periods and then bounce sharply off multi-month lows, often combined with reverse stock splits that reduce float. Demonstrates entry strategy waiting for news catalyst and momentum confirmation rather than predicting the bounce. Shows real trade examples on EVAX (+130%) and SPRC.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:00:25] Do not buy stocks that have been beaten down and are still trading sideways; wait for them to start bouncing with volume and momentum first.
- [00:00:41] Buy beaten-up stock entry when price breaks above VWAP (volume-weighted average price) with volume confirmation.
- [00:03:05] Do not preempt reverse splits; wait for news to come out AFTER the split before trading.
- [00:04:02] Stock must maintain price above $1 per share to remain on NASDAQ or NYSE; below $1 triggers delisting notice and forces reverse split.
- [00:05:09] 10-to-1 reverse split example: 50-cent stock becomes $5 after split; float reduces proportionally (10M shares becomes 1M shares).
- [00:06:27] Float reduction from reverse split increases volatility potential; watch for news catalyst after split to enter.
- [00:08:04] Entry trigger: wait for news announcement, then scan for beaten-up chart with recent reverse split showing lead gapper status.
- [00:15:01] Do not short small-cap reverse-split stocks near lows; float reduction causes "crazy things to happen" with uncontrolled upside gaps.
- [00:15:20] Never hold swing-short position in small-cap with small float due to borrow fees and margin exposure risk.

## Setups and patterns

- **Beaten-up bounce** — stock at multi-month/multi-year lows (defined low risk if it cannot go much lower) suddenly bounces sharply on catalyst. [00:00:19]
- **Reverse-split sequence pattern** — IPO → gradual decline to near-$1 → reverse split to $5-$6 → brief news pop → float dilution offering announced → decline back to $0.50 → another reverse split (cycle repeats). [00:03:20]
- **Gap-up, selloff, gap-up pattern** — SPRC example showed repetitive pattern of gap up, sell off, gap up, sell off suggesting predictable jack-knife rejections. [00:02:38]
- **Jack-knife reversal** — stock squeezes to high (e.g., $4.10), then flushes down (e.g., $3.42), reverses and retests high, fails again. Repeated multiple times indicating choppy entry trap. [00:11:20]
- **Topping tails** — repeated appearance of topping tails on rejections at resistance level ($4.28 on SPRC) signals seller control. [00:12:05]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Leading gapper scan** — daily rank of stocks by percentage gain; beaten-up bounce plays appear at top of gapper list after news. [00:08:11]
- **VWAP (Volume Weight Average Price)** — entry trigger when stock breaks above VWAP on beaten-up bounce with volume. [00:15:28]
- **Daily 200-moving average** — upside target on beaten-up bounce; stock can squeeze from current low toward 200-MA for multi-day move. [00:16:25]
- **SEC filings (S-3 / F-3)** — shelf registration shows company has right to offer up to $100M in shares, indicating dilution risk later. [00:09:14]
