# How to GET PAID to Trade with a Market Liquidity Strategy

## Summary

Explains how market makers profit from arbitrage (the spread). Shows how maker-taker fee structure gives traders rebates for adding liquidity and charges fees for taking it. Teaches that direct-access brokers (like Lightspeed) can profit from liquidity rebates while market makers profit from very small spreads and high volume.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:02:12] Add liquidity (place buy/sell orders on bid/ask that sit and get filled by incoming orders) to earn rebates from exchanges.
- [00:02:15] Take liquidity (buy at ask/sell at bid immediately) to pay exchange fees.
- [00:05:30] Market makers are licensed to simultaneously post buy and sell orders at same time; retail traders cannot do this.
- [00:12:03] NASDAQ rebate is 0.0015 (roughly) per share for adding; charge 0.003 per share for taking.
- [00:13:35] Commission-free brokers use payment for order flow (send order to market maker, not exchange) so you don't get rebates.
- [00:15:42] Direct-access brokers (Lightspeed, Interactive Brokers, Guardian) route orders to exchange, allowing you to earn/pay exchange fees directly.

## Setups and patterns

- **Market maker role** — Middleman buying at bid and selling at ask, profiting from the spread. [00:02:07]
- **Arbitrage** — Market maker buying at one price and selling at slightly higher price moments later. [00:02:14]
- **Level two** — Shows bid and ask with market maker name, price, and share quantity available. [00:02:26]
- **Spread** — Difference between bid and ask; market makers create and profit from it. [00:02:42]
- **Liquidity** — Presence of other traders willing to buy or sell; tighter spreads with more liquidity. [00:00:23]
- **Illiquid market** — Few buyers and sellers; large spreads and slippage. [00:00:55]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- Level 2 market data window — Shows bid/ask depth by market maker.
- NASDAQ exchange — Historical context starting 1990s with small order execution system. [00:08:04]
- Lightspeed broker — $2 per trade commission; route to NASDAQ gets rebates/fees. [00:15:35]
- Guardian broker — Charges $0.001 per share (e.g., $1.20 for 1000 shares). [00:17:54]

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:03:08] Example: Ford stock $10 bid, $10.25 ask; market maker buys 1000 at $10, sells at $10.25 = $250 profit.
- [00:04:31] Market maker holding stock 3-4 minutes before exit = time risk they take.
- [00:12:06] Lightspeed $2 per trade; NASDAQ 0.0015 rebate on 1000 shares = $1.50; 0.003 fee on taking = $3.
- [00:14:02] Per-share brokers: Guardian $0.001 per share × 1000 shares = $1.20; Lightspeed $2 flat cheaper for large orders.
- [00:22:47] Example trade: buy 100 shares, add liquidity both sides = $0.20 rebate on bid + $0.20 rebate on ask = $0.40 total; plus spread profit = $1 total on 100 shares = $1.40 profit; scale to 1000 shares = $14; scale to 5000 shares = $20 (with 5000 shares).
