# Circuit Breaker Halt Explained (With Examples)

## Summary

Comprehensive explanation of circuit breaker halts, including halt codes (T1, H10, LULD), volatility thresholds for different stock tiers, how reference prices work in halt calculations, and real-world trading examples with video playback of halt events. Teaches traders how to identify when halts are likely and how to trade breakouts after halts resume.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:18:31] When a stock breaks its volatility band and holds outside for 15 seconds, it triggers a 5-minute halt.
- [00:19:41] Rule of thumb for trading halts: trade after first halt, trade after second halt, but do not trade after third halt or beyond.
- [00:26:47] When stocks halt going down, they usually open lower when resumed (due to lack of buyers); when halting going up, they usually open higher (shorts covering and momentum continuing).
- [00:28:05] When trading out of halts, add over the high of the halt if stock opens lower, or add at whole dollar levels (e.g., $4) if stock opens higher.
- [00:13:29] Stocks must hold at least 50% of the initial move during pullback to be tradeable on the first pullback setup.

## Setups and patterns

- **Limit Up Limit Down (LULD) pause** — volatility circuit breaker triggered when stock moves 5% (Tier 1) or 10% (Tier 2) in 5-minute rolling period between 9:45 a.m. and 3:35 p.m.; doubled (10% and 20%) at market open (9:30-9:45) and close (3:35-4:00). [00:05:00]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **TradingView** — used for charting and technical analysis in examples.
- **Level 2 market data** — shows bid/ask and order book; when pinned at a fixed price, indicates imminent halt. [00:21:40]
- **Bookmap** — order flow visualization showing sell walls (50,000+ share orders) that resist price breaks.

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:05:56] Tier 1 securities (S&P 500, Russell 1000, some ETPs): need 2,000,000+ shares daily average volume; halt threshold is 5% move in 5 minutes (9:45 a.m.–3:35 p.m.), doubled to 10% at open/close.
- [00:08:37] Tier 2 securities (stocks $3+, lower volume): halt threshold is 10% move in 5 minutes (9:45 a.m.–3:35 p.m.), doubled to 20% at open/close.
- [00:09:19] Stocks between $0.75 and $3: larger bands with 40% threshold at open/close.
- [00:16:08] COOL example: gapped up 32%, then ran another 60%, but halted zero times because reference price (rolling 5-minute average) kept resetting upward.
- [00:18:07] DRYSU example: halted 5 times over 35-minute period after secondary offering during halt.
- [00:06:58] Flash crash (May 2010): Dow lost almost 1,000 points in 10 minutes; later another 500-point drop (22% loss) in 1987.
- [00:33:14] Black Monday August 24, 2015: over 1,200 circuit breaker halts across the market.
- [00:34:33] SPY dropped from 196 to low of 182 then bounced back.
- [00:23:20] Reference price example: COOL at open had rolling 5-min avg price of ~425; 20% threshold = $5.10 needed.
- [00:14:06] 5-minute rolling period calculation: average of high (497) and low (416) = 456.50; 20% = 91 cents added.
