# My Bear Market Day Trading Strategy

## Summary

Bear market trading requires discipline and patience to succeed where many traders fail. This video teaches how to adapt your strategy to lower-volatility markets, maintain strict quality standards for setups, and manage the psychological challenges of reduced trading opportunities. The presenter shares a framework for profitability based on risk-reward ratios and stock selection criteria optimized for bear market conditions.

## Mechanical rules

- [01:00:01] Risk-reward minimum is 1:1; aim for 2:1 ratio. If risking 10 cents, target 20 cents.
- [00:51:02] Stop loss is placed at the low of the pullback (bull flag pattern).
- [00:47:33] Enter on the first candle that makes a new high after the pullback.
- [01:00:01] In a bear market, trade fewer setups but remain aggressive on high-quality opportunities.
- [00:43:40] Stock selection criteria: 10% or higher price move, price between $2–$20, 5x relative volume or higher, news catalyst present, float under 10 million shares (20 million maximum).
- [00:51:56] Include the wicks when measuring the high for entry signals.
- [00:42:45] In a bull market you may see five quality setups per day; in a bear market, five per week.

## Setups and patterns

- **Bull flag** — stock moves up sharply, then consolidates with a brief pullback, then breaks above the consolidation high. [00:46:02]
- **Micro pullback** — first candle makes a new high followed by a dip and another candle making a new high on very short timeframes (1-minute, 10-second). [00:47:10]
- **ABCD pattern** — bull flag pullback fails to make new high initially, then consolidates tighter before breaking above resistance in a stronger move. [00:56:34]
- **Parabolic** — vertical move with only micro pullbacks, no proper 5-minute pullbacks due to fast trader entry on each dip. [01:19:03]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Scanners** — radar system identifying stocks hitting moving average breakouts and high relative volume in real time. [00:46:25]
- **Level 2** — shows bid/ask orders; green prints indicate buying strength and potential breakout confirmation. [00:47:58]
- **High-frequency trading algorithms** — turn off when stocks are up 20–30%, creating opportunity for volatile manual trading. [00:55:43]
