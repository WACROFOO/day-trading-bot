# Equity Swaps Explained (and How They're Used in GameStop & AMC)

## Summary

Equity swaps are OTC derivative contracts between investment banks and hedge funds that allow leveraged positions in stocks without direct ownership or regulatory disclosure. Unlike direct market purchases, swaps create no market impact initially, enabling institutional traders to establish massive positions off-market. Retail traders should avoid heavily institutional stocks during option expiration windows when price battles are most intense.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:14:43] Avoid actively trading stocks with high institutional ownership; the price action becomes unpredictable tug-of-wars between large players.
- [00:14:43] Monitor option expiration dates (especially quadruple witching) and avoid trading those stocks on those days if you lack institutional scale.
- [00:18:45] Watch for "hit and offer" patterns where large sellers walk price down incrementally across multiple levels.
- [00:18:46] If a stock has heavy options volume and open interest, there will be greater battles at key price levels around expiration.

## Setups and patterns

- **Equity swap with interest payments** — $100M notional short position at $20/share, 6% interest rate (5% benchmark + 1% spread), quarterly settlement. [00:08:21]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- DTCC (Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation) — clears and records all equity swap trades; details are difficult to publicly access.
- Level 2 market data — shows "tranches" of orders where institutional battles occur; large sellers/buyers create resistance/support.
- Options chain analysis — indicates where big money will likely defend price levels.

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:07:01] GameStop example: hedge fund shorts 5 million shares ($100M notional) at $20/share through swap.
- [00:09:44] Interest rate calculation: 6% annual = 1.5% quarterly ($1.5M/quarter on $100M position).
- [00:10:22] If stock drops 10%, hedge fund gains $10M; if stock rises 10%, hedge fund loses $10M.
- [00:12:00] Investment bank hedges by buying stock on open market or using options to neutralize risk.
