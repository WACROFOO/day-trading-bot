# Support and Resistance

## Summary

Ross Cameron teaches how to identify and draw horizontal and ascending/descending support and resistance trend lines on stock charts. Support and resistance work because thousands of traders recognize and respect these levels—they are self-fulfilling prophecies. Horizontal levels at whole dollars and half dollars are most common and predictable, while ascending and descending lines require careful drawing but offer significant edge when properly identified.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:01:16] Trend lines are only valid because traders use them; they are self-fulfilling prophecies.
- [00:02:44] Horizontal resistance formed by two double top candles hitting same price level represents clear rejection point.
- [00:03:04] Multiple tests of a support/resistance level increase validity; more tests = stronger level.
- [00:03:17] Support levels that are tested multiple times across 4+ days form flat-bottom pattern.
- [00:06:01] When price breaks above resistance and comes back down to retest it, that level becoming support is ultimate confirmation of correct trend line.
- [00:07:28] Connect resistance to resistance, not resistance to support in same trend line.
- [00:08:43] Horizontal trend lines at whole dollars and half dollars are most respected levels.
- [00:15:29] When drawing ascending/descending lines, extend the line forward in chart settings to see future projections.
- [00:16:28] Ascending trend line validity confirmed by multiple tests (dip to support, bounce up, repeat).

## Setups and patterns

- **Double top** — two candles making similar highs at exact same level creates strong horizontal resistance. [00:01:10]
- **Flat-bottom consolidation** — price bounces sideways at support level across multiple days. [00:03:07]
- **Red-to-green move** — price tested ascending support, dips, then reverses to green and rallies higher. [00:08:00]
- **Bull flag** — ascending support + ascending resistance with price consolidating between two trend lines. [00:14:08]
- **Ascending support becoming resistance** — previously broken support level now acts as resistance when price comes back down. [00:12:08]
