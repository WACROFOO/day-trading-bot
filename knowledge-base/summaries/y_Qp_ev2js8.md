# Understanding the Pattern Day Trader Rule (PDT) for Day Traders

## Summary

Comprehensive explanation of the Pattern Day Trader rule created after the dot-com bubble crash to protect retail traders. Covers cash vs. margin accounts, the four-to-one intraday leverage, minimum $25,000 balance requirement, penalties for violations, and workarounds like swing trading, options, or offshore brokers.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:01:00] Day trading defined as buying and selling a stock in the same day (any time).
- [00:01:20] If you hold overnight, PDT rule doesn't apply (swing trading exemption).
- [00:05:55] On margin account, execute more than three trades in a five-day period = flagged as PDT.
- [00:06:50] PDT-flagged accounts must maintain minimum $25,000 balance.
- [00:07:22] If you have less than $25,000, you're limited to three day trades every five days.
- [00:07:38] Cash account allows trading but requires 3-day settlement between trades.
- [00:05:38] Leverage on margin account: 4x intraday, 2x overnight.
- [00:10:04] You get one free pass if flagged; second violation freezes account for 90 days.
- [00:17:43] Once account balance exceeds $25,000, you can trade before 90-day freeze period ends.
- [00:16:21] A round trip is buying and selling same-day; counts as one day trade.
