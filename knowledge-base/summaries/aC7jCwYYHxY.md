# How to use Moving Averages (Beginners)

## Summary

Moving averages are a core technical indicator for day traders, particularly the 9, 20, and 200 EMAs, which serve as support and resistance levels. This video explains the difference between simple and exponential moving averages, how traders use them to identify pullback entries and reversals, and why high-float stocks require careful application of these indicators.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:09:16] On intraday charts, use only the 9 and 20 exponential moving averages, not the 50, to avoid overcomplication.
- [00:10:13] Buy at pullbacks to the moving average on breakout trades; sell when the stock squeezes away from the moving average.
- [00:13:05] For reversal trades, short when price extends far from the moving average, targeting the 9 EMA on 5-minute or 20 EMA on 1-minute for the cover level.
- [00:14:52] When a moving average breaks and holds in the opposite direction, that signals a real trend shift worth trading for a longer-term move.
- [00:20:18] Use Bollinger Bands as a reversal filter: short when price trades outside the Bollinger Bands, covering on the move back inside.
- [00:22:48] Only trade stocks with high relative volume; moving averages are only respected on stocks where thousands of traders are watching.

## Setups and patterns

- **Bull flag with moving average pullback** — price rises sharply, consolidates at the 9 EMA, and breaks higher. [00:09:53]
- **Reversal/rubber band snap** — price extends far above moving average, then reverses back down to the 9 or 20 EMA. [00:13:04]
- **Moving average as resistance** — price approaches a moving average from below and gets rejected. [00:05:34]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- Exponential Moving Average (EMA) — used on 1-minute, 5-minute, and daily timeframes for entry/exit signals.
- 9 EMA — primary reference for pullback entries on intraday charts.
- 20 EMA — secondary moving average; slower to respond than the 9.
- 200 EMA — long-term support/resistance level; slow-moving and well-respected.
- Bollinger Bands — used to identify extremes in price; shorts taken when price trades outside bands.
