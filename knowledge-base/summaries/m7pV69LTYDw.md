# How to Start Day Trading (Step-by-Step Guide)

## Summary

Five-step framework for starting day trading: learning basic market concepts via simulator, selecting one proven strategy, practicing consistently in simulation, analyzing trading metrics, and transitioning to real money with small position sizes. Emphasizes the critical importance of discipline, understanding risk relative to reward, and building consistency before scaling position size.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:02:41] Discipline and consistent rule-following are essential; without this, consistently making $10 per day is impossible.
- [00:05:22] Market orders work from 9:30 a.m. until 4 p.m.; limit orders work from 4:00 a.m. until 8:00 p.m. depending on broker (thinkorswim: 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.).
- [00:05:33] Stop orders work only between 9:30 a.m. and 4:00 p.m.; pre-market and after-hours trading requires limit orders due to higher volatility and no market orders.
- [00:06:00] Use offsets (5 cents above current offer) when placing limit orders to increase chances of filling full position.
- [00:16:22] Stock selection: trade only stocks generally between $2 and $20 with five times relative volume, up at least 10% on the day, having a float under 20 million shares, and a news catalyst.
- [00:24:35] Reduce daily loss risk: only place 100 shares on first trade, gradually increase share size weekly (100 → 200 → 400 → 1000+) once proving consistency in simulator.
- [00:27:42] Never trade with real money until reaching multiple weeks (5-6 weeks) of consistent profitability in simulator with good metrics.
- [00:28:01] First draw-down in simulator is critical psychological milestone; recover from it before trading real money.
- [00:40:16] Risk management: calculate maximum loss per trade (entry price minus stop price × shares); maximum daily loss is $5,000; must have favorable risk-to-reward ratio (ideally 1:2.5 or better).
- [00:43:45] Only trade during optimal window; previously focused on morning hours, eventually narrowed to 7 a.m. to 11 a.m.

## Setups and patterns

- **First pullback pattern** — stock squeezed up with green candles, pulls back slightly, then first candle that makes a new high triggers entry; max loss at low of pullback candle [00:13:07]
- **Reversal trading** — stocks extended with consecutive green candles (e.g., 11 straight); catch reversal on first red candle [00:11:04]
- **Bull flag** — sharp move up followed by pullback then higher move [00:13:12]
