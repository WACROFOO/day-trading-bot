# $PCSA +175% on the Reverse Split Setup

## Summary

Detailed breakdown of the reverse-split beaten-up-bottom bounce setup, using PCSA (up 175% on the day) and EVAX (up 300% previous day) as examples. Explains how small-cap companies nearing delisting execute reverse splits to regain compliance ($1 minimum), then announce news triggering multi-hundred-percent bounces. Covers scanner criteria, price action quality, identification of hidden sellers/insiders, and the cycle of secondary offerings that eventually kill these plays. Includes real trade examples and profit-taking discipline.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:01:13] Reverse split indicator of potential big bounce; check daily chart for recent reverse split notation.
- [00:01:47] Price should start under $10 (ideally under $5) to create percentage gains large enough to attract traders and hit scanners.
- [00:02:00] Small stock that moves from $20-$30 down to $1-$2 on news bounce can easily hit 50-100-200% gains in one day.
- [00:03:06] Scanner criteria: stocks up 10%+ in short period; look for news catalyst at 8:30 a.m. or during market.
- [00:04:38] Daily chart must show: recent reverse split, beaten down chart history, stock hitting multi-year or historical low yesterday.
- [00:04:47] Bias: long side (not short) for these bottoming bounces.
- [00:05:34] Pattern quality: pullback should not retrace more than 50% of initial move; if retraces >50%, price action is broken.
- [00:06:21] Aggressive entry: breaking through high-of-day; arbitrary stop: 15-20 cents if rejected.
- [00:06:30] Psychological level resistance: stocks respect half-dollar and whole-dollar marks ($3.50, $4.00, $4.50, $5.00).
- [00:07:27] Level 2 observation: if bid/ask is static (e.g., $3.50 ask not moving despite buying), indicates iceberg order (hidden large seller).
- [00:10:01] Minimum price listing requirement: $1 per share on NYSE/NASDAQ; below $1 risks delisting to OTC.
- [00:11:09] Reverse split example: 20-to-1 split converts $0.10 stock to $2.00 stock overnight.
- [00:12:14] Float changes during split: pre-split 20 million shares ÷ 20 = 1 million post-split.
- [00:13:39] Maximum float: prefer under 5 million shares; even under 1 million is favorable.
- [00:14:29] Volume rotation: with small float but massive volume (e.g., 800,000 shares float, 43 million volume), shares trade many times (float rotation).
- [00:15:20] Secondary offering cycle: once bounce occurs, company may announce secondary offering, diluting stock and causing pullback.
- [00:21:38] Exit discipline: take profit and walk away; do not overstay once choppy price action begins.

## Setups and patterns

- **Reverse split beaten-up bottom bounce** — stock previously down to cents/pennies, reverse split brings it to $2-5 range, news/catalyst triggers bounce to 50-300% [00:00:22]
- **Initial pop and pullback** — first move up, then pullback >50% retraces (sign of weakness) [00:05:02]
- **Break of high-of-day with iceberg seller resistance** — stock tries multiple times to break through round number ($3.50), big hidden seller keeps price pinned [00:06:32]
- **Grinding higher with rejection** — stock grinds up through multiple round-number levels with repeated high-volume red candles on rejections [00:25:46]
