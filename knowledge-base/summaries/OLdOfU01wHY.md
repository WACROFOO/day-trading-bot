# 13 Day Trading Rules and Regulations You NEED to Know About

## Summary

Deep dive into day trading regulations that govern account management and trading activity. Covers the Pattern Day Trader (PDT) rule, account violations, wash sales, short-sale restrictions, circuit breaker halts, and other SEC/FINRA requirements that beginner traders must understand to avoid account lockouts and compliance issues.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:06:09] Pattern Day Trader rule: any trader with margin account executing four or more trades within five business days must maintain minimum $25,000 account balance.
- [00:07:43] A trade is defined by how many times direction changes; buy-sell = 1 trade; buy-buy-sell-sell = 1 trade; buy-sell-buy-sell = 2 trades.
- [00:24:34] Free riding violation: if you trade money before settlement, your account is restricted to settled cash only for 90 days and profits from the trade are seized.
- [00:25:12] Good faith violation: if you sell a position and buy a new one before the original trade clears, account restricted to trading with settled cash only for 90 days.
- [00:26:28] If you hit PDT violation on margin account under $25,000, broker will demand deposit or lock account; you can call broker to "reset" the violation once.
- [00:36:02] To short a stock: must have margin account; margin agreement required because you are borrowing stock.
- [00:36:59] Short sale restriction (SSR): if stock drops 10% from previous day's close, can only short on uptick; SSR activated for rest of day.
- [00:39:01] Cannot short on bid when stock has SSR; must place order on ask and wait for buyer.
- [00:44:06] Tier one securities (S&P 500 companies): halt if move more than 5% within 5 minute period; only 9:30am-4:00pm.
- [00:44:34] Tier two securities (other stocks) above $3: halt if move more than 10% within 5 minute period; only 9:30am-4:00pm.
- [00:47:32] Pre- and post-market halts: no circuit breaker halts from 4:00am-9:30am or 4:00pm-8:00pm; stock can move 900-1000% without halting.
- [00:50:10] Order spoofing: placing orders with no intention of them being filled, only to influence other traders' perception of support/resistance; illegal practice.
- [00:57:15] Insider trading: trading on non-public information received from insiders; illegal.
