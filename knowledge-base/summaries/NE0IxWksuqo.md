# Alligator Indicator Definition | Trading Terminology

## Summary

Introduction to Bill Williams' Alligator indicator—a metaphorical trend-following tool based on three simple moving averages used to identify when the market is trending ("alligator eating") versus consolidating ("alligator sleeping"). Designed to keep traders in winning positions longer and to signal trend exhaustion. Covers formula, entry/exit signals, and relationship to other oscillators (MACD, Stochastics).

## Mechanical rules

- [00:03:33] Buy signal: green line (5 SMA) crosses up above red line (8 SMA) and blue line (13 SMA); alligator is waking and about to eat.
- [00:04:02] Sell/exit signal: green line crosses back down below red line; alligator stops eating and is ready to sleep.
- [00:03:39] When all three moving average lines are twisted/intertwined together, the alligator is sleeping and the market is in consolidation; avoid trading during this phase.
- [00:04:12] Hold positions as long as green line remains above red line; the wider the spread between the three lines, the stronger the trend and hunger of the alligator.

## Setups and patterns

- **Alligator awakening** — three moving averages cross and begin spreading apart after a long period of being twisted together; the strongest signal for entering trend trades. [00:03:48]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **Alligator indicator** — comprises three simple moving averages: 5 SMA (green line, "lips"), 8 SMA (red line, "teeth"), 13 SMA (blue line, "jaw"). [00:02:40]
- MACD (moving average convergence divergence) — oscillator often paired with Alligator to confirm trend direction; redundant with moving averages. [00:05:48]
- Stochastics — oscillator used alongside Alligator to identify overbought/oversold conditions and divergences. [00:05:59]
- Commodity Channel Index (CCI) — alternative oscillator to pair with Alligator indicator for additional context. [00:06:55]
