# Short Selling Stocks Explained

## Summary

Comprehensive explanation of short selling mechanics, including margin account requirements, borrowing shares, days-to-cover limits, short interest, and the asymmetric risk profile of shorting. Illustrates short squeeze dynamics and institutional bias toward long-side trading.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:03:01] Shorting requires a margin account; cash accounts cannot short.
- [00:08:30] Set a stop loss immediately when shorting; losses can be theoretically unlimited.
- [00:08:45] Cover position before days-to-cover expiration; broker will auto-liquidate after that deadline.
- [00:15:03] Short on uptick (short sale restriction applies); cannot short on bid when SSR is active.

## Setups and patterns

- **Reversal after extended move** — Short stocks that are statistically extended on multiple consecutive green candles, expecting reversal. [00:04:57]
- **Short squeeze** — Stock with high short interest gaps up, forcing shorts to cover by buying, creating parabolic move. [00:12:31]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- Scanner for identifying highly extended stocks (many consecutive green candles). [00:04:28]
- Short sale restriction (SSR) indicator; shows as "SSR" or "CB" on order entry. [00:14:47]
- Days-to-cover metric; varies 7-30 days depending on stock. [00:09:45]

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:04:37] Autodesk short example: 13 consecutive green candles, reversal entry at $8067, cover at $8013 = $600 profit per 1,000 shares.
- [00:05:17] DRYS: moved from $4 to $100+ in four trading days, example of parabolic squeeze.
- [00:05:32] HMNY and RGSC: both experienced multi-day runs of 50-100%+ moves.
- [00:12:55] Short interest of 30-50% on float creates squeeze conditions.
- [00:12:58] Float under 100 million shares and catalyst = conditions for squeeze.
- [00:04:39] Entry on first candle making new low after extended move; tight stops essential.
