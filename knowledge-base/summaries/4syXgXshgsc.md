# What Beginners Get Wrong About Level 2

## Summary

In-depth guide to reading Level 2 market data as a tool for predicting short-term price movements. Teaches interpretation of buyer and seller order depth to confirm technical setups and manage risk, with emphasis that Level 2 should complement (not replace) chart analysis.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:06:46] When stock is moving, buy at the ask rather than waiting on the bid; avoid missing the opportunity to save one penny per share.
- [00:05:52] Never sit on the bid waiting for someone to sell—use ask when momentum is present.
- [00:17:37] Pullback should not exceed 50% of initial move; ideal pullback is top 20-25% of the range.
- [00:37:57] Use 9 exponential moving average on all timeframes (1-minute, 5-minute, daily).
- [00:50:22] Three-step process: (1) find stock via scanner, (2) analyze chart for technical setup, (3) pull up Level 2 to validate entry before executing trade.
- [00:50:32] Cancel trade if Level 2 shows big sellers on the ask or many red (sell) orders on time-and-sales.
- [00:32:42] Do not trade if spread is too wide (e.g., over $1 on penny stock indicates poor liquidity).

## Setups and patterns

- **Micro pullback** — stock squeezes up, pulls back briefly (often just one candle), then immediately moves higher; occurs with strong, high-float-rotated stocks. [00:04:13]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- Level 2 (market depth display) — shows bid/ask prices and order quantities at each price level.
- Time-and-sales tape — shows every order executed; green orders indicate buys, red orders indicate sells.
- 9 exponential moving average — used on multiple timeframes to identify support.
- Scanners (top gainers, high of day momentum, running up scanners) — initial tool to find qualifying stocks.
- Thinkorswim platform — used for Level 2 display and charting in small account challenge.

- [00:30:07] Spread tolerance: tight spread (1-cent on high-volume stocks) preferred; wide spreads ($1+ on penny stocks) indicate poor conditions.
