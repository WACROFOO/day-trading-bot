# 🫠 THIS is Why Beginner Traders are Losing (Even on Big Short Squeezes)

## Summary

Why beginner traders lose money during hot markets with extreme stock moves. The core issue: most traders don't adapt position sizing and entry strategy when markets transition from cold (low volatility) to hot (high volatility). Specifically addresses how to trade 100%-1000% stocks, manage risk on volatile plays, and identify when stocks are worth actively trading.

- [01:35] At minimum, stocks need 10% move to capture profitability; 2% moves insufficient without leverage.
- [05:50] Looking for stocks already up at least 10% as minimum before entering scanner-based trades.
- [05:54] Use 5% standard deviation bands: 99% of stocks trade within ±5% range; break of this range signals 1% of stocks making big moves.
- [07:14] Relative volume: Looking for 5x above average volume for high-relative-volume setups.
- [08:07] Stock price preferably between $2 and $20 for highest retail interest and biggest moves.
- [08:14] Float filter: prefer float under 20 million shares available to trade; smaller float = more volatility.
- [09:46] High of day momentum scanner alert criteria: up 10%, high relative volume, price $2-20, float under 20M.
- [12:08] Risk management rule: if stock is very volatile (large candle wicks), reduce share size to accommodate volatility.
- [13:38] Max loss per trade: use 2:1 profit-to-loss ratio minimum; only need 33% accuracy to break even at this ratio.
- [16:41] Bull flag structure: must have high volume on green candles, light volume on red candles, then high volume on breakout green.
- [16:47] Bull flag retracement rule: should not retrace more than 50% of initial move.
- [16:55] Bull flag rule: must hold above VWAP (volume weighted average price); break below VWAP signals weakness.
- [21:18] Dip trading entry: take starter position at support (e.g., $6), stop at support level just below (e.g., $5.80).
- [21:26] If support holds, look for bounce-back spring move; can then add to position or take profit.
- [22:27] Scaling strategy in hot market: starter position low, add on first candle to new high, add again on next high.
- [22:50] Stop loss management: Trail stop up as position moves higher; move stop just above previous resistance level.
- [24:37] Cold market strategy (opposite of hot): focus on breakout trading, not dip buying; buy first candle making new high, exit quickly.
- [26:44] Nine moving average: stock support should be reasonably close; if 9 MA is too far below, not a good entry.
- [31:54] Stop checking windows: actively trade between 7:00 a.m. and 11:00 a.m. Eastern based on 24,446 trades analyzed.
- [34:00] Wednesday and Thursday are most profitable days based on trade data analysis.
- [36:44] Do not buy at very top; be willing to skip trades if risk is too high; avoid buying at absolute resistance highs.

## Setups and patterns

- **Hot market dip buy** — A stock making a big move gets bought during pullback to rising support, held through next leg higher. [02:22]
- **Bull flag pattern** — Sharp move up from catalyst news, sideways consolidation, break of consolidation high on volume. [15:39]
- **ABCD pattern** — Stock comes up forming bull flag, does not make new high, pulls back, then attempts next leg up. [17:06]
- **Ascending support trend line** — Line connecting lows of recent pullbacks; shows where to buy dips. [20:29]
- **False breakout** — Big red candle flush down right after appearing breakout, catching traders in max loss. [18:27]
- **Topping tail** — Candle with long upper wick rejecting resistance, signaling potential reversal coming. [16:35]

## Indicators, tools, platforms

- **High of day momentum scanner** — Alerts on stocks hitting new highs while meeting volume and float criteria. [09:27]
- **VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)** — Orange line used as support/resistance; breaking below signals weakness. [16:47]
- **Nine moving average (9 MA)** — Used as dynamic support level for trend confirmation; should be reasonably close for valid entry. [26:44]
- **Level 2 market data** — Shows bid/ask stacks; used to identify support buyers stepping in. [23:52]
- **Time and sales (tape)** — Green orders = buying pressure, red orders = selling pressure; shift from red to green signals entry opportunity. [25:34]
- **Standard deviation bands** — ±5% bands identify when stock breaks out of normal range. [05:50]
