# 5 Cent Tick Pilot Program Explained

## Summary

Explanation of the SEC's 5-cent tick pilot program launched October 3, 2016, which increased the minimum spread for ~1,200 small-cap stocks from 1 penny to 5 cents for two years. The program aims to improve market maker incentives and small-cap stock support but reduces volatility and liquidity for retail day traders.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:01:43] On April 9, 2001, all stocks above $1 required 1-cent minimum increments.
- [00:12:06] 5-cent tick program launched October 3, 2016 for 2-year pilot.
- [00:12:15] Approximately 1,200 stocks affected by pilot program.
- [00:14:26] 5-cent spread means only 20 price points between each dollar (vs 100 previously).
- [00:28:31] Identify 5-cent tick stocks by looking at Level 2: quotes only at nickels and dimes.

## Numbers and thresholds

- [00:00:46] Prior to decimalization (pre-2001): minimum spread was 1/16th of dollar = 6.25 cents.
- [00:00:56] Post-decimalization (2001 onwards): minimum spread 1 penny.
- [00:02:04] Decimalization created 600-1,000% increase in number of price points.
- [00:02:22] Between $0.90-$1.00: 1,000 increments (1/100th of a penny).
- [00:02:25] Between $1.00-$2.00: 100 increments (pennies).
- [00:03:04] Market maker profit: 5-cent spread with 10,000 shares = $500 profit per cycle.
- [00:04:11] Market maker profit: 1-cent spread with 10,000 shares = $100 profit per cycle.
- [00:12:35] Siri float: 3.79 billion shares, market cap $21 billion.
- [00:12:52] Bank of America float: 10 billion shares, market cap $227 billion.
- [00:13:00] Sprint float: 2.79 billion shares, market cap $33 billion.
- [00:16:27] Ship float: 13 million shares, market cap $40 million (post-pilot trading similar to large caps).
- [00:08:46] IPOs between 2001-2011 dropped sharply vs prior 10 years.

## Claims needing verification

- [00:03:13] Greg Godsey quoted as saying decimalization is negative because it narrowed spreads.
- [00:08:08] IPO task force 2011 report claimed decimalization was harmful to IPOs.
- [00:08:46] Number of IPOs declined between 2001-2011 as result of decimalization.
- [00:10:03] SEC report stated impact on small caps and willingness for IPOs is "uncertain."
- [00:20:03] Initial reaction shows pilot program is reducing volatility in small caps as intended.
