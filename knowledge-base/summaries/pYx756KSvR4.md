# How to Trade Penny Stocks for Beginners (with ZERO experience)

## Summary

Ross Cameron teaches strategies for trading penny stocks (stocks under $3/share) on the NASDAQ and NYSE, covering how to identify the best opportunities, find entry points, manage risk, and profit from volatility in low-price stocks. The video uses a live trade example at 19 cents to illustrate the mechanics of micro-pullback strategy and the role of float and volume in stock movement.

## Mechanical rules

- [00:25:01] First candle to break through the high of the previous candle is a buy signal (candle-over-candle formation).
- [00:28:42] Stop loss placed at the low of the pullback wave, never wider.
- [00:18:05] Trade only penny stocks listed on NASDAQ or NYSE (not OTC); minimum 10 cents per share preferred to reduce risk.
- [00:13:17] Focus on stocks with small float after reverse splits, making supplies very limited.
- [00:20:45] Avoid stocks below $1 (sub-penny and triple-zero stocks).
- [00:06:27] Stock above $1 has minimum 1-penny spread; below $1 trades down to 1/100th of a penny.
- [00:34:18] Take half off the table as stock squeezes to lock in profit early when account is small.
- [00:31:02] Use trailing stops only in regular market hours (after 9:30am ET), not pre-market.
- [00:32:38] Do not use leverage on stocks under $5/share.

## Setups and patterns

- **Penny stock surge with demand imbalance** — low float + high volume + price increase creates rapid squeezes to 50%, 100%+ in hours. [00:13:03]
- **First pullback entry** — stock makes quick move up, pulls back, then enters on first candle making new high. [00:24:31]
