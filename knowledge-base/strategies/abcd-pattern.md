## 1. What it is

> *"a proper ABCD pattern essentially requires part one to be a failed bull
> flag and then part two is the bull flag resolves"* — `xgnqOu_fchA` 13:47

It is not a separate strategy. It is **the same micro-pullback / bull-flag
trade, taken on the second attempt**, after the first attempt failed to break
the high of day.

> *"it's essentially the same as a bull flag… it's basically bull flag take
> two, we're gonna try again"* — `xgnqOu_fchA` 11:36

## 2. The four points

His labelling, verbatim (`xgnqOu_fchA` 08:17):

> *"we have the pullback here which is A, and then B is this point. So the low
> is A, this high is B. When we pull back on point C right down here, we do not
> want to see it break below A."*

| Point | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A** | the low of the **first** pullback (after the initial spike) |
| **B** | the high of the push that **failed** to break the high of day — he calls this **the pivot** |
| **C** | the low of the **second** pullback |
| **D** | the break back up through B |

So the spike itself is the flagpole, *before* A. A is a low, not the start.

## 3. What he does — the actions, in order

**He buys at C, not at D.** This is the part that gets read backwards. He
explicitly rejects buying the break of B:

> *"if you had bought as it broke through new highs you'd be in way up here and
> that feels rather extended, because your support level is the low of the last
> pullback"* — `xgnqOu_fchA` 09:39

The trigger at C is the same one as always: **the first candle to make a new
high**, provided the stock is holding its 9 EMA.

> *"what I look for is that first candle to make a new high, and as long as the
> stock is holding its 9 EMA I'm interested in being a buyer right here"*
> — `xgnqOu_fchA` 11:22

**The stop is the low of the last wave** — C, not A:

> *"a stock is moving up, it pulls back, it moves up… so if I buy here my stop
> is way down here; if I buy here my stop is right here, the low of the last
> wave. So I'd rather buy in this area than buy up in this area."*
> — `xgnqOu_fchA` 10:25

If C is too far below the entry, he **tightens the stop to a psychological
level** instead of taking the full risk:

> *"our low is down here at like 475, so that's like a 40¢ stop, that's a
> little ways away… rather than set a stop way down here at 475, I'm just going
> to set a stop at like 501. Why not $4.99? … you're going to lose less if you
> stop out faster than if you risk the breakdown."* — `xgnqOu_fchA` 39:22

**B is where he adds, not where he enters.** Live, over and over:

> *"Added at 42 to buy the dip. Now looking for the break through 1760… 65 is
> the pivot on the ABCD on the 1-minute."* — `PWkGpDlBZZA` 21:12

> *"this one I'm gonna hold for the breakthrough 12 because we're on an ABCD
> pattern, so now I'm going to watch to add this at 12 15. Adding at 12 13,
> we'll add at 12 25 and 35."* — `5g30dmYlT4s` 18:21

> *"I'm going to buy dips on this for the ABCD pattern… added at 1680, bought
> the dip, now looking for a second attempt through 17."* — `93-JpzGTWCQ` 1:53

**He takes smaller size than on a clean flag**, because the pattern already
failed once:

> *"now going with small size initially, this is an ABCD pattern"*
> — `YuNvqwJftVY` 1:18:25

> *"it's a bit top heavy still so that carries some risk"* — `obfEy0ChpWg` 41:55

**He draws the pattern before it exists**, and treats it as a hypothesis:

> *"what I think this now needs to form the perfect ABCD pattern — this is what
> I'm going to draw out… if I can envision what would be a perfect ABCD
> pattern, then I can start to dial in potential entries."* — `qCRNRcU2h7E` 23:57

> *"sometimes I'm right and sometimes I'm wrong, sometimes it works and
> sometimes it doesn't"* — `i0j2W4RqtpM` 20:30

**Why the break of B pays when it comes:** shorts who sold the double top at B
have their stops at the high of day.

> *"they're going to most likely set their stop at the high of day… they might
> even start covering just before it breaks the high. So what that means for
> longs is that as this starts approaching this level we're going to start
> seeing a lot of buying volume."* — `xgnqOu_fchA` 12:47

## 4. What disqualifies it

| Rule | Quote |
|---|---|
| **C must not break below A** | *"if it breaks below that level, the stock is basically stair-stepping down… that's not bullish, I'm not a buyer on something like that"* (`xgnqOu_fchA` 08:45) |
| **The B→C pullback must stay shallow** | *"it did pull back a little bit more than you'd like for a typical ABCD pattern, it pulled back like 60%… an ideal ABCD pattern would be consolidation a little bit closer to the top"* (`G9lRdz0RaWE` 32:04). The bull-flag half of it *"will retrace not more than 50% of the move"* (`xgnqOu_fchA` 05:44) |
| **No third attempt** | *"if it fails a third time, it's not an A-B-C-D-E-F-G-H-I-J-K-L-M-N-O-P pattern. At that point that is just a stock that is really struggling to break out, it's just going sideways"* (`xgnqOu_fchA` 19:31) |
| **It must not take too long** | *"this really does need to go fairly quickly. If it takes too long then that by itself is a bit of a red flag, it's a warning sign"* (`xgnqOu_fchA` 19:51) |
| **Extended off the 9 EMA** | *"sometimes the ABCD pattern forms and it's a little extended off the nine moving average… it's not able to break through new high of day"* (`xgnqOu_fchA` 06:33) |
| **Volume must build into D** | decent volume on the move up, light on the pullback, *"volume is starting to creep up here… increasing volume means more buyers"* (`xgnqOu_fchA` 13:20) |

He also abandons it live when the pivot fails to hold:

> *"going flat, it's not holding above the half dollar. I'll let it do the
> 5-minute pullback and then try again. Lost about $400 on that trade."*
> — `PWkGpDlBZZA` 22:58

## 5. Timeframes

> *"I am happy to trade this on any time frame; they're usually the most
> powerful on the 5-minute chart… it's a very clear pattern, it has had the
> opportunity for a lot of people to see it, and because a lot of people have
> seen it you just have more eyes on the pattern, and that creates more buying
> volume."* — `xgnqOu_fchA` 13:59

**Multi-timeframe alignment is the reason he likes it:**

> *"a lot of times when we're having a 5-minute bull flag, a 5-minute bull flag
> usually is a 1-minute ABCD pattern… that is multi-timeframe alignment"*
> — `xgnqOu_fchA` 17:09

Live, that mapping is the thing he checks first:

> *"if this had been a 1-minute ABCD pattern we'd have perfect multi-timeframe
> alignment — we'd have the 1-minute ABCD going into a 5-minute bull flag
> breakout. This 1-minute broke down right here… it makes me more inclined to
> look for an ABCD pattern on the 5-minute."* — `PWkGpDlBZZA` 33:01

> *"you can see how it made a new high by 5 cents, but not a lot of
> follow-through, because you don't have multi-timeframe alignment."*
> — `PWkGpDlBZZA` 33:36

And the same 10-second finding as everywhere else in this project:

> *"it's a 10-second ABCD; it's also a one-minute ABCD"* — `obfEy0ChpWg` 1:23:33

**Formation time on the 5-minute: 30–45 minutes minimum** — one green, two red,
one green, two red, then the break (`xgnqOu_fchA` 18:11). Live he plans around
that: *"it's gonna take another five, ten minutes for the pattern to reset"*
(`qCRNRcU2h7E` 36:25).

## 6. Which stocks

The same five pillars, restated inside this lesson (`xgnqOu_fchA` 21:23–25:19):
news, price under $20, up ≥10% already, relative volume ≥5×, float <20M. His
worked example, SPRC, had **500,000 shares outstanding** and relative volume of
14 when it hit the scanner.

## 7. Register note

The mention split at the top matters. ABCD is **taught heavily and traded
constantly, but almost never named in the recaps** (3 of 68 files). In the
recaps he calls the same trades "buying the dip" or "micro pullback". So the
gap is vocabulary, not practice — unlike the third-pullback and halt findings,
where the divergence was real behaviour. The one recap where he names it and

## Summary — the mechanical version

1. Spike, first pullback → low is **A**
2. Push fails to take out the high of day → that high is **B**, the pivot
3. Second pullback → low is **C**. **Reject the setup if C < A**
4. **Enter** on the first candle to make a new high off C, while price holds the
   9 EMA. Smaller size than a clean flag
5. **Stop** at the low of C, or tighter at a whole/half-dollar just below entry
   if C is more than ~20¢ away
6. **Add** through B on volume; that is where shorts cover
7. **Abandon** if it fails at B a third time, if it drifts too long, or if the
   pivot does not hold once broken
