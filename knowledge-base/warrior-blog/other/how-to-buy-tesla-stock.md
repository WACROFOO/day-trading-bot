# How to Buy Tesla Stock

Tesla Inc ([Nasdaq: TSLA](https://warriortradingnews.com/quote/tsla/)), is an automotive and energy company specializing in the manufacture of electric vehicles. Based in Palo Alto, California, the company has been accelerating the world’s transition from fossil fuels with electric cars.

Founded in 2003 under the name Tesla Motors, the company is the world’s best-selling plug-in car maker in 2018. At the time of writing, Tesla had delivered more than 245,000 units. Currently, it has a market share of 12% in the plug-in segment sales.

Thanks to Tesla’s success, many investors have been looking to buy equity. Want to know how to buy Tesla stock?

Read on!

## How Tesla Stock Has Been Performing in 2019

Tesla is a publicly-traded company. Its [IPO](https://www.warriortrading.com/initial-public-offering-ipo/) was on June 29th, 2010 with the price at $17 a unit. Missed expectations and a string of surprises led to a sharp plunge in Tesla stock this year. In its second quarter, Tesla announced that the company had delivered 95,200 vehicles. This topped Wall Street estimates setting a new record.

[](https://warriortradingnews.com/quote/tsla/)

In May 2019, Tesla shares were in a state of free fall. In fact, they nearly erased $7 billion in market value. This was Tesla’s longest losing streak in nine months. This decline came after Tesla analysts slashed earnings estimates and price targets.

In the past 6 years, Tesla’s stock has fallen 20% in a week. Every time this has happened, a bounce always follows. So far, the stock has experienced a 42% decline in 2019 which experts project to be the worst annual performance for the company since its 2010 IPO.

The only other negative year was in 2016 when the shares fell 11%. Its best performing year was in 2013 when the stock rose 344%.

In 2018, Elon Musk [tweeted](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1026872652290379776) “Am considering taking Tesla private at $420. Funding secured.” This was followed by another tweet “Shareholders could either sell at $420 or hold shares and go private.”

On that day, the shares rose as high as $387.46 but since then, they have fallen 50%.
