Summary
 
Siebert is an old school style brokeridge with no charting software. Although Siebert has excellent customer service they have extremely high fees. Seibert is meant to target high net worth individuals.

Siebert’s brokerage services are only a small part of the services that it offers to its generally wealthy clients, and it shows.

While Siebert’s broker service may be adequate for wealthy investors who are looking to manage some or all of their portfolio themselves, it is inadequate for the contemporary [day trader’s](https://www.warriortrading.com/day-trader-definition-day-trading-terminology/) needs, and features some of the highest commissions and fees in the industry.

Let’s take a look at Siebert’s broker services, and see why it is better left to casual investors than hardcore and committed day traders.

## Pros

• Some of the best customer service in the industry.

• Broker services connected to a wide range of wealth management products and services.

• No account minimum required.

• Mobile functionality.

## Cons

• Very expensive commissions and fees.

• Archaic trading execution platform.

• No analytical or charting software.

• Takeover concerns.

## Fees and Commissions

• [Stocks](/stock-definition-day-trading-terminology/) and [ETFs](/exchange-traded-funds-etf/) – $14.95 per trade up to 1,000 shares, then an additional per share price depending on trade volume above 1,000.• [Options](/option-definition-day-trading-terminology/) – $34 minimum regardless of order size, and then a per contract charge up to a maximum of $370.

• [Mutual funds](/mutual-fund-definition-day-trading-terminology/) – $35.

• Corporate bonds – $2.50 to $3.50 per bond depending on order size with a minimum $35 per trade charge.

• Foreign [securities](https://www.warriortrading.com/security-definition-day-trading-terminology/) – an additional $75 per trade on top of any other fees.

## Trading Platform

Siebert’s trading platform is among the least sophisticated and aesthetically appealing on the [market](https://www.warriortrading.com/stock-market-definition-day-trading-terminology/).

It is simply a trading window that allows you to input your order without any kind of charts, analysis, research, newsfeeds or any of the other features that are standard on all other contemporary trading platforms.

That said, the platform does allows the user to input a range of complex orders and trigger conditions, so users will feel comfortable that they can create and execute the same complex positions that they can expect to on more sophisticated platforms.

While investors who make a small number of long-term trades may appreciate the simplicity of Siebert’s trading platform, the average day trader will find it flatly inadequate for their needs.

## Charting, Analysis and Research

The bare-bones trading platform that Siebert offers has no charts on which to perform any kind of technical or statistical analysis. Therefore, users will need to employ outside charting software and data feeds if they want to perform any kind of analysis for their trading.

Siebert does offer a range of market research, but it is quite limited compared to what most day traders would expect to receive from a paid or free research service.

## Customer Service

Because Siebert is a client-centric wealth management firm, it offers some of the highest quality and most personalized customer service in the industry.

Not only are Siebert reviews full of extremely positive comments on the quality of their customer service, but Siebert has even been noted in popular financial magazines for its industry-beating levels of customer service quality.

## Takeover Concerns

It is important to note that Siebert has long been eyed as a willing [acquisition](https://www.warriortrading.com/acquisition-definition-day-trading-terminology/) target, with discussions stretching as far back as 2014 as noted by Bloomberg News.

Considering the niche services provided by Siebert and the extremely dated nature of many of its broker services, it is difficult to tell what the firm would look like in the event of an acquisition, and what sort of clients, if any, the acquiring company would keep.

Any acquiring broker service would be likely to switch all clients over to its existing digital trading platform, but this may mean reapplying to the new broker from scratch.

Alternatively, a wealth management acquirer would be more likely to scrap the broker services altogether, leaving existing broker service clients starting from scratch in the search for a new brokerage.

Neither future scenario in a takeover situation offers much hope for the long term prospects of Siebert as a strictly broker service.

Therefore, traders are cautioned against using the broker services of Siebert without also using their wealth management services as the primary reason for maintaining a client relationship.

## Siebert As a Full Service Firm

To understand Siebert’s broker services, it is critical to understand that they are merely an extension of Siebert’s role as a full service wealth management firm.

This means that the entire set-up of their broker service is designed to serve wealthy clients looking to input a small number of trades as a long term investor, either as a partial or complete direct management of their portfolio with Siebert.

Clients of their wealth management services have access to input and advisory services to guide their trades that straight broker service clients will not have, so the wealth management clients have little need for the robust range of analytical tools that most retail traders have come to rely on.

Similar in the range of fees and commissions that Siebert charges its clients, these are designed for wealthy clients who are inputting a small number of high value trades, where the fees and commissions will be next to insignificant when compared to the total trade value.

While[ day traders](https://www.warriortrading.com/day-trader-definition-day-trading-terminology/) making a number of small trades each day will find these prices prohibitive, they are next to meaningless for a wealthy individual making a few large block trades each month or year.

From this perspective, the arrangement for Siebert’s broker services makes perfect sense, and is well-reviewed by the wealth management clients who choose to use it.

Day traders, on the other hand, would be much better served by seeking out a full service broker than can offer them the advanced trading platform, analytical tools and pricing structure that will allow them to operate their day trading strategies effectively.

## Final Thoughts

Siebert’s broker services are designed to be an extension of its wealth management services, and they are meant for wealthy individual to input a small number of long term trades.

The extremely simplistic trading platform, lack of charting and analytical software and very high fee and commission structure makes Siebert’s broker services effectively unworkable for the average day trader.

Unless a day trader also required the full services of a wealth management firm, it is recommended that they seek out an alternate source for broker services.
