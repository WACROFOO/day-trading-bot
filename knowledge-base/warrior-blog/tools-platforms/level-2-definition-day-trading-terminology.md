## What Is Level 2 Trading?

Level 2 market data provides a detailed look at an order book, displaying the best bid prices and ask prices for a stock alongside the current bid. This visibility allows market participants to analyze [liquidity](https://www.warriortrading.com/liquidity-definition-day-trading-terminology/) and understand potential price movements in real time.

As the name suggests, Level 2 data goes beyond Level 1, which only shows the highest bid price and the lowest [ask price](https://www.warriortrading.com/ask-price-definition-day-trading-terminology/). Level 2 trading can reveal the full market depth, giving you a clearer picture of the number of shares available at various price levels.

Think of it like pulling back the curtain on the stock market. You’re not just seeing the current price — you’re seeing who’s buying and selling, how much they’re trading, and at what prices. Trying to trade without Level 2 is like trying to drive with one eye closed. Can you do it? Technically, yes. Does it make sense to do it? No.

## What Is the Level 2 Window?

Here’s what you’ll find in a typical Level 2 window:

- Bid and Ask Columns: These display the [bid prices](https://www.warriortrading.com/bid-price-definition/) buyers are willing to pay and the ask prices sellers are asking for.

- Order Sizes: The number of shares available at each price level.

- Market Makers and ECNS: [Market makers](https://www.warriortrading.com/market-maker-definition-day-trading-terminology/) and [electronic communication networks (ECNs)](https://www.warriortrading.com/electronic-communication-network-ecn-definition-day-trading-terminology/) facilitate trades by creating liquidity, matching orders, and setting ask quotes.

For instance, if a stock is trading at $50 and there’s a large order at $49.80, you might notice that sell orders are dominating, which could signal a downward move. On the other hand, spotting a big buyer just below the current stock price can indicate strong support.

## How To Use Level 2 To Make Trading Decisions

The beauty of Level 2 trading lies in its ability to help you interpret [price action](https://www.warriortrading.com/price-action-definition-day-trading-terminology/) and market depth. Here’s how you can leverage it in your trades:

- Spotting Support and Resistance Levels: Look for large bid prices to identify support and significant ask prices for resistance.

- Timing Your Entries and Exits: Use Level 2 data to find the best moments to jump into a trade and lock in profits.

- Analyzing Market Sentiment: Watching real-time orders helps you understand whether buyers or sellers are in control.

Let me share an example: If you’re watching a stock at $50 and see a massive sell order at $51, it’s a clue that breaking above $51 could be challenging. But if that ask price starts shrinking, it could be a signal to prepare for a breakout.

## What Are Some Advanced Techniques for Level 2 Data?

If you’re ready to go deeper, here are some advanced ways to maximize Level 2 data:

[ ](#)

### Routing Orders

When placing a trade, you can route your orders through specific ECNs like [NYSE](https://www.warriortrading.com/nyse-definition-day-trading-terminology/), ARCA, or [NASDAQ](https://www.warriortrading.com/nasdaq-definition-day-trading-terminology/) or let your broker choose for you. Personally, I prefer direct routing because it gives me faster order execution compared to letting your brokerage decide the route. Faster trades mean better prices, especially in volatile markets.

[ ](#)

### Dark Pools

[Dark pools](https://www.warriortrading.com/dark-pool-definition-day-trading-terminology/) sound mysterious, but they’re basically hidden liquidity. Sometimes, large trades occur away from public view. By routing your order to a dark pool, you can tap into these hidden price levels for better execution.

[ ](#)

### Time and Sales Data

Pairing Level 2 with time and sales data can give you a complete picture. While Level 2 shows pending orders, time and sales reveal completed trades, confirming the direction of price movements.

## What Are Some Common Mistakes To Avoid?

Even the best tools are useless if you don’t apply them correctly. Here are common pitfalls to look out for:

- Ignoring Volume: Without significant trading volume, gaps in the order book might lead to false signals.

- Overtrading: Not every opportunity is worth pursuing. Stick to your strategy.

- Forgetting Catalysts: Always check for news or events driving price movements. These could be earnings reports or sector-specific updates.

- Volatility: Failing to account for sharp price movements can result in poor trading decisions.

- Don’t Forget: Past performance does not guarantee future results.
