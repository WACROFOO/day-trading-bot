## Factors to Consider When Selecting a Broker

Products offered

It is important to consider the type of products offered by the broker. This should go hand in hand with geography. For example the brokerage firm may offer access to top markets like the London Stock Exchange, NYSE and NASDAQ but not Japan and China. When it comes to products, look for a broker that allows you to trade with all US stocks as well as mutual funds, forex and futures among others.

Security

This is very important especially when it comes to your funds. One feature to check out is the platform’s security. Does it have a valid SSL certificate or not? If you want to know whether a broker’s platform is secured with SSL, check the URL. It should start with “https” and not “http.” It is also important to consider what investor protection law you are under.

Pricing

What is the broker’s minimum balance? Is there a minimum fee charged for every trade? What about withdrawal fees? Does the broker charge any inactivity fee? Learning more about pricing will help you select a broker who is favorable to your budget.

## Dealing with Taxes

It is important to note that trading in the US stock exchange will result in you being charged a 30% tax on dividends paid out by US brokerage firms. This rule is overseen by the IRS and the brokerage firm is required to withhold the amount. If you are currently residing in a country that has tax treaties with the US, then you will end up paying a lower tax rate. Capital gains are exempt from US tax for non residents but that does not mean you are exempt from capital gains tax in your country.

Since you are required to pay taxes for dividends accrued, then you have to apply either a Social Security Number or Tax identification number from the IRS. This will ensure that you are able to file your tax returns as well as offer the IRS a way of tracking your tax returns.

However, there are few different ways that your account can be held and that will also affect how you pay taxes and what form you will receive from the IRS. Make sure to follow with a tax specialist from your country so you know you are trading according to the law.

If you are a US resident, you are required to pay taxes on interest, dividends and capital gains.

## Final Thoughts

As a non US citizen, it is important to know that today you can trade at the NASDAQ and NYSE even though you don’t reside in the US. To ensure this is possible, the first thing you ought to do is to select the right broker. Take your time while doing so in order to avoid scammers.

Next, make sure that you have a social security number or tax identification number. This will ensure that you keep trading as well as filing returns for any capital gains and dividends. Lastly, learn the best strategies to ensure your success.
