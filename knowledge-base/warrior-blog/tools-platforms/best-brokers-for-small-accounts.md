If you’re looking to day trade and trying to find a broker that can help you meet your needs, it is advisable to have a thorough look at the best options – and that is what we’re going to discuss in this post.

To help you choose an online broker that works best for your small trading account, we have reviewed these brokers with a focus on trading fees, account minimums, execution speed, education resources, research availability, [trading tools](https://www.warriortrading.com/the-5-best-tools-for-day-trading/) and platform, as well as ease of use.

## What is an online broker?

An online brokerage is a platform/firm that offers accounts that traders can use for trading financial securities, such as stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), mutual funds, and bonds.

The accounts that brokers offer their customers are also known as taxable accounts since the profit made within them is taxed as a capital gain. If you set up an account with your broker, you can transfer money in and out of the account in the same way as a bank account.

Brokerage firms allow these accounts to be opened for an individual, more than one individual, or on behalf of persons under age 18.

## Opening an online brokerage account

When creating a trading account with a broker, most brokers will require new clients to provide personal information, including legal name, date of birth, social security number, email address, and mailing address.

Your broker may also demand to know your employment status, trading objectives, trading experience, net worth, and annual income.

The rule defines a “pattern day trader” as any trader who executes four or more “day trades” within five business days, so long as the number of day trades represent more than 6% of the trader’s total trades in the margin account during that same 5-business day period.

Clients can buy and sell securities with TradeZero America’s mobile app. It functions on Android and Apple phones and tablets.

## Bottom Line

Most day traders dream of having a huge trading account that has tons of leverage and all the whistles and bells. However, only a few of us begin to trade with a big account, and that’s totally okay.

Starting out with a small account will help you develop and sharpen skills that will prevent you from blowing up that account when it grows fat in the future.

So, don’t despair if you’re day trading on a small account. Almost everyone starts out small. Once the account gets big, you can add more positions and take more risks.

Utilize this part of your trading journey and one day, you’ll remember with a touch of nostalgia the time you could get in and out of a volatile stock trade throughout the day.
