# Start with evidence, not recent winners

No strategy open to a France-based, long-only, unlevered retail investor has strong evidence of beating a cheap global equity ETF after publication, costs and French tax; the realistic menu is the equity premium itself plus a few tilts and overlays that either add roughly 0–2 percentage points a year (momentum, quality) or roughly halve drawdowns at a similar Sharpe ratio (slow trend filters, a managed-futures sleeve). Ranked on evidence-adjusted net value, the top three candidates are a long-only momentum tilt, a monthly trend-filtered multi-asset allocation and a 10–20% managed-futures UCITS sleeve, followed by quality, value and minimum-volatility tilts; the draft's own swing setups (RSI(2) pullbacks, sector rotation), stock breakouts, calendar effects and option selling rank last, and post-earnings drift, short-term reversal, pairs trading, intraday breakouts, small caps and LLM price prediction are excluded. The Ross Cameron dataset cannot test any of these, because it has the wrong horizon, the wrong universe (microcaps, where published anomalies die), too little clean history and vendor claims with synthetic dates; it is useful only for the post-mortem. "List the most profitable strategies right now" is the wrong starting question: published returns fall about 26% out of sample and 58% after publication, and factor leadership flipped repeatedly in 2025–26, with momentum crashing in October 2026, value up 44% this year only six years after its worst drawdown on record, and trend swinging from a record loss to +15.8%. The better method is a pre-registered evidence funnel with explicit decay, cost, timing-luck and tax haircuts, a budget of about ten trials per strategy family, decades-long backtests on index and factor histories, a held-out final five years and region, and an after-tax comparison against a PEA-held index fund. For the current draft, that means testing the trend-filtered core, a momentum tilt run head-to-head against sector rotation, and a managed-futures sleeve first; demoting RSI(2), which the post-publication evidence says cannot pass gate G5; and accepting that the draft's defensive-sector core is a minimum-volatility bet, a style that has lagged by about 3 points a year since 2015.

## The Ross dataset answers a different question

**The Ross dataset is not appropriate for backtesting any candidate in this report.** It holds transcripts and summaries of Ross Cameron's videos, a metadata index of 2,211 videos whose upload dates are synthetic below the year (1,867 of them carry a date of 31 July) ([knowledge-base data README](../../../knowledge-base/data/README.md)), and no stored price history at all: the paper-trading platform fetches small-cap quotes live from free data that runs about 15 minutes late ([LESSONS.md](../../LESSONS.md)). The candidates below need something entirely different: 20–50 years of daily or monthly total-return series for broad indices, factor portfolios, government bonds, gold and futures-trend indices, converted to euros. As the next section shows, telling a Sharpe ratio of 0.5 apart from luck after ten trials takes roughly 18 years of data, and nothing in the Ross set covers those instruments or that span.

**The universe is the deeper problem, because small caps are where published edges go to die.** Microcaps make up about 60% of US listed names but only about 3% of market capitalisation, and about 65% of 452 published anomalies fail a basic significance test once microcaps are neutralised by value-weighting ([Alpha Architect on Hou, Xue & Zhang](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/); [HXZ, RFS 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html)). Machine-learning stock-picking profits fall about 62% when microcaps are excluded ([CUHK summary of Avramov, Cheng & Metzker](https://www.media-outreach.vn/View/45101/cuhk-business-school-research-looks-at-the-limitations-of-using-artificial-intelligence-to-pick-stocks)), and post-earnings drift has been dead even in microcaps since about 2016 ([Martineau, CFR 2022](https://cfr.ivo-welch.org/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)). The size premium itself turned negative after 2015, with US SMB at −2.2% a year over 2015–2026 ([Ken French Data Library, researcher calculation](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)), and size is one of only three themes that fail Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen's global replication ([JKP, NBER w28432](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)). An edge "found" in Ross's universe would therefore not transfer to the UCITS ETFs and large caps this investor can trade, and it would face the cost structure that sank the old project, where modelled costs alone put every trade **0.25R in the hole** before it started ([LESSONS.md](../../LESSONS.md)).

**The transcripts are claims, not data.** The repo's own audit found that the corpus's rules imply about **+3.2% per day**, that its parameters contradict each other (float caps of 20M and 10M stated about equally often), and that 424 of 963 actionable "support" mentions name no level at all ([LESSONS.md](../../LESSONS.md)). The dates cannot be joined to prices point-in-time, and the educator is visible precisely because he survived. The population evidence for that trading style is bleak: fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders earn predictable positive returns net of fees ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, JFM 2014](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)), 97% of Brazilian futures day traders who persisted beyond 300 days lost money ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)), and 89.4% of French retail CFD and forex clients lost money over four years with no learning effect ([AMF 2014](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_etude_analyse/epargne_prestataire/Etude%20des%20resultats%20des%20investisseurs%20particuliers%20sur%20le%20trading%20de%20CFD%20et%20de%20Forex%20en%20France.pdf)). Across these datasets, trading frequency is the strongest predictor of loss; in the classic US broker study, the most active households earned 11.4% a year net against 18.5% for infrequent traders, with similar gross returns, so costs explained the gap ([Barber & Odean, JF 2000](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/Returns.html)). The dataset keeps three legitimate jobs: the ROADMAP's Step 1 post-mortem of where your own money went, test fixtures for order-handling code, and a "do-not-test" list of rules already shown to rest on inputs nobody can compute. Keep it as an archive and a mirror, not a laboratory.

## Ranking by recent profit selects yesterday's winners

**"Most profitable right now" mixes up decay, luck and risk, and every one of them points the wrong way.** Across 97 US predictors, returns were 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication ([McLean & Pontiff, JF 2016](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)), and the Sharpe ratios of published strategies roughly halve after publication ([Falck, Rej & Thesmar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)). Once trading costs, publication and the post-2005 era are all accounted for, the average anomaly's expected return is "a measly 4 bps per month" ([Chen & Velikov, JFQA 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)). Retail backtests barely predict anything either: across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe explained **less than 2.5% of the variance** in out-of-sample Sharpe, and developers who ran more backtests overfit more ([Quantpedia summary of Wiecki et al.](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)). With more than 300 published factors on the books, Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue that any new factor needs a t-statistic above 3.0, not the usual 2.0 ([NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)).

**The last two years show how badly a recency ranking would have timed every major style.**

A list compiled in November 2026 would put value and trend on top after their runs and momentum near the bottom after its crash, which is exactly the chasing pattern behind the investor "behaviour gap": the average US fund dollar earned **1.2 points a year less** than the funds themselves over the decade to 2024, rising to 1.6 points in alternatives, against about 0.1 points in rarely touched target-date funds ([Morningstar, Mind the Gap 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025)). Recent windows also carry almost no statistical information. Eleven or twelve years of out-of-sample factor data produce t-statistics of only about 1–3, too weak to tell decay from bad luck for any single factor, and momentum has had several decade-long losing stretches in data back to 1801, including 2004–2013 ([Swedroe, etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-momentum-faltering-part-ii)). Self-reported winners are a biased sample: on Collective2, a verified-execution platform, one member checked 19 strategies older than five years and found "zero strategies that didn't have at least one calendar year with a net loss", while another paper-followed 700–800 new strategies and saw "all of them" fail ([Collective2 forum](https://forums.collective2.com/t/are-there-any-c2-strategies-that-stay-profitable/16231)). The forum evidence used in this report comes from Collective2, QuantConnect and The Chartist forum; Reddit could not be accessed during the research, and nothing here relies on it.

### A pre-registered evidence funnel with hard numbers

A sounder process starts from the objective rather than the leaderboard. Fix the objective first, in euros and after French tax, against the investor's real alternative: a world index fund held in a PEA. Then admit only candidates that have a durable mechanism (a compensated risk premium, or a behavioural mispricing protected by limits to arbitrage), that have replicated across countries and after publication, and that survive costs in liquid instruments this investor can actually trade. Cut every candidate's published numbers before running a single backtest, spend a small and fully logged trial budget on decades of data, hold out a final stretch of time and an untouched region to look at once, and only then move to paper and live trading with abandonment criteria written down in advance. The table turns that funnel into guardrails.

| Guardrail | Concrete rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothesis space | Test only families that replicate across studies: momentum, value, quality/profitability, low-risk and time-series trend. Exclude size, seasonality, leverage, liquidity/friction, microcap-driven and intraday signals. | Both [HXZ](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/) and [JKP](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf) |
| Decay haircut | Plan on 40–60% of a published long-short premium; subtract 1–3 points a year from any published TAA backtest's CAGR | [McLean & Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [Chen, Lopez-Lira & Zimmermann](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10317) |
| Cost haircut | Deduct at least 1% of one-sided monthly turnover; prefer strategies under 50% monthly turnover; run everything again at 2× costs | [Novy-Marx & Velikov, RFS 2016](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf) |
| Timing-luck haircut | Subtract 0.57% a year from single-date monthly backtests; split rebalancing across 2–4 dates | [Allocate Smartly](https://allocatesmartly.com/diving-deeper-does-the-day-of-the-month-matter/); [Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2013/08/the-luck-of-the-rebalance-timing/) |
| Tax layer | Apply the 31.4% PFU to realised gains each year, with the 10-year loss carry-forward; compare against a buy-and-hold PEA benchmark | [service-public.fr F21618](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F21618) |
| Trial budget | Log every run, including "quick looks"; at most about 10 independent configurations per family; cluster correlated variants to estimate the effective trial count | [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf) |
| Minimum backtest length | Years ≈ 2·ln(N)/SR², which is about 18 years for 10 trials at Sharpe 0.5 and about 51 years at Sharpe 0.3 | [Bailey et al.](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf), researcher calculation |
| Significance | t ≥ 3 for any novel signal; t ≈ 2 only for an established family confirmed in another region or period; deflated Sharpe ratio ≥ 0.95 using the real trial count, skew and kurtosis | [Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592); [Bailey & López de Prado](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf) |
| Out-of-sample design | Hold out the last ~5 years plus one region (for example Europe) and look once; walk-forward any fitted parameter; estimate the probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) by combinatorial cross-validation and reject above ~0.2–0.3 (a judgement call, not a published cutoff) | [Bailey et al., PBO](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [Arnott, Harvey & Markowitz](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3275654) |
| Execution realism | A signal at the US close fills at the next European session for UCITS lines, never at the signal close | [QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/18821/timing-issue-using-market-orders-on-daily-resolution/) |
| Plausibility cap | Never extrapolate a backtest Sharpe above ~1 for a single long-only equity tilt; treat anything implying more than 20–25% a year as a red flag | [LESSONS.md A4](../../LESSONS.md) |
| Vendor discount | Treat index histories before launch dates as back-tests, and discount vendor-only evidence (MSCI, AQR, Man, Cboe and Concretum all sell products) | [MSCI USA Momentum factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-usa-momentum-index-usd-net.pdf) (index launched Feb 2013) |

**The arithmetic is unforgiving for a small project.** The draft's swing in-sample window, 2003–2016, is 14 years long, which by the minimum-backtest-length approximation supports only about **six independent configurations** at a target Sharpe of 0.5 before an equally good result is expected from noise (researcher calculation). Live trading cannot fill the gap: a realistic long-only tilt with an information ratio of 0.1–0.3 needs **44 to 400 years** of live returns to reach a t-statistic of 2 (researcher calculation, years = (2/IR)²). The evidence therefore has to come from long external histories, such as Ken French's US data from 1926, AQR's international factors from about 1990 and MSCI's indexes from the mid-1990s ([Ken French](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html); [AQR datasets](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets)). Live trading can verify the implementation; it cannot prove the edge.

Rank 0 is not a candidate; it is the hurdle every candidate has to clear. Sources for the figures in both tables appear in the narrative that follows them.

### Momentum, slow trend and managed futures earn the top three slots

**Rank 0, the hurdle, is the equity premium itself.** The US market's excess return rose from 6.1% a year (Sharpe 0.40) over 1963–2014 to **11.9% a year (Sharpe 0.77) over 2015 to September 2026** ([Ken French, researcher calculation](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)). PEA-eligible synthetic world trackers such as iShares WPEA and Amundi DCAM charge 0.20% ([justETF WPEA](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-profile.html?isin=IE0002XZSHO1); [justETF DCAM](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-profile.html?isin=FR001400U5Q4)). The premium's weaknesses are drawdowns of 55–58% and the behaviour gap, and every candidate below is judged by whether it improves on this after tax.

**1. Long-only momentum is the best-evidenced return tilt, and it is strongest outside the US.** Both large replication studies place momentum among the robust families ([Alpha Architect on HXZ](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/); [JKP](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)), and its post-publication record in Europe matches its in-sample one. AQR's European long-short factor earned 11.85% a year over 1990–2014 and **11.46% a year (Sharpe 1.10, t 3.74) over 2015 to August 2026**, with France at 11.29%, while the US version fell from 7.29% to 3.57% (t 0.84) ([AQR datasets, researcher calculation](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Betting-Against-Beta-Equity-Factors-Monthly)); Ken French's US momentum factor likewise dropped from 8.2% a year over 1963–2014 to 3.1% over 2015–2026 ([Ken French, researcher calculation](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)). That US-only decay fits Jacobs and Müller's finding that the US is the only market with a reliable post-publication decline ([JFE 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:1:p:213-230)). The long-only indexes this investor can actually hold confirm the pattern: over 2015–2025, MSCI World Momentum beat its parent by **1.97 points a year and in 8 of 11 years**, and MSCI Europe Momentum by 3.02 points, but MSCI USA Momentum added only 0.34 points, with a lower 10-year Sharpe (0.70 vs 0.81) ([MSCI World Momentum](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-world-momentum-index-usd-net.pdf); [MSCI Europe Momentum](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-europe-momentum-index-eur-net.pdf); [MSCI USA Momentum](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-usa-momentum-index-usd-net.pdf)). Costs are manageable. The indexes turn over 105–115% a year, UCITS World momentum ETFs (IS3R, XDEM, WMSE) charge 0.25% ([justETF momentum list](https://www.justetf.com/en/how-to/invest-in-momentum-etfs.html)), and momentum is one of the few mid-turnover anomalies that stays significant net of costs, especially with buy/hold bands and when combined with value and profitability ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). The price is crash risk. The long-short factor lost **34% in April 2009** and 57.8% from November 2008 to September 2009 ([Ken French](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)), crashes cluster in rebounds after panics ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf)), and the long-only index's relative loss of almost 10 points in October 2026 shows that holding only winners does not escape it. Because only about half of momentum's long-short profit sits on the long side ([Israel & Moskowitz](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:108:y:2013:i:2:p:275-301)), a realistic planning range is **0 to +2 points a year with 4–9% tracking error**, an information ratio of roughly 0.1–0.3.

**2. A monthly trend filter is the best-evidenced way to cut the core's drawdowns, and it pays for that with return.** Faber's rule holds each asset class only while its month-end price sits above its 10-month average and holds bills otherwise; a five-asset portfolio makes 3–4 round trips a year. In his own 2006–2016 out-of-sample revisit, timing beat buy-and-hold in 2008–09 and then **lagged stocks in six of the next eight years** ([Faber, JPM 2018](https://allocatortraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/A-Quantitative-Approach-to-Tactical-Asset-Allocation.pdf)). Independent replications show the same trade-off. On SPY the 10-month rule returned 8.2% against 10.9% a year, with a worst drawdown of 25.6% against 55.2% ([Empirica Lab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/faber-10-month-average)), and a daily 200-day rule over 2011–2026 gave up 3.7 points a year for 14 points less drawdown and did worse than buy-and-hold on the Nasdaq-100 ([The Long Trend](https://thelongtrend.substack.com/p/i-tried-to-debunk-the-200-day-moving)). Antonacci's dual momentum shows where the value lies: since 2014 GEM earned 8.4% (Sharpe 0.70), against 13.6% (0.94) for SPY and 9.1% (0.95) for 60/40 ([Quant for Free](https://quant4free.com/analysis/dual-momentum/)), and one decomposition finds the absolute-momentum gate did the work while the relative-momentum leg cost 1.4–3.2 points a year ([QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/research/antonacci-s-dual-momentum-taken-apart-gate-relative-leg-and-blends-across-four-l)). The key design lesson came in 2022, when published TAA strategies suffered an aggregate **−12.1% drawdown, worse than in 2007–08**, because long Treasuries failed as the defensive asset; strategies that required defensive assets to beat cash held their drawdowns to −8.0%, against −17.3% for rate-sensitive ones ([Allocate Smartly](https://allocatesmartly.com/tactical-asset-allocation-performance-during-the-2022-bear-market/)). Timing luck is large enough to swamp the edge, since shifting Faber's SPY rule by a single day moved its maximum drawdown anywhere between 19% and 30% ([Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2013/08/the-luck-of-the-rebalance-timing/)), so splitting rebalancing across several dates is mandatory rather than optional. Managed tactical funds have a poor record against 60/40 ([USM study](https://aquila.usm.edu/fac_pubs/20187); [Morningstar via Evidence Investor](https://evidenceinvestor.com/post/tactical-allocation-funds-have-been-stinkers-morningstar)), a reminder that the edge here is drawdown control, not alpha.

**3. A managed-futures sleeve is the one genuine diversifier available in UCITS form, with a long but uneven live record.** Time-series momentum across futures has a strong in-sample case ([Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, JFE 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250)). AQR's century backtest shows 11.2% a year net of 2/20 fees at a 0.77 Sharpe and positive returns in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns, and a 20% allocation lifted a 60/40 portfolio's Sharpe from 0.38 to 0.54 while cutting its maximum drawdown from −62.3% to −50.2% ([AQR, vendor](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf)). The live record is roughly half as strong. From 2000 to May 2025 the SG Trend index returned **4.9% a year at 13.4% volatility**, with a −0.10 correlation to global equities ([Meketa](https://meketa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/MEKETA_Trend-Following.pdf)); it gained a record 27.3% in 2022 ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/trend-followers-turn-leaders-ctas-deliver-record-returns-2022/)), made −4.16%, +2.42% and +2.39% over 2023–2025 ([Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-december-2025/)), and then produced the record loss and 2026 rebound shown earlier, leaving a compound return of 5.75% a year since 2000 at the end of September 2026 ([Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-september-2026/)). The academic case is contested, since Huang and co-authors find little asset-by-asset evidence of time-series predictability ([JFE 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:3:p:774-794)), and even Rob Carver, an ex-AHL practitioner, logged a −16.3% "worst ever year" in his futures account to April 2025 ([qoppac](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2025/04)). The vehicle is the iMGP DBi Managed Futures UCITS ETF, launched in March 2025 and now offering a EUR-hedged class, which replicates the pre-fee return of leading trend-following funds at a 0.75% TER ([HedgeNordic](https://hedgenordic.com/2025/03/europe-gets-its-managed-futures-ucits-etf-with-imgp-dbi-launch/); [RankiaPro](https://rankiapro.com/en/news/imgp-dbi-managed-futures-eur-hedged-etf-euronext-paris/)); its US twin DBMF returned +21.6% in 2022, −8.94% in 2023 and +13.84% in 2025 ([TotalRealReturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/DBMF)). The fund uses futures internally, so you must decide whether an unlevered holding of a levered fund respects your "no leverage" rule. Man's new trend-overlay UCITS ETF, at about 0.95%, bundles MSCI World equity with trend and is therefore not a pure diversifier ([RankiaPro](https://rankiapro.com/en/news/man-group-launches-its-first-ucits-etfs-in-europe-featuring-active-trend-following-and-infrastructure-strategies/); [QuotedData](https://quoteddata.com/2026/11/man-group-brings-trend-following-etf-to-london-as-ai-shares-tumble/)).

### Quality, value and minimum volatility are cheap tilts, not engines

**4. Quality is the best core tilt on long-only evidence, though its long-short premium is shakier.** Over 2015–2025 MSCI World Quality beat its parent by **2.08 points a year**, with a shallower maximum drawdown (−48.4% against −57.8%) and only 18–25% annual turnover ([MSCI World Quality](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-world-quality-index-usd-net.pdf)), and AQR's European quality factor kept earning 5.29% a year after 2015 ([AQR QMJ](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Quality-Minus-Junk-Factors-Monthly)). The US long-short version is weaker: QMJ fell from 4.15% a year over 1957–2014 to 2.61% after 2015, and Ken French's profitability factor, which earned 3.17% a year over 1963–2014, lost 10.85% in 2025 and another 12.6% in January–September 2026 ([Ken French](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)). That gap suggests much of the long-only gain came from overweighting mega-cap tech rather than from a durable quality premium, and Linnainmaa and Roberts find no reliable profitability premium before 1963 ([RFS 2018](https://finance.wharton.upenn.edu/~mrrobert/resources/Publications/anomaliesRFS2017.pdf)). Quality still ranks well because it is cheap (IS3Q or XDEQ at 0.25%) and low-turnover, so held in an accumulating ETF it creates almost no tax drag.

**5. Value belongs in the portfolio as momentum's partner, not as a bet on its rebound.** US HML, which earned 4.45% a year over 1963–2014, returned −0.69% a year over 2015–2026, including a **−51% drawdown from December 2016 to September 2020** ([Ken French](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)), and long-only MSCI World Value trailed its parent by 3.05 points a year over 2015–2025 ([MSCI World Value](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-world-value-index-usd-net.pdf)). Outside the US the premium survived, with ex-US HML at 5.04% a year (t 2.31) ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Betting-Against-Beta-Equity-Factors-Monthly)). The 2025–26 boom leaned heavily on memory and semiconductor stocks ([WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/gb/insights/blog/looking-back-at-equity-factors-in-q2-2026-with-wisdomtree)), and buying value now because of that rally is exactly the recency trap. Combining factors helps but guarantees nothing: the best net-of-cost strategy in Novy-Marx and Velikov combined value, momentum and profitability for 99 bp a month in-sample ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)), yet MSCI USA Diversified Multiple-Factor still lagged its parent by 2.23 points a year over 2015–2025 ([MSCI USA DMF](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-usa-diversified-multiple-factor-index-usd-net.pdf)). Amundi PEA MSCI USA Value Advanced (USVE, 0.50%) is a rare PEA-eligible factor fund, though it is small at €42m ([justETF](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-profile.html?isin=FR001400KH45)).

**6. Minimum volatility reduces risk and nothing else, and it is what the draft's defensive buckets already are.** Long-only MSCI World Min Vol lagged its parent by 3.28 points a year over 2015–2025, and its EUR version had a **lower 10-year Sharpe (0.74 against 0.90)** despite a shallower maximum drawdown (−39.9% against −53.4%) ([MSCI World Min Vol EUR](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-world-minimum-volatility-index-eur-net.pdf)). It earned its keep in 2022, when MSCI USA Min Vol lost 9.67% against 19.85% for the market ([MSCI USA Min Vol](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/255599/msci-usa-minimum-volatility-index-usd-net.pdf)). The academic low-risk premium is real on paper, with US betting-against-beta earning 10.4% a year (Sharpe 0.93) over 1957–2014 ([AQR BAB dataset, researcher calculation](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Betting-Against-Beta-Equity-Factors-Monthly)), but it is out of reach, because it relies on leverage and effectively equal-weights tiny stocks ([Novy-Marx & Velikov, JFE 2022](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:143:y:2022:i:1:p:80-106)), so long-only investors capture only its risk-reduction side through UCITS funds such as IQQ0, XDEB or WMMV ([justETF low-volatility list](https://www.justetf.com/en/how-to/invest-in-low-volatility-etfs.html)).

### Short-horizon and option strategies survive only as small overlays

**7. RSI(2) pullbacks still work after publication, but too rarely to matter.** The most careful dated replication finds that Connors' rule on SPY fell from a Sharpe of 0.97 before the 2008 book to **0.68 afterwards, below buy-and-hold's 0.87** ([Empirica Lab](https://empiricalab.substack.com/p/connors-rsi2-spy)). The rule is invested only about 11% of the time; it compounded at 5.0% against 10.8% over 1993–2026 and returned 8.8% against 22.3% a year over the last three years. It is robust on SPY, where all 15 neighbouring parameter sets were positive, but five other equity ETFs managed Sharpe ratios of only 0.38–0.53, and costs of 10 bp per side cut the return to 3.7%. Cesar Alvarez, formerly Connors' research director, reportedly calls the edge "still profitable but much more difficult" ([The Chartist forum](https://thechartist.com.au/forums/users/lmzirmdgmail-com/replies/page/6)). All of the evidence is practitioner backtesting, and none of it tests UCITS ETFs, whose European session closes hours before the US signal exists.

**8. All-time-high breakouts on US stocks have a positive, outlier-driven expectancy that small accounts struggle to harvest.** Concretum's survivorship-free study reports 15.19% a year gross and 6.18% alpha over 1991–2024, with under 7% of trades producing most of the profit, but it also calls the base strategy "difficult to implement for portfolios under $1 million" ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/does-trend-following-still-work-on-stocks/); [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/all-time-high-trend-following)). The 2005 original reported more than 18,000 trades with a 49.3% win rate and 0.5% round-trip costs and no significance tests ([Wilcox & Crittenden](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/trend.pdf)), and both studies come from firms that sell trading services. The behavioural profile, breakout-chasing with a sub-50% win rate and long dry spells, is uncomfortably close to the momentum day trading that already failed.

**9. Sector rotation, the draft's Setup A, has thin support after 2015.** Industry momentum "diminishes and becomes insignificant" once time-varying factor exposures are controlled ([vLex abstract](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/expected-versus-ex-post-855621482)), and a practitioner test on the nine sector SPDRs concluded that "momentum systems do not perform as well with ETFs" ([StockCharts, Arthur Hill](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2017-02-systemtrader---testing-a-trend-timing-system-and-momentum-rotation-system-for-the-nine-sector-spdrs/)). The familiar outperformance of about 4% a year comes from an in-sample 1928–2009 backtest ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/sector-momentum-rotational-system)), CXO flags data-snooping in the ETF versions ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=24699)), and momentum portfolios carry the largest rebalance timing luck of any style studied, about 4.4% a year ([Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/11/the-dumb-timing-luck-of-smart-beta/)). The UCITS toolkit is also incomplete: iShares covers 10 of the 11 S&P 500 sectors at 0.15%, Invesco covers all 11 synthetically at 0.14% but with some funds under €40m, and the PEA offers only a few Amundi sector funds ([justETF iShares list](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-provider/ishares-etfs.html); [justETF Invesco list](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-provider/invesco-etfs.html); [justETF Amundi list](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-provider/amundi-etfs.html)).

**10 and 11. Calendar effects and option selling are overlays or substitutes, not edges.** The classic turn-of-month window "has largely disappeared over the past decade" for US ETFs, leaving mostly a drawdown benefit ([QuantSeeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/turn-of-the-month-strategies-do-they)). The pre-FOMC drift, originally about 50 bp over 1994–2011, survived 2011–2018 only on press-conference days, at about 40 bp ([Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2018/11/the-pre-fomc-announcement-drift-more-recent-evidence.html)). The Halloween effect originally appeared in 36 of 37 countries ([Bouman & Jacobsen](https://www.tias.edu/docs/default-source/Kennisartikelen/ssrn-id76248.pdf?sfvrsn=2)), but the US effect turns insignificant once October 1987 and August 1998 are controlled for ([Econ Journal Watch](https://econjwatch.org/articles/stock-market-efficiency-withstands-another-challenge-solving-the-sell-in-may-buy-after-halloween-puzzle)). Option selling once matched the index, with Cboe's PutWrite returning 10.13% a year against 9.85% for the S&P 500 over 1986–2015 ([S&P Indexology](https://cloudfront.indexologyblog.com/?p=14955)), but it has lagged badly since. Over 2015–2025 the PutWrite index compounded at 7.8% and the BuyWrite index at 7.1%, against 13.5% for the S&P 500, all gross of costs, and in 2020 PutWrite made 2.1% while the index made 18.4% (researcher calculation from the [Cboe PUT](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/factsheet/CboeGlobalIndices_PUT-Index.pdf) and [Cboe BXM](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/factsheet/CboeGlobalIndices_BXM-Index.pdf) factsheets). US option-income ETFs are blocked by PRIIPs, and the draft forbids options anyway.

## The tax wrapper is worth more than most tilts

**For this investor, choosing the wrapper beats choosing a signal.** In a CTO at IBKR, gains and dividends pay the **31.4% PFU**, made up of 12.8% income tax plus social levies that rose to 18.6% from 2026 ([service-public.fr F21618](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F21618); [F2329](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2329)), while a PEA older than five years pays only the 18.6% levies on withdrawal, within a €150,000 contribution ceiling ([F2385](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2385)). At 10% a year pre-tax over ten years, the three cases compare as follows (researcher calculation from these rates):

| Case | Net annual return |
|---|---|
| CTO, all gains realised every year | 6.86% |
| CTO, all gains deferred to year ten | 7.67% |
| Mature PEA, same deferral | 8.66% |

That **1.8-point annual gap between a high-turnover CTO strategy and a buy-and-hold PEA** is larger than the realistic excess return of every candidate in this report, so the high-turnover candidates (RSI(2), sector rotation, calendar overlays, breakouts) start that far behind. Accumulating UCITS ETFs held in a CTO capture most of the deferral benefit, since the sources found describe no deemed-distribution tax on them ([justETF France](https://www.justetf.com/fr/academy/etf-et-fiscalite.html)), and that is the hidden reason factor exposure through an ETF beats a self-built factor portfolio at this scale.

**Split the accounts by job.** The PEA should hold the passive and trend-filtered equity sleeves, using PEA-eligible synthetic world, S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 trackers such as WPEA, DCAM, ESE, PSP5 and PUST, which must be synthetic to meet the 75% European-equity rule ([Finary](https://finary.com/en/blog/bourse/etf/etf-pea)); switches between equity and cash inside the plan are untaxed. No broker was confirmed to offer API trading on a PEA, but the ROADMAP already runs the core in advisor mode with a handful of manual orders a year, so the missing API costs little. The CTO at IBKR then holds everything else: bonds, gold, managed futures, Irish factor ETFs and anything that needs automation. That CTO carries its own admin. It needs a form 3916 every year, with a €1,500 fine per undeclared account per year ([Fiscaloo](https://www.fiscaloo.fr/1280-declaration-de-compte-a-letranger-comment-faire-et-sanctions-encourues/)), self-declared gains, and the monthly 12.8% prepayment form (2778-DIV-SD) for any distribution, which accumulating share classes avoid ([impots.gouv.fr](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2778-div-sd/2026/2778-div-sd_5389.pdf)). Dividends on US single stocks are withheld at the 15% treaty rate with a W-8BEN on file ([Rotek, secondary](https://rotek.fr/convention-fiscale-france-usa-dividendes-interets-plus-values/)). Purchases of the 121 French companies above €1bn also pay a 0.4% financial-transaction tax; ETFs are exempt, but the tax applies inside a PEA too ([Legifiscal](https://www.legifiscal.fr/placements/epargne/bourse-pea/taxe-transactions-financieres.html)). The French tax treatment of physical-gold ETCs was not found in any official source, so the draft's gold bucket carries unresolved tax risk.

**Costs favour monthly strategies, and ETFs below roughly €50k.** IBKR Ireland's tiered schedule charges 0.05% of order value with a €1.25 minimum and a €29 cap, and the fixed schedule has a €3 minimum ([IBKR Europe commissions](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/en/pricing/commissions-stocks-europe.php)), so a €2,000 order costs 6.3 bp tiered or 15 bp fixed. A €500 slice at the €1.25 minimum costs 25 bp a side, which means a self-built portfolio of 20–30 stocks only makes sense toward the upper end of the €10k–€200k range (researcher arithmetic). Other costs are small: US stocks cost USD 0.0035 a share tiered ([IBKR Americas commissions](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php)), FX conversion costs 0.2 bp with a USD 2 minimum ([IBKR FX](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/en/pricing/commissions-spot-currencies.php)), and realistic market data for Xetra ETFs runs €0–16 a month ([IBKR market data pricing](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/en/pricing/market-data-pricing.php)). A self-built momentum book turning over about 100% a year at 10–30 bp per round trip costs roughly 0.1–0.3% a year, about the same as an ETF's TER.

**The operating details push in the same direction.** A signal computed after the US close at 22:00 Paris time fills on Xetra or LSE the next morning, a gap that hurts the 1–5-day holds of RSI(2) far more than a monthly rebalance. Unattended operation is also fragile: IBC, the usual tool for running IB Gateway unattended, was archived on 1 September 2026, and IBKR still requires a weekly re-authentication ([IBC on GitHub](https://github.com/IbcAlpha/IBC); [IBKR docs](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/tws-api/doc/tws-settings/daily-weekly-reauthentication.md)), and a monthly strategy tolerates a missed day where a daily one does not. Data is cheap for the top candidates. Ken French and AQR series are free for the factor work, EODHD covers UCITS end-of-day prices for USD 19.99 a month ([EODHD](https://eodhd.com/pricing)), and survivorship-free US stock data, needed only for the single-stock candidates, costs USD 630 a year at Norgate ([Norgate](https://norgatedata.com/stockmarketpackages.php)). Stooq has required a CAPTCHA-obtained key since March 2026, which rules it out for production ([pandas-datareader issue](https://github.com/pydata/pandas-datareader/issues/1012)).

## Test three candidates first and shrink the swing engine

**Test 1 is a trend-filtered global core run against the draft core.** It uses four to six UCITS sleeves: world equity (WPEA or DCAM in the PEA, IWDA in the CTO), optional emerging markets (EIMI), US Treasuries 7–10y in unhedged (IBTM) and EUR-hedged (CEMF or 7USH) versions, a physical gold ETC, and EUR cash via XEON ([justETF IWDA vs EIMI](https://www.justetf.com/en-nl/asset-comparisons/etf-comparisons/IE00B4L5Y983-ishares-core-msci-world-acc-vs-IE00BKM4GZ66-ishares-core-msci-em-imi-acc); [justETF Treasury 7–10y](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-profile.html?isin=IE00B1FZS798); [justETF XEON](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-profile.html?isin=LU0290358497)). Each sleeve follows a monthly 10-month-average or 12-month-excess-return rule, defensive sleeves are held only while they beat cash, and rebalancing is split across two to four dates. The benchmarks are an after-tax PEA world tracker, a EUR 60/40 portfolio and STRATEGY.md v0.1. Only three pairs of variants are pre-registered (with or without the crash ladder, hedged or unhedged Treasuries, and a 10-month or 12-month signal), about eight trials and inside the budget, and index proxies extend the test back before 2000.

**Test 2 runs a momentum tilt head-to-head against Setup A under identical guardrails.** It compares four arms: buy-and-hold of an MSCI World momentum UCITS ETF, using the MSCI index history (back-tested before its 2013 launch); the draft's sector rotation; a GEM-style regional model combining absolute and relative momentum on UCITS regional ETFs; and later, only above roughly €100k, a self-built European large-cap momentum book inside the PEA. That last arm is where the post-publication evidence is strongest, but point-in-time European stock data still has to be sourced. AQR's European momentum series provides the long-history cross-check, and any crash-control rule (such as Daniel–Moskowitz volatility scaling) enters as one pre-specified variant, never as a tuning knob.

**Test 3 sets a 10–20% managed-futures sleeve against the draft's cash, gold and ladder.** It uses SG Trend (net of fees, from 2000) and DBMF (from 2019) as proxies, converted to EUR, and judges them on 2008, 2020, 2022 and 2025 as well as on full-period Sharpe and drawdown.

**The draft already does much right.** It replaced four hindsight-picked stocks with an index proxy, runs every test at 2× costs, keeps an experiment log, locks its out-of-sample period, checks parameter plateaus, treats 2022 as a mandatory stress window, keeps the core in advisor mode and pre-commits to deleting Engine 2 if it fails. All of that matches the protocols of Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz and of Bailey and co-authors. The problems lie in what the rules bet on, and in how much the gates can actually prove.

The verdicts in this table draw on the sources cited earlier, plus Allocate Smartly on late execution ([Allocate Smartly](https://allocatesmartly.com/diving-deeper-does-the-day-of-the-month-matter/)) and the evidence on volatility management ([Cederburg et al., JFE 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117)).

**Engine 1 should become simpler, not cleverer.** Its stock and sector picks express opinions (defensives, Nasdaq leadership, Berkshire) that the factor evidence either contradicts or cannot support, and robust building blocks should replace them: world equity, a quality or momentum tilt, bonds held only on positive momentum, gold, and a trend sleeve. The crash ladder deserves one honest test. Its payoff depends on V-shaped rebounds, the very regime (2020, April 2025) in which trend filters whipsaw, which makes it a plausible complement to an exit filter, but four episodes can never validate it statistically.

**Engine 2 should become a monthly momentum engine rather than a swing engine.** The short-horizon edges are the most decayed, the most heavily taxed in a CTO, the most exposed to the gap between the US-close signal and the European fill, and the most fragile when IB Gateway needs a human every week. G5's benchmark of the S&P 500 in euros before tax is too weak on its own, and it should be joined by an after-tax PEA world tracker, because that is the investor's real alternative. G9 as written measures noise; it should judge whether live results stay inside the backtest's range and whether the rules were followed, which is what the ROADMAP's own G8 already asks. A vendor estimate that rules exiting after drawdowns cost trend investors about 2.2 points a year is a reminder that quitting on short windows carries its own cost ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/is-this-time-different)).

## Conclusion

For this investor, the useful question is not "which strategy is most profitable?" but "which durable premia can I hold, cheaply and tax-efficiently, through their worst decade?" Framed that way, the biggest levers are not signals at all. At 10% returns the wrapper is worth about 1.8 points a year, the behaviour gap costs about 1.2 points, and cost discipline separates net from gross; each is worth as much as or more than the best factor tilt's realistic 0–2 points. That inverts the Ross-era instinct that more activity means more edge, because across every retail dataset reviewed here, activity is the most reliable predictor of losses. A good bot here earns its keep by enforcing patience, rebalancing, split rebalancing dates, tax-lot accounting and kill switches, not by finding trades.

The practical consequence is that the project's most likely successful outcome looks underwhelming on paper: a mostly passive world-equity core in a PEA, a monthly trend filter and momentum tilt in the CTO, and a managed-futures sleeve, with Engine 2 shrunk or deleted after an honest test. Success should therefore be defined in advance as following the rules through a trend sleeve's −18% year, a momentum tilt's −11% month and a filter's whipsaw while keeping drawdowns inside the pain limits. Beating the S&P 500 over the next twelve months would be noise, and that should be written down before the first euro is committed.
