# Slow trend survives where crypto hype fails

Across retail forums, Reddit threads as reported by the press, Discord and Telegram groups as studied by researchers, open-source bot communities, retail bot and copy-trading platforms, yield products and the academic record, **only one crypto strategy family has credible evidence of adding value after costs while fitting a spot-only, unleveraged, daily-or-slower French account: a long-or-flat time-series trend rule on bitcoin, built as an ensemble of 1–12-month lookbacks and sized by a volatility target capped at 100% invested.** Its value since 2020 has been risk control, not extra return. In the 2021–2023 window, which predates PLAN.md's hold-out, the team's replication shows the volatility-scaled ensemble at a Sharpe ratio of 0.79 with a −21.5% maximum drawdown, against 0.52 and −76.7% for buy-and-hold; the 1.5–2.6 Sharpe ratios in published papers come from in-sample bull eras. Almost everything communities and platforms advertise as profitable fails out of sample, after costs, or on the followers' side of the ledger: grid and martingale bots, copy-trading leaderboards, pump and signal groups, memecoins, RSI/Bollinger mean reversion, on-chain top-calling, altseason rotation and machine-learning prediction. Carry, staking and DeFi yields have shrunk to roughly 2–6% gross, no better than money-market funds, and mostly need derivatives or custody outside MiCA. Costs and tax shape the design. At Kraken Pro's entry tier (0.40% maker, 0.80% taker since July 2026), a rule that turns over 12 times a year pays 4.8–9.6% a year in fees, so only banded, volatility-scaled rules with turnover near 5 a year make sense. Bitcoin-to-EURC swaps stay tax-deferred under current law, although the 2027 budget has twice tried to tax them; losses cannot be carried forward; and frequent, sophisticated trading risks being taxed as professional income (BNC). The recommended design has four parts: the trend ensemble as the signal, the volatility target as sizing, monthly contributions invested at the current target weight, and exits swapped directly into EURC. Measured against PLAN.md, the evidence confirms the core and the choice of a regulated exchange, demotes C1 to a benchmark, makes a bitcoin-only, volatility-targeted version of C2 the primary candidate, turns ETH into a robustness check, drops C4 and raises the cost model. It also exposes a validation problem: the research already evaluated C1–C3 over January 2024 – November 2026, which covers essentially all of the hold-out, so forward paper trading, counted against the trial budget, has to become the real out-of-sample test.

## Sixteen strategy families, and only one survives costs

The five research workstreams behind this report covered retail forums (BitcoinTalk, Elite Trader, Hacker News, TradingView, DEV and Medium), Reddit only as it appears in press reports, Discord and Telegram only through published research, the open-source bot ecosystem (Freqtrade, Hummingbot, Jesse, OctoBot), retail bot and copy-trading platforms, yield and DeFi products, and the academic and institutional record to November 2026 ([research notes](../research_notes/Crypto%20strategy%20community%20scan/)). One pattern runs through all of them: **the louder a venue's profit claims, the weaker its evidence.** Leaderboards, bot announcement threads, "grid profit" counters and TradingView strategy-tester statistics are the most promotional and the least audited, while peer-reviewed papers, Hacker News threads and the rare blog that uses out-of-sample methods are the most sober ([forums note](../research_notes/Crypto%20strategy%20community%20scan/forums_and_communities.md)). Population data set the base rate. The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) estimates that **73–81% of retail crypto-app users lost money on their first bitcoin purchase** over 2015–22 ([BIS WP 1049](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1049.htm)), and eToro traders who buy dips in stocks chase recent gains in crypto ([NBER w31317](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31317)).

### Trend rules are where forums and journals finally agree

**Time-series trend is the one family where the academic record and the sober end of the community point the same way.** Liu and Tsyvinski find "a strong time-series momentum effect" in bitcoin up to May 2018, concentrated at horizons of one to four weeks ([NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)). Detzel et al. show that the ratio of price to its moving average forecasts daily bitcoin returns in and out of sample, although their 2010–2017 Sharpe ratios of 2.2–2.6 are before costs ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:finmgt:v:50:y:2021:i:1:p:107-137); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/currency-trading/timing-bitcoin-with-smas/)). Deprez and Frömmel tested 75,360 rules, corrected for multiple testing after transaction costs, and found that the selected rules beat buy-and-hold out of sample, "especially risk–return wise" ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:reveco:v:93:y:2024:i:pb:p:858-874)).

Practitioner studies agree on the direction but inflate the size. Le and Ruthbah's 20-day rule earns a Sharpe of 1.25 net of 0.5% trading costs up to January 2023 ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/applying-simple-trend-following-rules-to-cryptocurrencies)). Concretum's ensemble reports about 18% a year net and a Sharpe above 1.5 up to March 2025, but with leverage up to 200% and entirely in-sample ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/crypto-asset-trend-following-strategies/)). The one genuine post-publication test, Quantpedia's re-run of its 2022 rules on February 2022 – August 2024, found that the rule buying new highs held up while the rule buying new lows decayed ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)). The live record is humbling: Bitwise's trend-rotation ETF returned **+42.5% over the three years to March 2026, while bitcoin rose 139.6%** ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/symbol/BITC/momentum/performance); [Bitstamp OHLC API](https://www.bitstamp.net/api/v2/ohlc/btcusd/?step=86400&limit=1000)).

Community voices land in the same place with less rigour. A TradingView author concedes that a 50/200-day crossover "underperforms buy&hold, but with much less risk" ([TradingView](https://kr.tradingview.com/chart/BTCUSD/b1ikY0qA-BTC-Simple-strategy-for-hodlers)). Quantpedia's multi-timeframe MACD filter cut bitcoin's drawdown from nearly −80% to −12.4% but earned 6.6% a year against "over 60%" for holding, with no costs modelled ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-to-design-a-simple-multi-timeframe-trend-strategy-on-bitcoin/)). A vendor that graded 13 classic rules out of sample net of fees found the trend rules barely positive (+0.7 bp per trade for the 50/200 crossover, +10.7 bp for an ADX rule), with none passing all five of its tests ([DEV/Tessen](https://dev.to/tessen/we-graded-13-famous-trading-strategies-on-6-years-of-data-all-13-failed-26o4)).

The team's own replication puts numbers on this. It uses Bitstamp daily closes, rules fixed in advance, no look-ahead, and a charge of 0.25% per unit of turnover ([replication notes](../research_notes/Crypto%20strategy%20community%20scan/academic_and_institutional_evidence.md); [Bitstamp OHLC API](https://www.bitstamp.net/api/v2/ohlc/btcusd/?step=86400&limit=1000)). The table shows only the two windows that end before PLAN.md's hold-out.

| BTC/USD rule, net of 0.25% per trade | 2015–2020: CAGR / Sharpe / max drawdown | 2021–2023: CAGR / Sharpe / max drawdown |
|---|---|---|
| Buy and hold | 111.6% / 1.38 / −83.4% | 13.3% / 0.52 / −76.7% |
| Price above 200-day average (C1) | 111.5% / 1.55 / −67.7% | 13.8% / 0.51 / −65.0% |
| 50/200-day crossover | 99.2% / 1.41 / −71.6% | 13.6% / 0.50 / −57.0% |
| Price above 50-day average | 108.0% / 1.62 / −62.3% | 33.9% / 0.88 / −60.2% |
| Price above 20-day average | 102.6% / 1.63 / −51.7% | −6.5% / 0.06 / −70.8% |
| Momentum ensemble: signs of 1/3/6/12-month returns (C2) | 103.5% / 1.54 / −67.0% | 23.2% / 0.71 / −48.2% |
| Ensemble × 25% volatility target | 41.8% / 1.65 / **−30.7%** | 13.0% / **0.79** / **−21.5%** |
| 200-day average × 25% volatility target | 50.5% / 1.80 / −23.9% | 13.8% / 0.75 / −28.7% |
| Volatility target alone | not reported | 17.5% / 0.73 / −41.5% |

Three findings matter for design. First, **the ensemble's Sharpe advantage over buy-and-hold did not decay**: it was +0.16 in 2015–2020 and +0.19 in 2021–2023, while the single 200-day rule's advantage fell to about zero because the rule switched 19 times in 2021 and lost 23.4% while bitcoin gained 59%. Second, **short lookbacks die on costs**: the 20-day rule went from the best 2015–2020 Sharpe to 0.06 in 2021–2023, and the 50-day rule's strong 2021–2023 figure is one of about ten variants tried, so picking it now would be data-mining. Third, **only the volatility-scaled versions kept drawdowns near or below about −31%**. Trend alone still lost 48–68%, because crashes start before a slow average is crossed, and volatility targeting without a trend filter raised the 2021–2023 Sharpe but still drew down 41.5%. Man Group's +0.40 Sharpe from a 30% volatility target depends on leveraging up in calm periods, which a 100% cap forbids ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/crypto-too-hot-to-handle)).

**DCA is the community's most defensible habit, but it is a buying schedule, not an edge.** The most-cited community backtest, an r/CryptoCurrency series republished by CoinStats, modelled $500 a month with 0.3% fees. Plain DCA into bitcoin from January 2018 doubled the money invested, dip-buying rules added a little and RSI timing did worse, while a start at the May 2021 top was **down $3,400 on $9,500 invested**; the author concluded that "most strategies sold online rely on cherrypicked data" ([CoinStats](https://coinstats.app/news/ussxkHfBHp_I-do-backtesting-for-DCAbased-investment-strategies-community-update-DCA-or-buy-the-dip)). Tilted variants claim more. An r/CryptoCurrency post reported by Cointelegraph found that DCA tiered by the Fear & Greed index returned 140.1% against 124.8% for plain weekly DCA over 2018–2023; its headline 332–385% figures came from a commercial risk model and from the Pi Cycle indicator, which later failed at the 2025 top, and fees were excluded and the results not cross-checked ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-dca-strategy-extreme-greed-most-profitable)). Bitcoin Magazine's Fear & Greed rule beat holding by 1,145% to 1,046% before fees, which its own analyst called "not monumental" ([Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/markets/how-a-bitcoin-fear-and-greed-index-trading-strategy-beats-buy-and-hold-investing)). A "multiplier DCA" bot posted on Show HN claimed about 30% more bitcoin in a one-year static backtest ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45221852)), and a DEV author running a similar rule nearly ran out of cash early in a bear market ([DEV](https://dev.to/dcabot/multiplier-dca-almost-blew-up-my-budget-heres-the-fix-that-saved-it-3imi)). Academic support for sentiment exists but is short-dated: the Fear & Greed index predicts returns at horizons of one day to one week ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v58y2023ipas154461232300778x.html)), too fast for a fee-constrained daily bot.

**Most other timing ideas have decayed, reversed or never worked net of costs.** In the vendor's out-of-sample grading, every mean-reversion rule lost money after fees despite high win rates: Connors RSI-2 lost 3.7 bp per trade over 12,141 trades, RSI reversion 19.8 bp and a "quiet dip buyer" 28.3 bp ([DEV/Tessen](https://dev.to/tessen/we-graded-13-famous-trading-strategies-on-6-years-of-data-all-13-failed-26o4)). Caporale and Plastun's bitcoin Monday effect produced trading profits mostly "not significantly different from the random ones" ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_6716.html)), and in the replication the turn-of-month premium fell from +62.6 to +26.2 bp a day between 2015–2020 and 2021–2023 before reversing sign† ([Bitstamp OHLC API](https://www.bitstamp.net/api/v2/ohlc/btcusd/?step=86400&limit=1000)). On-chain cycle tools fared worse. Pi Cycle, MVRV, Mayer and Puell all stayed silent at the October 2025 top, with MVRV's cycle peaks falling from 5.88 to 2.74 ([Quantpedia on Molnar](https://quantpedia.com/bitcoin-runs-on-a-clock-why-every-price-indicator-dies-and-the-halving-clock-doesnt/)); Look Into Bitcoin conceded that its top indicators were never reached and proposed refits while selling subscriptions ([Look Into Bitcoin](https://www.lookintobitcoin.com/bitcoin-research/are-bitcoins-top-calling-metrics-broken/)); and a 2026 thesis found that only **4 of 67** crypto predictors that were significant in-sample beat a random walk out of sample ([UDP thesis](https://doctorados.udp.cl/cms/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Tesis_Doctoral_Aliro_Sanhueza.pdf)). The halving clock rests on three tops, and its author calls his own p-values unreliable (same Quantpedia source).

Cross-sectional momentum is a genuine in-sample factor, worth 2.5–4.1% a week long-short at 1–4-week horizons and concentrated in large coins ([Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/LiuTsyvinskiWu2019%20COMMON%20RISK%20FACTORS.pdf)). But it earns its alpha "largely from short positions" and "incurs substantial trading costs" ([IRFA 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924001509)), and a November 2026 code audit cut the Sharpe of the crypto trend factor published in the Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis from **2.08 to 0.01** ([arXiv 2611.01951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01951)). Its retail version, altseason rotation, failed outright: altcoin market value excluding stablecoins never regained its November 2021 peak even while bitcoin sat 84% above its previous high ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/how-retail-altcoin-traders-lost-800-billion-betting-against-bitcoin/)), and the median 2025 altcoin rally lasted about 20 days ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/the-death-of-the-altseason-why-the-2025-cycle-never-happened/?rand=52384)). Machine learning did no better. The newest live benchmark of ML and LLM traders, run on OKX, found 23 of 24 methods profitable in a 2025 backtest, none beating buy-and-hold live in August–September 2026, and execution slippage **six times** the paper-trading level ([arXiv 2609.34510](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510)).

### Bots, leaderboards and chat groups count the leaders, not the followers

**Retail bot and copy-trading platforms publish the leaders' numbers and none of the followers'.** No exchange or bot platform found publishes the share of grid or DCA bots that end in profit ([GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/trading-bots/kucoin-trading-bot-review/); [Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/en-BH/square/hashtag/gridbot)), and the "grid profit" shown in apps leaves out the loss in value on the coins the grid accumulates: one Binance Square user posted a SUI grid showing **+197.52 USDT of grid profit and a total return of −33.31%**. Theory says a static grid's expected return is "essentially zero" under simple assumptions ([arXiv 2506.11921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)), and a 58-month test that restarted a bitcoin grid every month from January 2022 lost $2,283 while holding gained $11,779 ([DEV](https://dev.to/walid_koussa_0941941b1fdb/grid-trading-bot-backtest-a-fresh-bitcoin-grid-every-month-for-58-months-3n7a)). Martingale "DCA" bots (OKX labels its product "Spot DCA (Martingale)") buy more as the price falls, with ever-larger safety orders; they win small and often, lose big when every safety order fills, and no dataset shows them ending positive ([OKX Help](https://www.okx.com/help/iii-spot-dca-martingale); [Bybit Learn](https://www.bybit.com/en/learn/strategies/martingale-strategy-risks-crypto)).

The open-source world is more honest but no more useful. About 60% of the 67 strategies in Freqtrade's community repository run on 15-minute candles or faster and only two on 12-hour or slower bars, all with parameters tuned by the hyperopt optimiser, and Freqtrade's own documentation says backtests "will always be biased" and that "only forward-testing (dry-mode) can really confirm a strategy" ([freqtrade-strategies](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade-strategies); [Freqtrade docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)). The flagship community strategy, NostalgiaForInfinity, needs 5-minute bars, 40–80 pairs and 6–12 trades open at once, and publishes only backtests, in commit comments ([NostalgiaForInfinity](https://github.com/iterativv/NostalgiaForInfinity)). Hummingbot's market-making "miners" shared $376k of rewards on $837m of filled volume, about 4.5 bp, or roughly one taker fee ([Hummingbot](https://hummingbot.org/blog/hummingbot-miner-year-1-recap/)).

The best copy-trading dataset, a 90-day snapshot of 1,486 leaders and 100,236 copier outcomes on Binance, Bybit and MEXC published by a firm that does not sell copy trading, found **97.04% of leaders profitable on their own books but only 43.61% profitable for their followers**, and 48.48% of copier outcomes positive ([YieldFund](https://www.yieldfund.com/news/is-copy-trading-profitable-a-90-day-multi-exchange-study)). Carnegie Mellon researchers found leaderboard rankings "poorly correlated with future returns", a median portfolio lifetime of 2.9 days and leaders who quietly close their losing portfolios ([Kawai et al., CHI 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf)). An experiment published in Management Science shows that being able to copy others raises risk-taking ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:66:y:12:i:2020:p:5608-5622)). On Hyperliquid's public leaderboard, 69% of lead traders who blew up gave no gradual warning in their behaviour, and the best early-warning detector scored at chance, an AUC of 0.49 ([arXiv 2611.27988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27988)). Copy products also run mostly on leveraged futures, which the no-leverage rule forbids.

**Private chat communities fare worst in the published research.** Hamrick et al. counted 952 Discord and 2,469 Telegram pumps in the first half of 2018, with median price rises of 3.5–5.1% that measure insiders' maximum possible profit rather than members' returns, and profitability falling 50–60% within six months ([UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10133232/1/SSRN-id3303365%20%281%29.pdf)). Dhawan and Putniņš call pump schemes "a negative-sum game where most participants, except manipulators, incur losses" ([Review of Finance](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:27:y:2023:i:3:p:935-975.)), and Li, Shin and Wang find that a typical pump lasts only minutes ([JFQA](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/24639_Cryptocurrency-Pump-and-Dump.pdf)). Influencer buy calls on more than 1,600 assets returned +1.83% on the first day and **−18.90% after 90 days** ([Merkley et al., RAST 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v29y2024i3d10.1007_s11142-024-09838-4.html)). No peer-reviewed study shows paid signal groups making money for their members. The same pattern moved on-chain: only about 0.4% of pump.fun wallets realised more than $10,000 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/300403/pump-fun-traders-millionaires?amp=1)), about 98.6% of its tokens showed rug-pull or pump-and-dump traits ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)), and Keyrock rated only 11% of 62 airdrops in 2024 a success ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/airdrops-in-the-barren-desert/)). Even honest, experienced posters say live results reach one quarter to three quarters of the backtest, as one equities quant put it on Hacker News ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39833025)).

### Yield and carry fell to money-market rates after the ETFs

**Crypto carry was a real premium, and the spot-ETF era competed it away.** The BIS finds that futures carry averaged more than 10% a year and reached 60%, driven by retail demand for leverage, with high carry predicting crashes and margin calls hitting arbitrageurs during drawdowns ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)). After the spot ETFs arrived, basis traders on the CME absorbed most of it. Deribit bitcoin perpetual funding averaged **10.3% in 2024, 5.4% in 2025 and 2.3% so far in 2026**, gross ([Deribit API](https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/public/get_funding_rate_history?instrument_name=BTC-PERPETUAL)); the CME basis sat near 4.7% in January 2026 against about 17% a year earlier ([Advisor Perspectives/Bloomberg](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2026/01/22/wall-street-pulls-back-bitcoin-trade)); and the most rigorous community study found cash-and-carry netting about 5.6% a year over January 2023 – May 2026 and turning negative in 2026, calling its own headline Sharpe of 8.5 "a Sharpe artifact" ([Alpha Research](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper2.html)). Packaged carry followed the same arc. Ethena's sUSDe realised 17.5% in 2024, 6.5% in 2025 and 4.3% in 2026, traded at $0.65 on Binance on 10 October 2025, and left the EU after the German regulator BaFin ordered a wind-down ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/yields/pool/66985a81-9c51-46ca-9977-42b4fe7bc6df); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/347532/regulator-halts-ethenas-usde-stablecoin-offering-in-germany-over-concerns)). Both need a derivatives leg, and ESMA said in February 2026 that perpetual futures sold to EU retail investors are "almost certainly" CFDs, contracts for difference ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/regulation/esma-tells-firms-perpetual-futures-fall-under-eu-cfd-rules/)).

The rest of the yield menu pays less, and carries more risk, than its marketing suggests. Lido's realised ETH staking yield fell every year, from 4.53% in 2022 to **2.41% in 2026** ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/yields/pool/747c1d2a-c668-4682-b9f9-296708a3dd90)), and its worst moments were liquidity events: staked ETH traded 5–6% below ETH in June 2022 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/06/17/steth-token-trading-pool-crypto-investors)), and the queue to unstake ETH reached 43–46 days in September 2025 ([Figment](https://www.figment.io/insights/ethereums-exit-queue-hits-record-high-what-stakers-need-to-know/)). Aave's USDC lending rate peaked at 7.17% in 2024 and averaged 3.45% in 2026, outside MiCA ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/yields/pool/aa70268e-4b52-42bf-a116-608b370f9501)). Passive Uniswap v3 liquidity providers suffered $260.1M of impermanent loss for $199.3M of fees ([arXiv 2111.09192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09192v1)), and in the flagship WETH-USDC pool fees covered only about 80% of arbitrage losses over 2022–23 ([arXiv 2404.05803](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05803v2)). Systematic bitcoin covered calls made −0.1% a year unfiltered over October 2021 – April 2026 and left the drawdown at −73.5%, against −76.7% for holding bitcoin ([Anchorage](https://www.anchorage.com/research/synthetic-yield-on-bitcoin-implementation-discipline-and-performance-boundaries-of-systematic-covered-call-writing)). The regulated benchmark is a tokenised euro money-market fund: Spiko's AMF-approved EUTBL paid **2.35–2.43% net** in November 2026 ([Spiko data](https://data.spiko.io/eutbl/)). Against that hurdle, no crypto yield product pays enough extra to make up for its record of large losses: FTX, Celsius, the stETH discount, USDe's 10 October print, the Ribbon vault exploit.

## A 0.40% entry fee and French tax shrink the menu further

### Turnover decides survival at Kraken's new entry tier

Since 9 July 2026, Kraken Pro charges **0.40% maker and 0.80% taker** below $2,500 of 30-day volume, 0.30%/0.60% from $2,500, and 0.22%/0.38% only from $10,000 of monthly volume or $20,000 held on the platform ([Kraken blog](https://blog.kraken.com/product/pro/new-kraken-pro-fee-tiers)). An account of a few thousand euros that trades a few times a year will sit in tier 1 most months. Coinbase reports 0.25%/0.50% for EU users from 16 September 2026, but accounts below $10K have been reported on "Intro" tiers at 0.40%/0.60% and even 0.60%/1.20%, and Coinbase describes its fees as "subject to experimentation" ([CryptoDaily](https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/09/coinbase-advanced-fees-usdc-vip-tiers); [BrokerChooser](https://brokerchooser.com/broker-reviews/coinbase-review/coinbase-fees); [Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/cryptocurrency/coinbase-faces-criticism-for-fee-structure-again-nansen-ceo-calls-it-insane/cmUY2VlR49B)). Bitstamp's entry rate is reported at 0.30%/0.40% but was not confirmed on its own fee page ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/exchange/bitstamp-eu)). App channels cost more: Kraken charges 1% on recurring buys in its app ([Kraken fee schedule](https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule)). Spreads, by contrast, are about 3 bp on Kraken ([CryptoSlate/Kaiko](https://cryptoslate.com/mica-euro-stablecoins-doubled-did-btc-eth-liquidity-in-europe-follow/)), so **at small size the fee, not the spread, is the cost.**

| Rule | Turnover per year | Annual fee drag at 0.40% maker | At 0.80% taker |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20-day average | 38.3 | 15.3% | 30.6% |
| 50-day average | 18.3 | 7.3% | 14.6% |
| Momentum ensemble, unsized (C2) | 12.0 | 4.8% | 9.6% |
| 200-day average (C1) | 6.2 | 2.5% | 5.0% |
| Ensemble × 25% volatility target | 5.0 | 2.0% | 4.0% |
| 200-day average × 25% volatility target | 3.6 | 1.4% | 2.9% |
| 50/200-day crossover | 1.8 | 0.7% | 1.4% |
| Volatility target alone, 10-point band | 1.2 | 0.5% | 1.0% |

Turnover is the sum of absolute position changes per year (a full round trip counts as 2.0), measured over 2015 – November 2026 in the replication ([Bitstamp OHLC API](https://www.bitstamp.net/api/v2/ohlc/btcusd/?step=86400&limit=1000)); fee drag is turnover multiplied by the rate (my arithmetic), before spread. Because the replication's net figures assumed 0.25%, tier-1 maker execution costs a further 0.15 points per unit of turnover, which is 1.8 points a year more for the unsized ensemble and 0.75 for the volatility-scaled one.

Two design rules follow. **A turnover budget of about 5 a year keeps fees near 2%** at maker rates, a budget only the banded, volatility-scaled rules meet; PLAN.md's estimate that ten round trips cost 8–10% a year is right at maker rates and doubles to 16% if orders cross the spread. And **maker execution matters as much as the choice of signal**, although it opens a known gap between backtest and live trading: Freqtrade's backtester assumes every limit order fills within the candle ([Freqtrade docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)), and community testers warn that strategies earning under 0.5% per trade die on slippage and unfilled orders ([Backtesting Traps](https://brookmiles.github.io/freqtrade-stuff/2021/04/12/backtesting-traps)).

### MiCA leaves a handful of venues and makes withdrawal-proof keys standard

Since the French transition period ended on 1 July 2026, only MiCA-authorised providers may serve French residents ([ESMA statement](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/ESMA75-113276571-1710_Public_Statement_MiCA_transitional_period_ends.pdf); [AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/news-publications/news-releases/crypto-assets-end-pacte-law-and-european-mica-regulation-transitional-period-establishes-new-role)). ESMA's register, updated 18 November 2026, lists Kraken (Ireland), Coinbase and Bitstamp (Luxembourg), Bitpanda (Austria), Bitvavo (Netherlands), and Crypto.com and OKX (Malta) among the exchanges authorised to serve France, and **no Binance or Bitget entity** ([ESMA CASPS.csv](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-12/CASPS.csv)); Binance cancelled French spot orders from 1 July and force-closed French margin positions by 1 October 2026 ([Journal du Coin](https://journalducoin.com/economie/crypto-en-france-trois-mois-apres-la-fin-des-psan-ce-que-mica-a-change-pour-vous/)). USDT has gone from EU order books, with Kraken auto-converting remaining balances by 31 March 2025 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/kraken-usdt-delisting-europe-mica-stablecoin-compliance)), so backtests on offshore USDT pairs will not match the execution actually available. USDC and EURC are MiCA e-money tokens issued by Circle's Paris entity under the supervision of the ACPR, the French banking supervisor ([ESMA EMTWP.csv](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-12/EMTWP.csv)). EURC is small, though, with $0.50B outstanding ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/stablecoins)); regulated stablecoins can wobble, as USDC did when it traded near $0.877 in March 2023 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/usdc-depegs-as-circle-confirms-3-3b-stuck-with-silicon-valley-bank)); and MiCA bars providers from paying interest on e-money tokens, so the risk-off position earns nothing ([ESMA, MiCA Art. 50](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-50-prohibition-granting-interest)).

Automation is feasible and can be made reasonably safe. Kraken puts "Withdraw Funds" in a permission group of its own and recommends a separate key for each purpose, locked to the IP addresses the bot runs from ([Kraken API docs](https://docs.kraken.com/exchange/guides/rest/api-keys.md)); Coinbase's API keys take View, Trade or Transfer scopes plus an IP allowlist ([Coinbase CDP](https://docs.cloud.coinbase.com/advanced-trade/docs/auth)). The 3Commas affair shows why this matters and why it is not enough: leaked third-party API keys caused **$27.3M of verified losses across 86 victims** in 2022, and even keys that could only trade were used to dump victims' funds into illiquid pairs ([ForkLog/HAPI](https://forklog.com/en/news/api-key-leaks-and-exchange-inaction-a-hapi-analysis-of-the-3commas-incident); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/179237/ftx-api-keys-3commas-exploited)). Exchange failure is the risk that remains. FTX creditors received about 119% of the dollar value of their claims at the bankruptcy date, but because claims were fixed in dollars, bitcoin claims were worth about 20% of bitcoin's price when repayments began ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348774/ftx-major-repayments-to-begin-may-30-btc-claims-currently-worth-just-20-of-market-price)).

### French tax rewards slow switching inside the crypto book

Crypto gains are taxed at a **31.4% flat tax** (12.8% income tax plus 18.6% social levies after the 2026 rise in CSG), and under the tax authority's own guidance "les opérations d'échange sans soulte d'actifs numériques bénéficient d'un sursis d'imposition": crypto-to-crypto swaps without a cash adjustment are tax-deferred ([impots.gouv.fr](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/particulier/questions/comment-declarer-les-plus-ou-moins-values-sur-cessions-dactifs-numeriques)). Under current law, swaps into USDC or EURC count as such swaps ([Kohen Avocats](https://kohenavocats.fr/2026/10/18/imposition-crypto-stablecoin-assemblee-vote-taxation-conversions-2027/)). This is the structural edge of a trend bot on an exchange: switching between bitcoin and EURC triggers no tax, and tax falls due only when euros leave the crypto book. The 2027 budget tried to end it. Assembly amendment I-3898, adopted on 16 October 2026 by 38 votes to 37, would have taxed swaps into MiCA e-money tokens from 1 January 2027 and replaced the whole-portfolio gain formula with a per-asset calculation. It lapsed when the Assembly rejected Part I of the bill on 4 November, and the Senate rejected its twin, I-2421, on 24 November ([Assemblée nationale](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/amendements/3210A/AN/3898); [Kohen Avocats](https://kohenavocats.com/crypto-stablecoin-echange-imposable-vote-16-octobre-2026-texte-reel/); [Journal du Coin](https://journalducoin.com/analyses/crypto-le-senat-rejette-les-amendements-crypto-du-plf-2027/)). The bill is not final until about 17 December, and a one-vote margin says the idea will come back.

Three other rules shape the design. **Losses cannot be carried forward**; they only offset crypto gains in the same year (impots.gouv.fr, above), so cashing out should be planned by the owner, not left to the bot. **Every taxable sale uses the whole-portfolio formula**, which needs the total value of all crypto holdings at the moment of sale ([Notice 2086](https://static.impots.gouv.fr/tdir/static/2026-03-30T16:36:08Z/aide/notices/notices2024/2086/notice2086.pdf)), so the bot must log portfolio valuations rather than just profit per trade. And since 2023, gains from trading "dans des conditions analogues à celles qui caractérisent une activité professionnelle", in conditions similar to a professional activity, are taxable as non-commercial profits (BNC). BOFiP says this applies only in exceptional cases judged one by one ([BOFiP](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/bofip/13985-PGP.html/ACTU-2023-00099)), but impots.gouv.fr gives as its example investors doing "opérations nombreuses et sophistiquées" with professional tools, a description an API bot partly fits, which argues for few trades and long holding periods. Paperwork also scales with venues: each foreign exchange account needs a form 3916-bis every year, with a €750 fine per missing account ([BOFiP](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/bofip/11969-PGP.html)), and exchanges report transactions to tax authorities under DAC8 from 2026 ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/fra/legal-updates/loi-fraudes-plusieurs-mesures-concernent-les-crypto-actifs)). Staking rewards are taxed as BNC (impots.gouv.fr, above), so staking would bring a second tax regime for about 1.7% a year after tax.

Deferral is worth roughly one point a year. A strategy compounding 10% a year that realises its gains every year keeps 6.86% a year after the 31.4% tax, while deferring until a withdrawal after ten years is equivalent to about 7.67% a year (illustrative arithmetic). That matters because Route A, physically backed exchange-traded notes (ETNs) held in a securities account, has become cheaper. The 21Shares and Invesco bitcoin ETNs charge 0.10% a year and CoinShares and WisdomTree 0.15% ([justETF](https://www.justetf.com/fr-be/how-to/invest-in-bitcoin.html)), and IBKR commissions are quoted from 0.05% ([Finary](https://finary.com/fr/blog/crypto/investir/etf-crypto)). ETNs are taxed as securities, so each trend exit is a taxable sale ([Boursobank](https://www.boursobank.com/aide-en-ligne/bourse/etn-crypto-actifs/question/quelle-fiscalite-est-applicable-aux-etn-crypto-actifs-50316192)), but under the standard securities rule, which this research did not verify for ETNs, losses can be carried forward. At a turnover of 5, tier-1 maker fees cost about 2% a year on the exchange, against under 0.5% a year for ETNs if the 0.05% commission held at small order sizes (minimum commissions were not verified). The post-ETF weekend slowdown also shrinks the cost of weekday-only trading, with the weekend share of bitcoin volume down to about 13–16% in 2024 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2024-06-30-bitcoin-sees-all-time-low-weekend-trading-levels-following-etf-approvals-kaiko-302705)). **The fee gap that once favoured the exchange now runs the other way at small size.** Route B keeps its tax deferral and 24/7 execution, but the choice is now close enough that it has to be computed rather than assumed, and if e-money-token swaps become taxable, Route A probably wins.

**The top of the ranking rests on drawdown control, not on beating bitcoin.** The ensemble ranks above the single 200-day rule because averaging four lookbacks counts as one trial rather than four, and it kept its Sharpe advantage through 2021–2023 while the 200-day rule whipsawed. The volatility-scaled ensemble ranks above the unsized one on fit rather than return. PLAN.md's gate CG1 requires a maximum drawdown under half of buy-and-hold's, and in both windows before the hold-out only the volatility-scaled versions pass: −30.7% against −83.4% in 2015–2020, and −21.5% against −76.7% in 2021–2023. The unsized ensemble's −67% and −48% fail that test and also break PLAN.md's −30% pain limit. That the volatility-scaled 200-day rule scored almost as well (Sharpe 0.75, drawdown −28.7% in 2021–2023) is reassuring, because it suggests the volatility layer does most of the drawdown work and the exact signal matters less. DCA ranks fourth even though it is not an edge, because monthly contributions have to enter the book somehow and plain DCA is the honest benchmark for doing so; the 200-day rule ranks below it because, once the ensemble exists, it adds nothing but a comparison. Everything from rank 10 down is excluded on at least two of the four criteria, usually evidence and fit together.

## Four pieces combine into one trend-gated bitcoin accumulator

The recommended design combines the top four rows of the ranking into one bot, with each piece covering a weakness in another.

| Piece | Rule | Why it is there | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signal | Average of the signs of bitcoin's 1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month returns, giving 0/25/50/75/100% exposure | The ensemble's Sharpe advantage held at +0.16 to +0.19 before the hold-out; single lookbacks whipsaw | Lags rallies; −23% in 2021 for the 200-day rule while bitcoin gained 59% |
| Sizing | Multiply by min(1, 25% ÷ 60-day realised volatility); never above 100%; skip changes under 10 points | Drawdowns of −21.5% to −30.7% before the hold-out; turnover falls from 12 to 5 | Average exposure about half or less; lower CAGR |
| Contributions | Each monthly euro deposit joins the next rebalance; it buys bitcoin only up to the current target weight, and the rest waits in EUR | DCA is the community's robust habit; gating it with the trend avoids buying all the way through a 2022-type bear market | Cash earns nothing on the exchange |
| Risk-off leg | Exits swap bitcoin directly into EURC, never through EUR | Tax-deferred under current law; a MiCA e-money token | Issuer risk at Circle; zero yield; risk of a law change |
| Execution and safety | Post-only limit orders with a timed fallback to market orders; a trade-only, IP-locked key on a sub-account; alert and halt on any order the bot did not send; stop buying if EURC moves away from €1 | Maker orders halve fees; lessons from 3Commas and the USDC wobble of 2023 | Some fills missed |

**The pieces fit together.** Trend provides the exit that buy-and-hold lacks, which matters because retail crypto investors chase price rises (Kogan et al., above) and bought into the 2022 crashes while large holders sold ([BIS Bulletin 69](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm)); a rule that steps aside in downtrends removes that pattern by construction. The volatility target trims losses before the slow trend signal reacts, which keeps drawdowns inside the pain limit, and it cuts turnover by more than half, which matters twice over: once at 0.40% per trade, and again for the professional-income test, which looks at how often the account trades. Gating contributions by the trend turns DCA from a blind schedule into one that pauses in bear markets; the community backtest that started at the May 2021 top lost money precisely because it kept buying through 2022, a year in which the 200-day rule sat in cash throughout. And EURC makes the frequent exits invisible for tax, so the 31.4% falls only on the withdrawals the owner chooses to make, in the years the owner chooses.

**Four things are deliberately left out.** ETH stays out of the live book at the start: with a correlation of 0.78–0.90 in 2018–2023 ([Bitstamp OHLC API](https://www.bitstamp.net/api/v2/ohlc/ethusd/?step=86400&limit=1000)), an equal-risk BTC/ETH book is only about 3–6% less volatile than bitcoin alone (my arithmetic from √((1+ρ)/2)), and it would double the number of small orders at tier-1 fees, so ETH serves as a test that the rule generalises with the same parameters, plus one pre-registered variant. Staking stays out because unstaking queues of up to 46 days conflict with a rule that must exit within a day, and because it adds a second tax regime. Fear & Greed and on-chain tilts stay out because their evidence is in-sample and decaying, and each would spend a trial from a budget that is already overdrawn, as the final section explains. Altcoin rotation stays out because its evidence collapsed under audit.

**The realistic expectation is modest.** The academic workstream estimates a post-ETF Sharpe of about 0.5–0.8, CAGR of about 8–20% and maximum drawdowns of −18% to −35% net of retail costs, with long flat spells and large lags in bull years ([replication notes](../research_notes/Crypto%20strategy%20community%20scan/academic_and_institutional_evidence.md)); that estimate itself draws partly on hold-out data. Two practical consequences follow. First, the bot would start mostly in cash: bitcoin closed at $58,910 on 24 November 2026, below its 200-day average near $72,000 ([Bitstamp OHLC API](https://www.bitstamp.net/api/v2/ohlc/btcusd/?step=86400&limit=1000)), so a bot started now would hold mostly EURC, and its first real test would be a re-entry months away. Second, volatility scaling at 25% leaves the book about half invested on average (47% for the volatility target alone over 2015–2026), and MiCA forbids interest on the euro tokens that hold the rest. The PEA-versus-crypto split in PLAN.md §11 should therefore size the crypto book at about twice the average bitcoin exposure the owner wants, and accept that the cash half earns nothing.

## PLAN.md keeps its core but reshuffles its candidates

[PLAN.md](../../crypto/PLAN.md) asked the right questions and got most of the answers right. The evidence confirms its three main choices: bitcoin and ether rather than altcoins, trend with volatility targeting as the edge, and a MiCA exchange with EURC as the risk-off asset. It changes the candidate list, the cost model and the basis for choosing a venue, and it adds rules for contributions, execution and tax. Each change below is marked by whether it rests on evidence from before December 2024 ("clean") or uses the hold-out window. Only clean evidence should drive a change to pre-registered candidates.

Several of the most tempting conclusions from the research are **hold-out-informed** and should not drive the candidate list: the 50/200 crossover's collapse after the ETFs (−2.4% a year†), ETH's weak post-ETF record (−14% a year buy-and-hold†), the 200-day rule's 2025 whipsaw (17 switches†), and volatility targeting alone lowering the post-ETF Sharpe from 0.41 to 0.27†. Every change in the table can be justified without them: pre-2024 correlation alone limits ETH's value, the 2021 whipsaw alone demotes C1, and the 2015–2023 drawdown results alone make the volatility layer compulsory.

**The venue decision changes the most.** PLAN.md lists trading fees of "~0.1–0.4% per trade" for Route B and treats that route as cheaper as well as tax-efficient, which at tier 1 is no longer true, as the previous section showed. The choice among Kraken, Coinbase and Bitstamp should therefore turn on four things. The first is whether the exchange lists **direct BTC/EURC (and ETH/EURC) pairs** with acceptable depth, because selling bitcoin for euros and then buying EURC would be a taxable sale and defeat the purpose. The second is the fee actually charged at the account's size, as shown in the order preview. The third is withdrawal-free, IP-locked API keys, which are verified for Kraken and Coinbase but not for Bitstamp or Bitpanda. The fourth is how stablecoin pairs are priced: Kraken charges stablecoin and FX pairs on a separate 0.20% schedule and excludes them from volume tiers ([Kraken Pro fees](https://www.kraken.com/fr/pro/fees)), and whether a BTC/EURC trade counts as such a pair still has to be checked.

**The evidence also adds several things PLAN.md does not yet have.** Contributions need an explicit rule, under which deposits join the next rebalance at the target weight and otherwise wait in euros, and backtests should run the same contribution stream into the strategy and into plain monthly DCA, because DCA rather than lump-sum buy-and-hold is the realistic alternative for someone adding money every month. Results should be reported by window (2015–2020, 2021–2023), because whole-sample statistics are dominated by 2016–2017 and 2020. Execution needs a maker-first order routine with a timed fallback, a model of unfilled orders, and slippage measured against that model. The tax module must log total portfolio value at every euro withdrawal, respect the absence of loss carry-forward, and model a scenario in which e-money-token swaps are taxed. The risk module needs a halt on any order or trading pair the bot did not originate, since a trade-only key can still be abused. Every data series should come from EUR or USD pairs, never USDT. And because the 00:05 UTC decision falls in the thin Asian session, where maker orders may fill slowly, execution timing should be modelled rather than assumed equal to the close.

## The hold-out is already spent, so the future must be the test

**The academic notes include the researcher's own backtest over January 2024 – November 2026, which covers almost all of the December 2024 – November 2026 hold-out that PLAN.md reserved to be opened only once.** The overlap runs from 1 December 2024 to 24 November 2026: every month of the 24-month hold-out except its last six days, and about 70% of the replication's post-ETF window. In that window the replication evaluated the 200-day rule (C1), the momentum ensemble (C2) and the ensemble with a 25% volatility target, which is C3's bitcoin sleeve. It also evaluated the 50/200 crossover, the 50- and 20-day rules, the volatility-scaled 200-day rule and the volatility target alone; ETH versions of most of these, which cover C3's ether sleeve; and euro-priced versions, three cost levels, calendar-year results for 2024, 2025 and 2026, whipsaw counts, and weekday and turn-of-month tests ([replication notes](../research_notes/Crypto%20strategy%20community%20scan/academic_and_institutional_evidence.md)). Those post-ETF figures are informative, because they show the trend rules still controlled drawdowns after the ETFs. **But they contaminate the hold-out.** Gate CG2 can no longer be passed honestly by C1, C2, C3 or their variants, and any choice made in light of those figures carries hold-out information into the design. No earlier window can stand in for it either, since January–November 2024 was also seen and 2015–2023 is the in-sample era of the literature itself. **For these rules, the only data nobody has looked at is the future.**

Validation should proceed in five steps.

**First, record the contamination.** The replication's roughly eight bitcoin rule configurations go into `research/experiments.csv` as trials already spent in the trend family, with a note that the hold-out was exposed. Together with the configurations PLAN.md has not yet run, the honest count comes to about 13, against the evidence funnel's budget of about 10 per family ([STRATEGY.md](../../STRATEGY.md)). Because the family is over budget, the candidate list should be frozen now: the bitcoin-only volatility-scaled ensemble as primary, C1 as the benchmark, and only two variants that answer real design questions, namely BTC+ETH at equal risk (diversification) and weekly rebalancing (turnover and the professional-income test). The 100-day and 20%/30% volatility-target variants should be dropped rather than run, and deflated Sharpe ratios should use the honest count. The minimum-history rule of 2·ln(N)/Sharpe² years implies 9–11 years of data for 10–13 trials at a post-2020 Sharpe of about 0.7. Bitstamp's bitcoin/dollar series before the hold-out, about 14 years from 2011, can supply that if converted with ECB exchange rates; the euro series from April 2016, about 8.6 years, cannot.

**Second, run CG1 as planned on history up to November 2024,** with drawdown-halving as the primary test. It is the large, consistent effect in every window, whereas the Sharpe advantage over buy-and-hold (+0.16 to +0.19) is too small to prove statistically on crypto's short history.

**Third, turn CG2 into a reconciliation check.** The project's engine runs once over December 2024 – November 2026 and must reproduce the direction and rough size of the research replication. A mismatch reveals bugs, or differences in euro prices or fees. The check can fail a candidate but can no longer pass one.

**Fourth, make forward paper trading the true out-of-sample test.** Before the first paper-trading day, the rules, parameters, cost model and expected ranges are frozen in a dated git commit. Only the frozen primary candidate is paper-traded, alongside the C1 and plain-DCA benchmarks, and every configuration traded forward counts as a trial; any rule change restarts the clock and adds a trial. CG3 splits in two. The existing eight-week engineering gate stays as it is: signals match a backtest re-run 100%, slippage stays at or below 1.5× the model, and no unhandled errors occur. A performance record then runs for at least 12 months and through at least one change in exposure, judged against bands set in advance: the exposure path matches the re-run, realised costs stay within 1.5× the model, and the drawdown is less than half of bitcoin's in any bitcoin decline of 20% or more during the window.

**Fifth, allow small live capital (CG4) in parallel once the engineering gate passes,** because real fills test the cost model, which the ML benchmark showed can degrade six-fold between paper and live trading. The stake grows only after the forward record passes.

**The forward test cannot prove an edge.** The standard error of an annual Sharpe ratio is roughly 1/√T years: about 1.0 after one year and 0.6 after three, so 12 months cannot tell a Sharpe of 0.6 from zero. What the forward test can do is falsify the mechanics: the exposure path, the costs, and the drawdown behaviour in a real decline. The decision to go live must therefore rest on the mechanism the clean literature supports, on cost realism and on small initial capital, not on a forward Sharpe ratio. The revised gate sequence keeps PLAN.md's phase timings.

| Gate | PLAN.md | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| CG1, backtest | History before the hold-out; deflated Sharpe with about 8 trials | Same window (to November 2024); honest trial count of 10–13; results by window; drawdown-halving as the primary test |
| CG2, hold-out | December 2024 – November 2026, opened once | A reconciliation check against the research replication; it can fail a candidate but cannot pass one |
| CG3, paper trading | At least 8 weeks | An 8-week engineering gate (unchanged), then a frozen forward record of at least 12 months and at least one exposure change, counted against the trial budget |
| CG4, live | A few hundred euros, then increase | Small live trading from the end of the engineering gate (about April 2027); increase only after the forward record passes |

## Conclusion

The edge this project can actually bank is structural more than predictive. Leverage, fee drag, and buying into crashes after chasing rises are the documented channels through which retail crypto money disappears, and a spot-only, low-turnover, trend-gated bot on a MiCA exchange closes all three, while the EURC swap turns its exits into tax non-events. The trend signal's job is to keep the book alive through −75% crashes, not to out-earn bitcoin. That reframes the build: the assumptions most likely to break the design are no longer about the signal but external to it, namely an entry-tier fee that has risen to 0.40%/0.80% and a parliamentary majority one vote from taxing EURC swaps. Either could tip the venue decision towards ETNs, which is why the engine should compute both routes rather than assume one.

Validation becomes a problem of time. Crypto's history is short and has now been mined up to last week, so each month that forward paper trading is delayed is a month of genuinely out-of-sample evidence lost. The practical implication is to freeze the primary rule and start a forward record in advisor or paper mode as soon as it is fixed, even before the full engine is finished, and to tell future research workstreams where the evaluation boundary sits so the next hold-out is not spent by accident.
