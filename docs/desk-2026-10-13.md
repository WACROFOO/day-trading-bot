## 5 · Older candles — how they are fetched

IBKR's page (https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/historical_limitations.html, read
2026-10-13): the hard pacing limits — no identical request within 15 seconds, no six for one
contract within two seconds, no more than 60 in ten minutes — are stated for bars of 30 seconds
or less; "Historical Data Limitations for barSize = "1 mins" and greater have been lifted", with
a "soft" slow and the warning that "requesting too much historical data can lead to throttling
and eventual disconnect of the API client"; requests should return "only a few thousand bars at
a time"; its table pairs "1 D" with "1 min - 1 day".

| | |
|---|---|
| client | its own read-only client, **30** (`IBKR_HISTORY_CLIENT_ID`), on its own thread — never the decision worker |
| a request | one session: "1 D" of one-minute TRADES ending 20:00 ET, extended hours; bars outside 04:00–20:00 ET dropped |
| pace | ≥ 2 s apart, ≤ 30 in any ten minutes — with the desk's own refresh (one every 20 s on client 27) the two stay within 60 |
| cache | every completed session on disk, one file a symbol and day in the desk's git-ignored data cache: a past day is asked for once |
| splits | a cached session whose regular-hours close is more than 20 % from the desk's fresh daily close is asked for again, once a run |
| days skipped | weekends, the 2026 NYSE holidays, and days missing from the name's daily bars; a timeout is never cached as an empty day; ten empty weekdays, or the start of a name's daily bars inside the year, end the walk |
| daily | five years before the oldest daily bar on the pane, one request |
| the page | `GET /api/v1/chart/history?symbol=SYM&tf=1m&before=YYYY-MM-DD&sessions=N` — only a name on the desk; a replay answers that it has no older candles |

## 7 · What this could not check

- **Nothing here has run against TWS.** This container has no IBKR. On the first live desk the
  log should say `IBKR chart history connected read-only, client 30`; if another program holds
  client 30, set `IBKR_HISTORY_CLIENT_ID` in `.env`.
- **Whether price turns at these levels is not measured.** They describe where trading
  happened. "From where the bounce would happen" is a hypothesis to test — on the five-year bar
  cache it is measurable: how often a pullback holds a zone against random levels at the same
  distance.
- Whether IBKR rescales one-minute history after a split as it does daily bars is unverified;
  the split check covers the cache either way.
- Volume by price from one-minute bars spreads each bar's volume over its range; the prints
  would place it exactly.
- The S/R fan and trend lines carry the Pine's own parameters — five-bar swings, half an ATR,
  0.2 ATR, two touches — which are its approximations, not measurements of where price turns.
- The runaway seen on the Mac was not reproduced in this container; the mechanisms found are
  fixed and tested, and the next live session is the check.
- Tested in Chromium with up to four sessions on a pane; a trader who drags back weeks loads
  thousands of bars a pane — not measured for speed.

Mistakes caught while building: at 0.3 % of price alone, no two swings on the synthetic gapper
ever joined (its five-minute bars move 1–2 %), so the reach became the larger of that and half
the median bar range; the history client's status, first added to the health block, pushed the
session event over its own size test and was taken back out; and the legend's labels carried the
colour-swatch class (a 10 × 2 px box) — they have a class of their own now.

## 8 · Verdict

**Feasible, and built — except running a Pine script inside the desk, which only TradingView's
own charts can do; the desk runs a port of ours instead.** It keeps older candles (IBKR, a session
at a time, on its own client), draws `ross-fp-v4.pine`'s support/resistance with each level's
volume density and its trend lines with the breakout flag, the prior day's levels, the whole and
half dollars and a volume profile, and handles like TradingView: a visible grid, MACD in its own
pane, dates across sessions, the price zoomed by wheel, a » back to the newest bar. The runaway
view in the owner's recording was this desk's bug, fixed at the mechanisms found. **It shows where
trading happened and when live price crosses a line; it does not know in advance where a bounce
or a breakout will hold** — that stays untested. Paper only.
